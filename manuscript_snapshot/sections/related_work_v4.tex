\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Certified copyright defences.} Near-access-freeness \citep{vyas2023provable} bounds the served law
against a safe model. Its authors' CP-$k$ already pays once per output, rejection-sampling complete
outputs against models trained on disjoint shards with a bound that grows as acceptance falls; CP-Fuse
\citep{abad2025cpfuse} fuses such shards per token and anchored decoding \citep{he2026anchored} charges per
token from a bucket. Selection needs one safe model and no likelihood, and its $\log n$ depends on neither
the rule nor an acceptance rate. No model pair here meets CP-$k$'s premise of disjoint shards. Run with
the anchor as its one safe model, its rule serves the risky model on no prompt at $\log 64$ and on $26.8\%$
at $83$ nats, and best-of-$64$ and installments beat it at every matched certificate (Appendix~\ref{app:cpk}).
\citet{segal2026provably} report that CP-style guarantees can be numerically uninformative and demonstrate
extraction against NAF-protected models \citep[see also][]{cohen2026barriers}. We are downstream of that
observation: Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} gives the exact point at which a budget stops saying anything
and shows that no R\'enyi order moves it, which licenses changing the primitive rather than the constant.
An earlier audit \citep{vijayavallabh2026audit} measured anchored decoding's vacuity on the released logs of
one $8$B pair; every number in Figure~\ref{fig:horns} comes from runs made for this paper, and the exact
threshold, the window corollary, Propositions~\ref{prop:selection}--\ref{prop:imitation}, the windowed
meter and every head-to-head are new.

\textbf{Defences without a divergence certificate.} $n$-gram blocklists \citep{ippolito2023preventing},
datastores \citep{min2024silo,golatkar2024cpr} and filtering \citep{liu2024shield}, compared by CoTaEval
\citep{wei2024cotaeval}, speak only about the works they are handed; on those (Appendix~\ref{app:blocklist}) a blocklist costs no utility and TokenSwap
\citep{prashant2025tokenswap} suppresses as completely as selection more cheaply, and neither bounds the
served law.

\textbf{Best-of-$n$ and private selection.} We claim no novelty for best-of-$n$ sampling or its divergence
bound: selecting among draws is standard inference-time alignment
\citep{gao2023scaling,mudgal2024controlled,wang2023selfconsistency}, the bound
$D_{\mathrm{KL}} \le \log n - (n-1)/n$ is \citet{beirami2025bestofn}'s, its coverage and optimality are
characterised by \citet{huang2025bestofn}, it is near-optimal in win rate against KL
\citep{gui2024bonbon,yang2024asymptotics}, and blockwise best-of-$K$ \citep{mudgal2024controlled} is
selection in installments. Charging the choice among repeated runs is private selection's accounting
\citep{liu2019private,papernot2022hyperparameter}, and private next-token prediction mixes a public model in
per token \citep{ginart2022submix,flemings2024pmixed,majmudar2022dpdecoding} or pays only where private and
public disagree \citep{amin2024private}, Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}'s sparse option in privacy's currency. That
literature reads the divergence as a \emph{cost}, spent before reward overoptimisation or by an attacker
\citep{hughes2024bestofn}; we read the inequality as a copyright certificate. CP-$k$ aside, we know
of no prior statement that a rule choosing among a safe model's draws satisfies near-access-freeness
whatever the rule, nor a comparison of the two mechanisms on one divergence axis. The closest neighbour,
\citet{kalai2026consensus}, serves only where several models agree and pays by abstaining
(Appendix~\ref{app:kalai}).
