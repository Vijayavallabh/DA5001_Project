\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Certified copyright defences.} Near-access-freeness \citep{vyas2023provable} bounds the served law
against a safe model; CP-$\Delta$/CP-$k$ instantiate it with models trained on disjoint shards
\citep{abad2025cpfuse}, and anchored decoding \citep{he2026anchored} per token with a bucket.
\citet{cohen2025blameless} show that per-query access-freeness does not compose, and \citet{segal2026provably} report that CP-style
guarantees can be numerically uninformative and demonstrate extraction against NAF-protected models
\citep[see also][]{cohen2026barriers}. We are downstream of that observation: where they change what is
metered, Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} shows that no R\'enyi order moves the threshold, which licenses
changing the primitive rather than the constant. An earlier audit of anchored decoding
\citep{vijayavallabh2026audit} measured its vacuity on released logs; the order-invariant threshold, the
sparsity bound, the selection certificate and every head-to-head here are new. Whether
near-access-freeness tracks infringement at all is contested \citep{elkinkoren2024copyright}; our
statements are about mechanisms, not law.

\textbf{Defences without a divergence certificate.} $n$-gram blocklists \citep{ippolito2023preventing},
datastores \citep{min2024silo,golatkar2024cpr} and filtering \citep{liu2024shield}, compared by CoTaEval
\citep{wei2024cotaeval}, guarantee something about the works they are handed and are silent on an unlisted
work, a paraphrase, or an adversary who supplies the prefix. Measured as decoders on listed works
(Appendix~\ref{app:blocklist}), a blocklist costs no utility and TokenSwap \citep{prashant2025tokenswap}
suppresses as completely as selection at a lower utility cost; neither publishes a bound on the served law.

\textbf{Best-of-$n$.} We claim no novelty for best-of-$n$ sampling or its divergence bound. Selecting among
$n$ draws is standard inference-time alignment
\citep{gao2023scaling,mudgal2024controlled,wang2023selfconsistency}; the bound
$D_{\mathrm{KL}} \le \log n - (n-1)/n$ is \citet{beirami2025bestofn}, its coverage and optimality are
characterised by \citet{huang2025bestofn}, and it is near-optimal in win rate against KL
\citep{gui2024bonbon,yang2024asymptotics}. Charging the choice among repeated runs is the accounting of private selection in
differential privacy \citep{liu2019private,papernot2022hyperparameter}. That literature reads the
divergence as a \emph{cost}, the budget spent before reward overoptimisation or the one an attacker spends
to jailbreak \citep{hughes2024bestofn}; we read the same inequality, in its pointwise form, as a copyright
certificate. To our knowledge no prior work states that a selection rule satisfies near-access-freeness,
nor compares the two mechanisms on one divergence axis. The closest neighbour, \citet{kalai2026consensus},
serves only where several models agree, needing no named safe model and paying by abstaining
(Appendix~\ref{app:kalai}). The accounting tools are standard
\citep{feldman2020renyifilter,whitehouse2023fully,rogers2016odometers,loynes1962stability,cruz1991calculus},
as is the extraction protocol \citep{carlini2021extracting,carlini2023quantifying,cooper2025extracting}.
