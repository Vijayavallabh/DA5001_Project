\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

Near-access-freeness \citep{vyas2023provable} asks a served model to stay within a divergence of a
safe model that never saw the protected work; CP-$\Delta$/CP-$k$ instantiate it with models trained
on disjoint shards \citep{abad2025cpfuse}, and anchored decoding \citep{he2026anchored} enforces it
per token with a bucket. Two results bear directly on ours: \citet{cohen2025blameless} shows
per-query access-freeness does not compose, which makes Section~\ref{sec:selection}'s composition
arithmetic load-bearing, and \citet{segal2026provably} report that CP-style guarantees can be
numerically uninformative and demonstrate extraction against NAF-protected models
\citep[see also][]{cohen2026barriers}. We are downstream of that observation, and what differs is
the shape of the claim: they show a particular bound is loose and propose a tighter mechanism, so
the natural reading is that the accounting can be repaired. Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} says it
cannot, for every order at once --- which is what licenses changing the primitive rather than the
constant.

\textbf{Scope.} This paper is about defences that publish a \emph{divergence certificate}; the
widely deployed ones publish none --- $n$-gram blocklists \citep{ippolito2023preventing},
datastores \citep{min2024silo,golatkar2024cpr}, filtering \citep{liu2024shield}, compared by
CoTaEval \citep{wei2024cotaeval} --- and differ in \emph{kind}: their guarantee names the works it
was handed, so it is silent on an unlisted work and on an adversary supplying the prefix. We
measure one as a decoder, not classify it, and it costs nothing
(Appendix~\ref{app:blocklist}).

We claim no novelty for best-of-$n$ sampling or its divergence bound. Selecting among $n$ draws is
standard inference-time alignment \citep{gao2023scaling,mudgal2024controlled,wang2023selfconsistency}, the bound
$D_{\mathrm{KL}} \le \log n - (n-1)/n$ is \citet{beirami2025bestofn}, and its coverage and
optimality are characterised by \citet{huang2025bestofn}. That literature prices the divergence as a \emph{cost} --- the budget before reward
overoptimisation, or the one an attacker spends to jailbreak \citep{hughes2024bestofn}. What is new
is the opposite reading: the same inequality, in its sharper pointwise form $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$, is
a \emph{copyright certificate} of the kind Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} governs, at
$K = \log n$ and under the pathwise order. To our knowledge no prior work states that a selection
rule satisfies near-access-freeness, nor compares the two mechanisms on one divergence axis.
The closest neighbour is \citet{kalai2026consensus}, who aggregate $k$ distributions and serve only
where they agree: the same instinct from the opposite premise, since they need $k$ models of which
some unknown subset is safe and pay by abstaining, where we need one \emph{named} safe model and
never abstain (Appendix~\ref{app:related}).

Our proof shape is the one used for inference-time impossibility results generally
\citep{zhang2024watermarks,sadasivan2025can}, but is \emph{partial}: it rules out one primitive and
exhibits another that survives. Every tool it uses is standard --- the R\'enyi filter
\citep{feldman2020renyifilter}, adaptive composition \citep{whitehouse2023fully,rogers2016odometers},
the token bucket \citep{loynes1962stability,cruz1991calculus}, and the extraction literature
\citep{carlini2021extracting,carlini2023quantifying,cooper2025extracting}; Appendix~\ref{app:related}
gives the genealogy, the other inference-time copyright defences and the legal framing.
