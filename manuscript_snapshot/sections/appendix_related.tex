\paragraph{Access-free guarantees and their discontents.}
\citet{cohen2025blameless} show that per-query access-freeness does not compose across queries, and
\citet{cohen2026barriers} prove barriers for counterfactual credit attribution whose non-composition is
autoregressive: imposing it on the next-token predictor does not make the sequence-level model satisfy it.
Ours is the same shape for a different quantity, priced in nats: a per-step charge honoured at every step
leaves the event $\{\text{output}=x\}$ unprotected once the budget reaches $S(x)$. \citet{segal2026provably}
report that CP-style guarantees can be numerically uninformative in realistic fine-tuning and demonstrate
extraction against NAF-protected models; their diagnosis is that memorisation puts sharp spikes on a few
tokens, and their repair charges relative rather than raw probabilities, a change in \emph{what} is
metered. Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} closes a different lever: for every order of the same per-step
charge the certificate becomes vacuous at the same budget, so a stronger charge buys precision below the
threshold and never moves it. Neither result subsumes the other. That a KL bound does not imply a
worst-case event bound is classical \citep{sason2016fdivergence,vanerven2014renyi}; what we add is which
budgets survive the requirement that the certificate be informative about a specific work and the
mechanism remain usable.

\paragraph{Consensus sampling, the closest neighbour.}\label{app:kalai}
\citet{kalai2026consensus} aggregate $k$ generative models, an unknown subset of them safe, and serve a
sample only where enough of them assign it comparable probability, abstaining otherwise. The instinct is
ours, certifying the output relative to models that did not misbehave, and the premises are opposite: they
need several models and no knowledge of which are safe and pay by abstaining; we need one named safe model,
never abstain, and pay in draws. Both certificates inherit what their safe models hold, and ours is
checkable in advance by vetting (Appendix~\ref{app:vetting}). A deployer with a vetted anchor and several
risky models could run both.

\paragraph{TokenSwap, measured: cheaper than selection and just as suppressive.}\label{app:tokenswap}
TokenSwap \citep{prashant2025tokenswap} substitutes the probabilities of high-frequency grammatical tokens
from a small auxiliary model and proves that a memorised sequence's probability decays exponentially in its
length; TRBS \citep{fu2026duplicate} reranks beams against an index of a code corpus. Both guarantee
something about a catalogue supplied, not about the served law relative to a safe model. We reimplemented
TokenSwap from its Algorithm~1 with the published $110$-word $G$ on our $100$ protected passages and $850$
ordinary prompts. It suppresses completely (recall $0.0000$, ROUGE-L $\ge 0.5$ on $0$ of $100$ and $\ge 0.3$
on $0$, against $45$ and $69$ for the memoriser) and, judged against its own rule-off control, costs
$-0.1040$ $[-0.1255, -0.0820]$, where selection at $n=64$ costs $-0.1655$ $[-0.2000, -0.1320]$: TokenSwap is
ahead of selection by $+0.0615$ $[+0.0285, +0.0950]$. The rule-off control is the risky model served
through its chat template, judged against the committed templateless opponent, so selection's cost here
is its distance from that model, which gains $+0.272$ over the anchor to selection's $+0.1065$: less than
half the gap, as in Table~\ref{tab:served}'s chat-template block. \textbf{The mechanism this paper is about is the most
expensive of the three and the only one that publishes a bound on the served law.} The margin depends on
the auxiliary: at \texttt{DistilGPT-2}, the $82$M model its authors use, the two tie ($-0.0325$
$[-0.0645, +0.0005]$). And the suppression is fragile in a way $|G|$ does not reveal: with
\texttt{KL3M-170m} as the auxiliary recall returns to $0.2113$, with $23$ of $100$ passages over ROUGE-L
$0.5$, because that tokenizer keeps $171$ of $431$ $G$ token ids but $1.0\%$ of the probability mass, so the
swap fires on $1.8\%$ of steps instead of $25$--$29\%$. All four candidate auxiliaries read $0.0000$ on the
protected passages alone.

\paragraph{The blocklist, measured.}\label{app:blocklist}
Refusing any continuation that completes an $n$-gram of a listed work \citep{ippolito2023preventing} is the
incumbent a deployer would otherwise run. \textbf{It refuted the argument we built it to support}: collateral
on ordinary text does not grow with the catalogue. At $n=10$ the fraction of ordinary tokens blocked is
$0.000\%$ at $758$ passages, $0.140\%$ at $4{,}935$ and $0.140\%$ again at $9{,}870$, where the index has
doubled to $2.24$M $n$-grams ($0.000\%$, $0.147\%$, $0.147\%$ at $n=8$). Run as an exact rejection-sampling
decoder against the memoriser on its own training split, it binds on $1.035\%$ of steps and takes recall
from $0.4192$ to $0.0201$; on ordinary prompts it fires on $0$ of $850$, so its judged utility is the
unconstrained model's exactly ($+0.272$ $[+0.247, +0.2965]$ over the anchor with and without it, the
chat-templated model's gain). We predicted it would leak
the non-literal event and it did not: $0$ of $100$ passages reach ROUGE-L $\ge 0.5$ under the rule, against
$52$ without it. On a \emph{listed} work the blocklist costs no utility, which selection cannot approach,
and it does not suppress more ($0.0201$ against its own $0.4192$; selection $0.0000$ against $0.3925$). The
difference is in kind: the blocklist must enumerate the works and is silent on one nobody listed, and it
publishes no number about the served law, so nothing about it composes across queries, where $\log n$ adds
exactly. Selection costs $21.8\times$ a metered decode in a server that runs only the anchor
(Appendix~\ref{app:anchoronly}); a blocklist costs a hash lookup. A deployer can run both.

\paragraph{CP-Fuse, measured on leakage.}\label{app:cpfuse}
We rebuilt CP-Fuse from \citet{abad2025cpfuse}'s description as two LoRA copies of the
risky model, each fine-tuned on one of two disjoint halves of the protected passages and fused by the
balancing rule. Each shard reproduces its own half (recall $0.6905$ and $0.6965$) and not the other's
($0.0000$); the fusion reproduces neither, $0.0000$ from a single query and $0.0209$ and $0.0199$ under
oracle-prefix windows (\texttt{results/\allowbreak cpfuse\_\allowbreak audit\_\allowbreak rebuild.csv}). A
$50/50$ mixture without the balancing already holds single queries to $0.0295$ and $0.0000$, so most of the
suppression is the disjoint split. An earlier build with its own fine-tunes has the same shape,
$0.7538$ and $0.7988$ on the own shard against $0.0000$ fused
(\texttt{results/\allowbreak cpfuse\_\allowbreak audit.csv}). The utility half was fixed in advance and withdrawn before it ran: on
ordinary prompts CP-Fuse is the risky model with a device attached, and judged against that model a win is
arithmetic, not evidence. Its guarantee holds per query given a split only the trainer can attest, and
per-query access-freeness does not compose \citep{cohen2025blameless}.

\paragraph{Other defences.}
BloomScrub certifies takedowns by Bloom-filter screening with abstention \citep{zhang2025bloomscrub}; SILO
isolates legal risk in a nonparametric datastore \citep{min2024silo}; others refuse \citep{liu2024shield} or
randomise the decoder \citep{chen2024randomization}; for diffusion models retrieval carries a NAF guarantee
\citep{golatkar2024cpr} in response to replication in image models \citep{somepalli2023diffusion}.
\citet{alshehyari2026copyshield} benchmark output-, behavioural- and representation-level defences; the level
their benchmark omits is the certified one, where a low measured leakage and a vacuous certificate can
coexist. VA3 \citep{li2024va3} amplifies a probabilistic copyright guarantee for text-to-image models through
repeated interaction: choosing among $m$ samples raises a per-sample probability $p$ to $1-(1-p)^m \le mp$,
the attacker's side of Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}.

\paragraph{Self-consistency.}
Self-consistency \citep{wang2023selfconsistency} serves the majority answer over $n$ sampled paths; a mode
is a score, so it satisfies $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ and carries a pathwise certificate of exactly $\log n$
against the model it samples. As deployed it samples web-trained models, relative to which $\log n$
certifies nothing about copyright; over a safe model the same inequality is a copyright guarantee its users
already have. The best-of-$n$ literature prices $\log n$ as a \emph{cost}, the budget spent before reward
overoptimisation \citep{gao2023scaling,beirami2025bestofn} or the one an attacker spends to jailbreak
\citep{hughes2024bestofn}; we read it as a guarantee, compare it with a metered decoder on one divergence
axis, and measure it. The mechanism is theirs.
