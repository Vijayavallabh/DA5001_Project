\section{Limitations and conclusion}\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{The two mechanisms are not substitutes, and the scorer binds first.} $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$
means a served string is one the safe model would have drawn, so an anchor that cannot do the task
gains nothing at any $n$: head to head on TriviaQA with no judge the metered decoder reaches $0.618$
and selection $0.190$ --- but only at $k=20$, where it \emph{is} the risky model, certified at $480$
nats for a $24$-token answer. Below that the scorer binds: a reward gains $3.4\times$ less than
majority vote on GSM8K and \emph{loses} accuracy on TriviaQA.

\noindent\textbf{And it is paid for in compute, which no nat measures.} Selection needs $n=64$, at
$61.3\times$ the metered decoder's forward-pass cost, to reach $+0.1045$. The gain is the
\emph{scorer's} capability, not the mechanism's: a $0.5$B scorer buys the same $\log n$ and never
reaches the meter, while capability saturates by $1.5$B, which reaches $87\%$ of the $7.6$B gain for
$35\%$ of the cost (Appendix~\ref{app:saturation}). The price is the scorer we chose and not the
mechanism, and where that bar sits is open. The corpus is sixteen English novels, judged levels are
position-dominated, and the quantitative half is open: how close a policy of the shape
Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse} permits comes to Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl}'s frontier.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusion.}\label{sec:conclusion}
A copyright budget is not a rate: charged per token it must grow with the work or stop changing the
output. Spend it once, on the draw: an amplification of $8$, not $e^{2000}$.
