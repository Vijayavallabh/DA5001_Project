\section{Limitations and conclusion}\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{The two mechanisms are not substitutes, and the scorer binds first.} A served string is one the
safe model would have drawn, so an anchor that cannot do the task gains nothing: on TriviaQA with no judge
the meter reaches $0.618$ against selection's $0.190$, but only at $k=20$, where it \emph{is} the risky
model, certified at $480$ nats for a $24$-token answer. Below that the scorer binds: it gains $3.4\times$
less than majority vote on GSM8K at $n=64$ and \emph{loses} F1 on CoTaEval, whose infringement event not
even the risky model reaches (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}). A capability gap is the regime a deployer most
wants covered, and \emph{neither} mechanism covers it. \textbf{The judged comparison measures the serving
configuration as much as the mechanism}: it reverses under a chat template, on instruction following and at the authors' temperature $0.7$ and penalty $1.1$, and vanishes against a stronger opponent, when empty answers count as losses or with a scorer from another family (Table~\ref{tab:served},
Figure~\ref{fig:h2h}); every judge is a language model and no human rated anything. \textbf{And it is paid
for in compute}: $61.3\times$ the meter's forward passes and $21.8\times$ its wall-clock in draws alone (batched, $0.22\times$ its time per request at the $70$B pair), and held to the meter's own compute
it \emph{loses}. The \emph{gain} is the scorer's capability: a $0.5$B scorer never reaches the meter, a
$1.5$B one buys $87\%$ of the $7.6$B gain on the judged workload and nothing on GSM8K, and where that bar
sits is open. The \emph{price} is the drawing, not the scorer's one batched pass
(Appendix~\ref{app:saturation}). Nor does the certificate say much about short works: on TriviaQA answers
$\log 64$ reaches $S(x)$ on $35.4\%$ of questions, against $89.6\%$ for the meter's smallest budget, so
$n$ must be set against the shortest work protected. Extraction is sixteen English novels plus four in
French and German. What we prove about per-token budgets concerns a budget on the whole sequence and
meters that tilt toward $p_r$: a windowed meter could certify exact windows at tens of nats
(Section~\ref{sec:uncovered}), we did not measure selection in installments, and how close a policy of the
shape Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse} permits comes to Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl}'s frontier is open.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}\label{sec:conclusion}
A per-token budget on the whole sequence must grow with the work or be spent almost nowhere, and a meter
that tilts toward the risky model pays the imitation cost on every token it imitates. Spent instead on the
choice among a safe model's draws, a copyright budget is $\log n$ nats at any output length, holds against
an adversarial scorer for one response, and composes to a query horizon a deployer can enforce. Where the
metered decoder's certificate excludes anything it is its own anchor, which selection improves on; where
it is the risky model, which of the two is better depends on how that model is served and decoded and which scorer selects.
