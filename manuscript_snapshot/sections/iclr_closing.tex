\section{Limitations and conclusion}\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{The two mechanisms are not substitutes, and the scorer binds first.} A served string is one the
safe model would have drawn, so an anchor that cannot do the task gains nothing: on TriviaQA
\citep{joshi2017triviaqa} with no judge the meter reaches $0.618$ against selection's $0.190$, but only at
$k=20$, where it \emph{is} the risky model, certified at $480$ nats for a $24$-token answer. Below that the
scorer binds: it gains $3.4\times$ less than majority vote on GSM8K at $n=64$ and \emph{loses} F1 on
CoTaEval, whose infringement event not even the risky model reaches (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}). A
capability gap is the regime a deployer most wants covered, and \emph{neither} mechanism covers it with a
certificate a rights-holder can audit. Nor
does selection improve facts: its FActScore precision is its anchor's, $0.042$, below every metered arm,
though the best of its $64$ draws reaches $0.5054$ (Appendix~\ref{app:hemetrics}). \textbf{The judged
comparison measures the serving configuration as much as the mechanism}: against the risky model it
reverses under a chat template, on AlpacaEval \citep{li2023alpacaeval} and at the authors' temperature $0.7$
and penalty $1.1$, and vanishes against a stronger opponent, when empty answers count as losses or, under the
pre-specified judge, with a scorer from another family (Table~\ref{tab:served}, Figure~\ref{fig:h2h}); under Holm's correction over $21$
rows the headline row does not survive, every judge is a language model, the pre-specified one
order-consistent on a third of items, and no human rated anything. \textbf{And it is paid for in compute}:
$61.3\times$ the meter's forward passes and $21.8\times$ its wall-clock in draws alone, though batched it takes $0.22\times$ its time per request at the $70$B pair, and held to the
meter's own compute it \emph{loses}. The \emph{gain} is the scorer's capability: a $0.5$B scorer never
reaches the meter, a $1.5$B one buys $87\%$ of the $7.6$B gain on the judged workload and nothing on GSM8K,
and where that bar sits is open; the \emph{price} is the drawing, not the scorer's one batched pass
(Appendix~\ref{app:saturation}). On TriviaQA answers $\log 64$ reaches $S(x)$ on $36.2\%$ of questions,
against $83.6\%$ for the meter's smallest budget, so $n$ must be set against the shortest work protected, and a vote gains only where $\log n \ge S$
(Appendix~\ref{app:gainwhere}); we tested no lyrics, poems or code, and extraction is sixteen English novels plus four in French and German.
\textbf{Why certify at all}: through the chat template the meter first beats selection at $k=3$, before a
memoriser served the same way leaks, and a deployer may rationally prefer that measured protection; only a
certificate covers works nobody tested and paraphrases nobody scored. How close a policy of the shape Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse} permits comes to Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl}'s frontier is open.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}\label{sec:conclusion}
Spent choosing among a safe model's draws, a copyright budget is $\log n$ nats at any length, or a few
times that per window in installments, and beats every meter we ran at a matched
certificate; where the risky model is far stronger a windowed meter wins, for exact windows only.
