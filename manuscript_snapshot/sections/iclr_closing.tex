\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A scalar divergence budget is a rate, and safety against reproducing a particular work is a
workload maximum; queueing theory has known those come apart since \citet{loynes1962stability}.
That mismatch has consequences a deployer can compute. The budget's natural unit is the safe
model's surprisal of the work it protects, the certificate is exactly vacuous at $k = s(x)$ for
every R\'enyi order, and extraction begins below it at a budget the two models predict, on pairs
held out from the fit, to within $0.03$ nats. We found no budget that is both informative and
useful, but Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl} and the measurement beside it say the obstruction is not
fundamental: the decoder spends hundreds of nats to buy utility worth hundredths, so the budget
goes on imitating the risky model rather than on improving upon the safe one. All three boundaries
are computable in advance from the models and the content, so a deployer who must publish one
number should publish $k/s(x)$, and a rights-holder who reads one should ask which work it was
computed against.

\paragraph{Limitations}
Four, in full in Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}. Every measurement uses the same sixteen English
genre novels, so $p = 3.1\times10^{-5}$ is the smallest value sixteen paired items can produce; the
collapse rests on four pairs spanning $1.33\times$ in $s(x)$; Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl} is a converse,
so its $10^{3}$--$10^{4}$ gap bounds what is possible rather than this decoder; and one judge
scores utility, resolving about four points.
