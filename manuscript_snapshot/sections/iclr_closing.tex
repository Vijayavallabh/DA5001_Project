\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A scalar divergence budget is a rate, and safety against reproducing a particular work is a
workload maximum; queueing theory has known those come apart since \citet{loynes1962stability}.
The mismatch has consequences a deployer can compute. The budget's unit is the safe model's
surprisal of the work it protects, the certificate is exactly vacuous at $k = s(x)$ for every
R\'enyi order, and extraction begins within about a fifth of that point on every pair, on either
side of it. We found no budget both informative and useful, but Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl} says the
obstruction is not fundamental: the decoder spends hundreds of nats for utility worth hundredths.
All three boundaries are computable in advance, so a deployer publishing one number should publish
$k/s(x)$, and a rights-holder reading one should ask which work it was computed against.

\paragraph{Limitations}
Four, in full in Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}: all seven pairs use the same sixteen English genre
novels, so $p = 3.1\times10^{-5}$ is the smallest value they can give; the onset ratio splits by
tokenizer granularity rather than holding fixed; Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl} is a converse, bounding what
is possible rather than this decoder; and two judges bracket the useful region but do not agree on
where it opens.
