\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A scalar divergence budget is a rate, and safety against reproducing a particular work is a
workload maximum; queueing theory has known those come apart since \citet{loynes1962stability}.
That mismatch has consequences a deployer can compute. The budget's natural unit is the safe
model's surprisal of the work it protects, the certificate is exactly vacuous at $k = s(x)$ for
every R\'enyi order, and extraction begins below that at $r(x) = s_s(x) - s_r(x)$. We did not
find a budget that is both informative and useful, but Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl} and the measurement
beside it say the obstruction is not fundamental: the utility a metered decoder delivers is worth
hundredths of a nat and it spends hundreds, so the budget is consumed by imitating the risky model
rather than by improving on the safe one. All three
boundaries are computable in advance from the models and the content, so a deployer who must
publish one number should publish $k/s(x)$, and a rights-holder who reads one should ask which work
it was computed against.

\paragraph{Limitations}
Four bound what this supports; Appendix~\ref{app:limitations} gives each in full. Everything runs on sixteen
English genre novels, so the nine robustness cells re-analyse one corpus and
$p = 3.1\times10^{-5}$ is the smallest value sixteen paired items can produce; the collapse rests on
two pairs spanning $1.35\times$ in $s(x)$; Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl} is a converse whose gap to our
decoder is $10^{3}$--$10^{4}$, bounding what is possible rather than what this decoder does; and
utility is scored by one judge, resolving about four points.
