\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A scalar divergence budget is a rate, and safety against reproducing a particular work is a
workload maximum; queueing theory has known those come apart since \citet{loynes1962stability}.
The mismatch has consequences a deployer can compute. The budget's unit is the safe model's
surprisal of the work it protects; the certificate is exactly vacuous at $k = s(x)$ for every
R\'enyi order, and extraction begins within about a fifth of that point on every pair, on either
side of it. We found no budget both informative and useful, but Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl} and the
measurement beside it say the obstruction is not fundamental: the decoder spends hundreds of nats
for utility worth hundredths. All three boundaries are computable in advance, so a deployer who
must publish one number should publish $k/s(x)$, and a rights-holder who reads one should ask which
work it was computed against.

\paragraph{Limitations}
Four, in full in Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}. All six pairs use the same sixteen English genre
novels, so $p = 3.1\times10^{-5}$ is the smallest value sixteen paired items can give; the onset
ratio splits by tokenizer family rather than holding fixed; Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl} is a converse,
whose $10^{3}$--$10^{4}$ gap bounds what is possible rather than this decoder; and one judge scores
utility, resolving about four points.
