\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}: tightness and extensions.}
If $f$ is maximised at a single $y^\star$ with $p_s(y^\star) = p$, then $q(y^\star) = 1-(1-p)^n \to np$ as
$p \to 0$, so no constant below $n$ works in $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ and the $\log n$ charge cannot be
improved without assuming something about $f$ or $p_s$, which is the point of assuming neither. For a
deterministic $g$, $\Pr_q[g(y) \in A] = \Pr_q[y \in g^{-1}(A)] \le n\Pr_{p_s}[g(y) \in A]$. A rule that
draws one candidate at a time and stops at any data-dependent time, capped at $n_{\max}$, serves one of at
most $n_{\max}$ i.i.d.\ draws, and the union bound over the first $n_{\max}$ gives $\log n_{\max}$.

\paragraph{The certificate over $m$ adaptive queries.}\label{app:compose}
Let a user issue queries $x_1,\dots,x_m$, each answered by a selection rule over $n$ draws and each chosen
from everything served so far. Write $q_j(\cdot \mid \mathcal{H}_{j-1})$ for the served law at round $j$
and $p_{s,j}$ for the anchor's. The $n$ draws at round $j$ are i.i.d.\ $p_{s,j}$ whatever the history is,
so Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} gives $q_j(y \mid \mathcal{H}_{j-1}) \le n\,p_{s,j}(y \mid
\mathcal{H}_{j-1})$ pointwise, and multiplying along any realised history,
\[
  Q(y_1,\dots,y_m) \;=\; \prod_{j \le m} q_j(y_j \mid \mathcal{H}_{j-1})
  \;\le\; n^{m} \prod_{j \le m} p_{s,j}(y_j \mid \mathcal{H}_{j-1}) \;=\; n^{m}\,P_s(y_1,\dots,y_m),
\]
hence $D_\infty(Q \,\|\, P_s) \le m \log n$ exactly, with no slack from adaptivity. A per-query
access-freeness guarantee does not compose this way in general \citep{cohen2025blameless}, and a
per-token meter composes to $mkT$, growing in the length of every response.

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}.}
Nothing below uses the form of $q$, only its budget. For $\alpha = \infty$,
$q(E) \le e^{K} p_s(E) = e^{K - S(x)}$, at least $1$ exactly when $K \ge S(x)$. For
$\alpha \in (1,\infty)$ the R\'enyi-to-probability conversion \citep{mironov2017renyi} gives
$q(E) \le (e^{K} p_s(E))^{(\alpha-1)/\alpha}$, at least $1$ exactly when $K \ge S(x)$. For $\alpha = 1$
the tightest bound is the binary-KL inversion $\max\{p : d(p \,\|\, e^{-S(x)}) \le K\}$, which attains $1$
exactly when $d(1 \| e^{-S(x)}) = S(x) \le K$. \qed

\paragraph{Below the threshold the orders part company.}\label{app:belowthreshold}
Write $S = -\log p_s(E)$ and take $K < S$. At $\alpha = \infty$ the bound is $e^{K-S}$, and for
$\alpha \in (1,\infty)$ it is $e^{-(1-1/\alpha)(S-K)}$, both exponential in the shortfall. At $\alpha = 1$
it is only linear: every $q$ with $d(q(E) \,\|\, p_s(E)) \le K$ has $q(E) \le (K + \log 2)/S$, since
$d(a \,\|\, e^{-S}) \ge aS - \log 2$, and the law that emits $x$ with probability $a$ and otherwise follows
$p_s$ costs $aS - h(a) \le aS$ to first order in $e^{-S}$, so $a = K/S$ is within budget. A KL budget of
half a work's surprisal therefore permits the work half the time. At $k = 0.5$, $K = 100$ nats against a
$50$-token window's $159.8$ permits the window with probability $0.630$ under the KL charge and about $e^{-60}$ under the pathwise one (Table~\ref{tab:certificate}). A KL certificate that bounds
the window by $\delta$ needs $K \le \delta S$, which by Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse} leaves at most
$\delta S/\varepsilon$ steps above $\varepsilon$ in expectation, $1.6$ at $\delta = 0.01$ and $\varepsilon = 1$.

\paragraph{The threshold for a window event.}\label{app:windowvacuity}
Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} holds verbatim for $E_w$, ``reproduce a $w$-token window of the work'',
and every extraction number in this paper is a window event. The scored event is a union over windows, at
least as probable as any member, so each threshold in Table~\ref{tab:windowvacuity} is an upper bound on
where the certificate for the scored event goes vacuous.

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}.}
\noindent\emph{Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}, restated.} Let $q$ be any causal policy,
$q(y) = \prod_{t \le T} q_t(y_t \mid y_{<t})$, with sequence budget
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$, and let
$N_\varepsilon = \#\{t \le T : D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t(\cdot \mid y_{<t}) \,\|\, p_{s,t}) > \varepsilon\}$.
Then $\mathbb{E}_q[N_\varepsilon] \le K/\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$.

\emph{Proof.} The chain rule for an autoregressive factorisation is exact:
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \| p_s) = \sum_{t \le T} \mathbb{E}_{y_{<t} \sim q}[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t \| p_{s,t})]$,
every term nonnegative. Markov's inequality at each step gives
$\Pr_q[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t \| p_{s,t}) > \varepsilon] \le \mathbb{E}_q[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t \| p_{s,t})]/\varepsilon$,
and summing over $t$ bounds $\mathbb{E}_q[N_\varepsilon]$ by $K/\varepsilon$. \qed

\emph{Surely, for the deployed class.} The token bucket caps each trajectory's realised charges,
$\sum_t a_t \le K$, the accounting under which no violation occurs in more than $100{,}000$ trajectories,
so at most $K/\varepsilon$ steps of any single served trajectory carry a charge above $\varepsilon$. At the
authors' $k=3$ and $T_{\max}=200$ this is $600/\varepsilon$ against $200$ steps: it constrains nothing below
$\varepsilon = 3$ nats in a step, the first horn in another currency. \emph{At finite $T$, in probability.}
Markov on the count gives $\Pr_q[N_\varepsilon \ge K/(\varepsilon\delta)] \le \delta$, informative only when
$\varepsilon > K/(\delta T)$. For a rate, $K = kT$, the threshold is $\varepsilon > k/\delta$, independent of
$T$: at $k = 3$ and $\delta = 0.05$ the guarantee is silent below $60$ nats in a single step at every
length. For a bounded $K$ it is $\varepsilon > K/(\delta T) \to 0$: the decoder is the anchor at all but
$O(1)$ steps.

\emph{Selection does not evade the chain rule, and no separation is provable at the level of laws.}\label{app:noseparation}
It is tempting to argue that a selection rule is not a causal policy at all, every token being drawn from $p_{s,t}$ so that $N_\varepsilon = 0$; that describes the sampler, not the served law. Selection's served law factorises like any other, so the proposition applies to it with $K = 3.175$ nats
at $n=64$; its conditionals differ from $p_{s,t}$ because conditioning on which draw won reweights every
step, but they are not \emph{causally computable}: $q_t(\cdot \mid y_{<t})$ depends on the scores of
complete candidates. The statement ``no causal policy with an $O(1)$ budget matches selection's utility''
is false as a statement about laws: every law on sequences factorises, and Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl}'s
optimum $q^* \propto p_s e^{\lambda U}$ has conditionals $p_{s,t}(y_t)\,V_t(y_{\le t})/V_{t-1}(y_{<t})$ with
$V_t = \mathbb{E}_{p_s}[e^{\lambda U} \mid y_{\le t}]$. What separates a meter from both is information: its
kernel at step $t$ is a function of $p_{s,t}$, $p_{r,t}$ and its bucket, while $V_t$ is an expectation over
continuations neither next-token distribution contains. Proposition~\ref{prop:imitation} is the
separation that information structure supports.

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl}.}
The chain rule gives $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \| p_s) = \mathbb{E}_q[\sum_t D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t \| p_{s,t})]$, and
for the audited decoder the step charge is exactly $c_1(\theta_t) = D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\theta_t} \| p_{s,t})$,
so a per-trajectory budget $\sum_t c_t \le K$ gives $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \| p_s) \le K$; for $\alpha > 1$ the
charge dominates the KL \citep{vanerven2014renyi}. (Checked, not assumed: over $1{,}200$ trajectories and
$240{,}000$ steps at $k=3$ the largest gap between $\sum_t a_t$ and the recorded spend is
$1.5\times10^{-4}$ nats.) Donsker--Varadhan \citep{donsker1975asymptotic} gives, for bounded $U$ and every
$\lambda$, $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \| p_s) \ge \lambda\, \mathbb{E}_q[U] - \log \mathbb{E}_{p_s}[e^{\lambda U}]$;
the supremum over $\lambda$ is the Cram\'er rate function \citep{dembo1998large}, so
$K \ge \Lambda^*_s(\mathbb{E}_q[U])$. \qed

\paragraph{The bucket-metered class and its rates.}
Write $\ell = \log p_r - \log p_s$ and $Z(u) = \mathbb{E}_{p_s}[e^{u\ell}]$ along the geometric path
$p_\theta \propto p_s^{1-\theta}p_r^{\theta}$. The charge at order $\alpha$ is
\begin{equation}
c_\alpha(\theta) \;=\; D_\alpha(p_\theta \,\|\, p_s)
 \;=\; \frac{\log Z(\alpha\theta) - \alpha \log Z(\theta)}{\alpha - 1},
\label{eq:renyi-path}
\end{equation}
with $c_1(\theta) = \theta Z'(\theta)/Z(\theta) - \log Z(\theta)$, the deployed charge, and
$c_\infty(\theta) = \theta \max_y \ell(y) - \log Z(\theta)$, the pathwise one. A decoder of the class
serves, at each step, the largest $\theta_t$ whose charge fits the allowance $k_t$ of a token bucket of rate
$k$ started at a debt $\delta$. For a work $x$ of $N$ characters with per-token safe-model surprisals
$s_1,\dots,s_T$, $N_t$ characters after $t$ tokens and $S(x) = \sum_t s_t$, three rates govern the audit:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{s(x) = \frac{S(x)}{N}}_{\text{surprisal rate}}, \qquad
\underbrace{k_{\mathrm{crit}}(x) = \max_{t \le T} \frac{\sum_{i \le t} s_i + \delta}{N_t}}_{\text{Loynes running maximum}}, \qquad
\underbrace{c_{\mathrm{use}} = \operatorname{med}_{y \sim p_r} \frac{\log p_r(y) - \log p_s(y)}{|y|}}_{\text{divergence rate of ordinary traffic}} ,
\label{eq:rates}
\end{equation}
per character where safe models with different vocabularies are compared and per token where a rate meets
a budget.

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:imitation}.}
The charge $c$ is nondecreasing on $[0,1]$ with $c(0) = 0$, and the decoder serves
$\theta_t = \max\{\theta : c(\theta) \le k_t\}$. At a slack step, $c(1) \le k_t$, so $\theta_t = 1$: the
decoder serves $p_{r,t}$ and is charged $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{r,t} \| p_{s,t})$. Summing the nonnegative
charges over the slack steps gives the first claim. For the second, $\Lambda^*_s$ is convex, vanishes at
$\mathbb{E}_{p_s}[U]$ and is nondecreasing up to $u_{\max}$, so
$\Lambda^*_s(\mathbb{E}_q[U]) \le -\log \Pr_{p_s}[U = u_{\max}]$, which is $O(1)$ in $T$ under the third
proviso; dividing gives $\Omega(T)$. The proviso is substantive: a utility attained by one exact long
string has $\Lambda^*_s = \Theta(T)$ and no separation survives. Winning a judged comparison is not of that
kind: the anchor's own unassisted draw already wins over a quarter of them outright, so winning every one, at any length, would cost an optimal policy $1.30$ nats, and the decoder spends $132$ times that to reach parity. \qed

\paragraph{Protection outruns certification.}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:outrun}
Take $s(x)$ and $k_{\mathrm{crit}}(x)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rates} per token. Under the banking rule, if the
decoder emits each token of $x$ with probability at least $1-\eta$ then
$k \ge (1-\eta)\,k_{\mathrm{crit}}(x) - \log 2$, and $k_{\mathrm{crit}}(x) \ge s(x) + \delta/T$ for every work,
with equality iff the running ratio is maximised at the last token.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Proof.} By data processing on the indicator of the true token, the step charge is at least the binary
divergence between the served and reference probabilities of that token,
$c_i \ge d(q \,\|\, e^{-s_i}) \ge (1-\eta)s_i - \log 2$ whenever $q \ge 1-\eta$. The banking rule
\citep[Algorithm~1]{he2026anchored} caps the charge on the first $t$ tokens at $tk - \delta$, so
$tk \ge (1-\eta)(\sum_{i \le t} s_i + \delta) - t\log 2$; dividing by $t$ and maximising over $t$ gives the
first claim, and a running maximum dominates its endpoint $(S(x)+\delta)/T$. \qed Equality holds exactly when the running ratio peaks at the last token; under a constant rate with $\delta > 0$ it is $s(x) + \delta/t$, strictly decreasing, so the maximum sits at $t=1$ and the inequality is strict.
$k_{\mathrm{crit}}$ is the Loynes running maximum of a queue's workload \citep{loynes1962stability},
equivalently the smallest rate of a $(\sigma,\rho)$ arrival curve admitting the surprisal process
\citep{cruz1991calculus,leboudec2001network}: a budget publishes a \emph{drift} where protection is a
\emph{workload maximum}. It bounds regime boundaries rather than screening works: as a predictor of the
onset it misses by $21.9\%$ against a no-change null's $12.3\%$.
