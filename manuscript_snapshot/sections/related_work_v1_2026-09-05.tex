\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

The audit draws on three literatures: the definition it tests, the memorisation and extraction results that motivate the memorising risky model, and the auditing methodology it borrows.

\paragraph{Near-access-freeness and its critiques}
Vyas et al.~\cite{vyas2023provable} define $k$-near-access-freeness ($k$-NAF): a model is $k$-NAF with respect to a work $C$ if, for every prompt, its output distribution is within $k$ of a safe model that never saw $C$, either in maximum divergence ($\Delta_{\max}$) or in KL divergence. Their Lemma~2.2 turns the $\Delta_{\max}$ form into a per-event bound, $p(E \mid x) \le 2^{k} p_s(E \mid x)$, whereas the KL form yields such a bound only in expectation or under a concentration assumption; they also observe that $k$ must be small relative to the entropy of the work for the definition to carry meaning. Anchored Decoding~\cite{he2026anchored} is the first mechanism to enforce the KL form at inference time for an arbitrary risky model, using an anchor trained on the Common Pile~\cite{kandpal2025commonpile} and sweeping $k$ from $0.1$ to $20$. Elkin-Koren et al.~\cite{elkinkoren2024copyright} question whether copyright reduces to a privacy-style definition at all. Cohen~\cite{cohen2025blameless} proves two results that Sections~\ref{sec:composition} and~\ref{sec:attack-results} test empirically: NAF is \emph{tainted}, because a prompt derived from the protected idea can pull the safe model itself toward the work, and NAF does not \emph{compose}, because polynomially many bounded queries suffice to reconstruct training data. Haviv et al.~\cite{haviv2026separate} and Cooper and Grimmelmann~\cite{cooper2025files} argue, from different directions, that memorisation and infringement are distinct questions; our certificate-strength audit (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}) makes the distinction quantitative for one mechanism. Related inference-time defences include CP-Fuse~\cite{abad2025cpfuse}, which fuses two models trained on disjoint shards with a balancing property, and BloomScrub~\cite{zhang2025bloomscrub}, which certifies takedowns through Bloom-filter screening with abstention; SILO~\cite{min2024silo} instead isolates legal risk in a nonparametric datastore. Composition attacks transfer to any per-prompt guarantee and certificate vacuity to any KL-NAF instantiation, whereas bank-and-burst behaviour (Section~\ref{sec:bankburst}) is specific to banked budgets.

\paragraph{Memorisation and extraction}
Large language models reproduce training text verbatim at rates that grow with model size, duplication, and prompt length~\cite{carlini2023quantifying}, and extraction succeeds against production systems through divergence prompts~\cite{nasr2025scalable}, instruction-style prompting~\cite{kassem2025alpaca}, and best-of-$n$ sampling~\cite{ahmed2026extracting}. Cooper et al.~\cite{cooper2025extracting} show that Llama~3.1~70B reproduces large parts of specific novels from short prefixes, and Hayes et al.~\cite{hayes2025measuring} show that extraction probability depends strongly on the decoding scheme, which is why we report both greedy and sampled extraction. Ippolito et al.~\cite{ippolito2023preventing} demonstrate that filters against verbatim reproduction are evaded by trivial format changes; the same concern applies to a KL budget, since both models assign the reformatting similar probability. Benchmarks such as CopyBench~\cite{chen2024copybench}, whose book split we use, CoTaEval~\cite{wei2024cotaeval}, and Copyright-Bench~\cite{hui2026copyrightbench} measure literal and non-literal reproduction with metrics we adopt: ROUGE~\cite{lin2004rouge}, longest and accumulated common substrings, MinHash similarity~\cite{broder1997resemblance}, and the near-verbatim recall of Ahmed et al.~\cite{ahmed2026extracting}. The BookMIA passages behind CopyBench were introduced for membership inference~\cite{shi2024detecting}; Duan et al.~\cite{duan2024membership} caution that temporal shift confounds membership signals on such data, which is why we treat held-out passages as a prompt set rather than as evidence about training membership. Schwarzschild et al.~\cite{schwarzschild2024rethinking} and Karamolegkou et al.~\cite{karamolegkou2023copyright} study memorisation of copyrighted text from compression and legal perspectives, respectively, and Freeman et al.~\cite{freeman2024exploring} and Panaitescu-Liess et al.~\cite{panaitescu2025watermarking} evaluate frontier-model reproduction and watermark-based mitigation.

\paragraph{Auditing methodology}
Our protocol borrows its logic from the auditing of differential privacy, where a claimed $\varepsilon$ is tested by constructing lower bounds that could contradict it~\cite{jagielski2020auditing,nasr2023tight,steinke2023privacy,annamalai2024nearly,kong2023dpauditorium,dwork2014algorithmic}. An earlier audit of Anchored Decoding~\cite{vijayavallabh2026audit} produced the logs reanalysed here and checked the sequence-level budget with a fixed-sample empirical Bernstein bound on sampled spends; the present audit replaces that bound with the per-trajectory invariant (Section~\ref{sec:accounting}) and anytime-valid confidence sequences, and adds the certificate-strength, small-budget, and adaptive-attack audits. The analogue of the privacy-loss random variable is the realised log-likelihood ratio $L(y)$, whose tails we report directly instead of a mean bound. Where a mean is the target we use anytime-valid confidence sequences~\cite{howard2021timeuniform,waudbysmith2024estimating,chugg2025closedform}, which remain valid under optional stopping and have recently been proposed as the basis for adaptive audits of AI systems~\cite{zhou2026adaptive}; a fixed-sample empirical Bernstein bound~\cite{maurer2009empirical,mnih2008empirical} lacks this property. The cap on the reproduction probability follows from the data-processing inequality and the inversion of the binary KL divergence~\cite{polyanskiy2024information,tsybakov2009introduction,bretagnolle1979estimation}, and the best-of-$n$ equivalence of a KL budget follows from Beirami et al.~\cite{beirami2025bestofn}. Automated red teaming~\cite{perez2022redteaming,ganguli2022redteaming} and quality-diversity search~\cite{mouret2015mapelites,samvelyan2024rainbow} search for adversarial prompts; here the queries are fixed by the protected text itself, which is the composition setting, rather than searched.
