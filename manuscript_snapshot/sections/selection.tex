\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setting and notation}\label{sec:selection}

Fix a prompt and write $p_s$ for a \emph{safe} model, trained without the protected work, and $p_r$
for the \emph{risky} model a deployer would rather serve. An inference-time copyright defence serves
a law $q$ and certifies $D(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$, so a small $K$ means the served text is nearly as
unlikely to be the work as the safe model's own output is --- $k$-near-access-freeness
\citep{vyas2023provable}, charged by every deployed instance \emph{per decoded token}, a rate $k$
banked over $T$ steps so that $K = kT$ \citep{he2026anchored}. The budget is the right idea in the
wrong place.

\emph{Selection anchoring} puts it somewhere else. Draw $n$ completions $y_1,\dots,y_n \sim
p_s(\cdot)$, score each with any function $f$, and serve the highest scorer. Every token emitted was
drawn from $p_s$, but the served law is not $p_s$: selection changes which draws survive, and with
them the conditional law of every served step. It changes it only through the choice, so the whole
budget is spent once, on \emph{which draw to serve}, and that is what
Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} charges. The safe model is the only
\emph{generator}, but not necessarily the only model: the risky one may act as the scorer, through
the ranking rather than the sampling, its influence bounded at $\log n$ however it is used.

\begin{proposition}[The budget of a selection rule]\label{prop:selection}
Let $y_1,\dots,y_n$ be i.i.d.\ $p_s$ and let $q$ be the law of \emph{any} rule that serves one of
them. Then $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ for every $y$, hence
$D_\infty(q \,\|\, p_s) \le \log n$ and so $D_\alpha(q\,\|\,p_s) \le \log n$ for every
$\alpha \in [1,\infty]$, and $\Pr_q[E] \le n \Pr_{p_s}[E]$ for every event $E$. If the rule is the
argmax of a score with no ties the KL is smaller still,
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \,\|\, p_s) \le \log n - (n-1)/n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $y$ is served then $y$ was drawn, so
$q(y) \le \Pr[\exists i : y_i = y] \le \sum_i \Pr[y_i = y] = n\,p_s(y)$. A logarithm and a maximum
over $y$ give the $\alpha = \infty$ statement, which dominates every lower order by monotonicity in
$\alpha$; summing over $y \in E$ gives the event form. The union bound is all that is used, so the
selection may consult the scores, the strings, external randomness or nothing at all, and the bound
is tight (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}); the sharper KL form is \citet{beirami2025bestofn}, quoted not
reproved, and needs the argmax structure the pathwise bound does not.
\end{proof}

The inequality is elementary and the KL form known; what has not been said is what it
\emph{certifies}. Two extensions are immediate: any deterministic post-processing $g$ keeps the
factor, $\Pr_q[g(y) \in A] \le n\Pr_{p_s}[g(y) \in A]$, and a rule that stops after at most $n_{\max}$
draws is certified at $\log n_{\max}$. Because the hypothesis is only \emph{serve one of the draws},
the certificate covers rules with no score in them: \textbf{self-consistency
\citep{wang2023selfconsistency} is an instance}: it serves a draw carrying the modal answer, or that
answer canonicalised, so a deployer running it over a safe model already has this guarantee --- and over a web-trained model, as it is
usually run, the same bound certifies nothing about copyright.

\subsection{What $\log n$ certifies, and why it does not grow}
Put the budget beside the price of the work: write $S(x) = -\log p_s(x)$ for the safe model's
surprisal of a protected work $x$, the nats a mechanism must supply before it can be said to have
delivered that work rather than its own text.

\begin{proposition}[The vacuity threshold does not depend on the order]\label{prop:threshold}
Let $q$ be any law with $D_\alpha(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$ for some $\alpha \in [1,\infty]$, and let $E$
be the event that the output is $x$. For every such $\alpha$ the tightest bound on $\Pr_q[E]$
implied by the budget equals $1$ if and only if $K \ge S(x)$.
\end{proposition}

The proof (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}) is three standard conversions --- the $\alpha=\infty$ change
of measure, the R\'enyi-to-probability bound of R\'enyi differential privacy
\citep{mironov2017renyi}, and the binary-KL inversion --- and what matters is that all three break in
the same place. Below it they part company --- the pathwise bound falls as $e^{K-S(x)}$, the KL one
only as about $K/S(x)$, so half the threshold still permits the work half the time
(Appendix~\ref{app:belowthreshold}) --- but no order moves it, so the quantity carrying risk is
$K/S(x)$ and never the rate. \citet{vyas2023provable} report their budget as a fraction of the safe
model's entropy; new here is the exact threshold, for a \emph{specific} work and every order at once.
Selection meets it under the $\alpha = \infty$ charge at exactly $\log n$ nats, where the same
strengthening costs a metered decoder dearly (Section~\ref{sec:orders}), and
$\log n / S(x) \to 0$ while $K/S(x) \to k/s(x)$ (Figure~\ref{fig:selection}a).

As the amplification a rights-holder is promised --- the factor by which the mechanism may raise
the safe model's own probability of emitting their work --- selection at $n=8$ spends
$\log 8 = 2.08$ nats and gives exactly $8$, a bound no smaller constant satisfies. A KL budget
implies no such factor: its event bound is the binary-KL inversion of
Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}, which permits the work outright once $K \ge S(x)$ --- and the
meter's $K = 2000$ at $k=10$ is $2.4$ times the median target's $849$.

\textbf{It composes, at the order the certificate is written in.}\label{sec:compose}
Max-divergence adds exactly under adaptive composition, so $m$ queries at $n=8$ cost $m \log 8$
however they were chosen --- not true of per-query access-freeness in general
\citep{cohen2025blameless} --- the chance that \emph{any} of the $m$ responses is the work is at most
$m\,n$ times the safe model's own under the most favourable prompt ($6{,}400$ at $n=64$, $m=100$), and a colluding scorer can signal
at most $\log_2 n$ bits per response. Against the $400$-nat per-user odometer an earlier audit
proposes \citep{vijayavallabh2026audit}, deployed nowhere, that is $192$ queries against $2$ for a
metered decoder that spends a measured $165.0$ nats per $200$-token response, and none at all charged
the $600$ it is certified at; the sharper KL bound of $1.204$ nats gives $332$, and we quote the
pathwise $192$ (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).

\subsection{What the guarantee does not depend on, and what it costs}
The omissions are the engineering argument. Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} holds for \emph{any}
score --- a reward model, a classifier, a human or an adversary --- any tie rule and any $p_s$, and
unlike an $n$-gram blocklist it names no work, so paraphrase and unlisted works are bounded on the
same terms as exact quotation \citep{ippolito2023preventing}: the anchor reaches ROUGE-L $\ge 0.5$
on $0$ of $100$ passages, where the memoriser reaches it on $47$. The worst case $n$ is loose
wherever it can be measured at all --- amplification needs a base rate, and a clean anchor's is
$0$ --- at $1.0$ to $7.0$ over twelve contaminated anchors, and $1.0$ to $3.0$ on a second draw
(Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).

The price is compute. Counted in forward passes selection at $n=64$ costs $61.3\times$ the metered
decoder, most of it the reward model's prefill; timed in our harness the scorer is $9.3\%$ of the
clock, and the harness overstates the draws: its $35.4\times$ includes model loading, $61\%$ of the
metered arm's seconds, and it forwards \emph{both} models at every step whatever the budget is,
running an $8.03$B model it discards. Separated out, a draw costs $0.35$ of that: on a server that
runs only the anchor, $n=64$ costs $21.8\times$ a metered decode per request in draws alone, so the
lever is $n$ and not the scorer. Batched per request, $n=64$ serves in $0.22\times$ the meter's time at the authors' $70$B pair
and $0.51\times$ at the $8$B one (Appendix~\ref{app:batched}).
\textbf{Held to the metered decoder's own compute the mechanism still loses}: the matched cell is
$n=2$ at $0.68\times$, gaining $-0.0330$ $[-0.0625, -0.0025]$, where the forward-pass count had put
it at $n=4$ and $-0.0395$ $[-0.0720, -0.0065]$ (Appendix~\ref{app:costgrid}). Nor does the obvious
alternative substitute --- a $3.9\times$ larger safe model served alone gains $+0.015$
$[-0.0065, +0.0365]$, a seventh of selection's gain in the same pass, at $86\times$ less compute
(Appendix~\ref{app:biggeranchor}). Majority vote has no scorer term at all, $5.75\times$ the meter's forward passes at $n=32$, and where an answer is checkable
it dominates the pointwise reward at every scorer we ran up to $7.6$B --- but not at $14$B, which
overtakes it on both tasks (Appendix~\ref{app:scorerfree}). What is cheap is not what is best.
