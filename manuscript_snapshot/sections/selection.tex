\section{Selection anchoring}\label{sec:method}\label{sec:selection}

Fix a prompt. Write $p_s$ for a \emph{safe} model, trained without the protected work, and $p_r$ for the
\emph{risky} model a deployer would rather serve. A defence serves a law $q$ and certifies
$D(q\,\|\,p_s) \le K$ for some divergence $D$. For a protected work $x$ write $S(x) = -\log p_s(x)$ for
the safe model's surprisal of it and $s(x)$ for that surprisal per token. A \emph{selection rule} draws
$y_1,\dots,y_n \sim p_s$ i.i.d., scores them with any function $f$, and serves one. Every served token
was sampled from $p_s$; the served law departs from $p_s$ only through which draw survives, and that
choice is all the certificate charges.

\begin{proposition}[The budget of a selection rule]\label{prop:selection}
Let $y_1,\dots,y_n$ be i.i.d.\ $p_s$ and let $q$ be the law of \emph{any} rule that serves one of
them. Then $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ for every $y$, hence
$D_\infty(q \,\|\, p_s) \le \log n$ and so $D_\alpha(q\,\|\,p_s) \le \log n$ for every
$\alpha \in [1,\infty]$, and $\Pr_q[E] \le n \Pr_{p_s}[E]$ for every event $E$. If the rule is the
argmax of a score with no ties the KL is smaller still,
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \,\|\, p_s) \le \log n - (n-1)/n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $y$ is served then $y$ was drawn, so
$q(y) \le \Pr[\exists i : y_i = y] \le \sum_i \Pr[y_i = y] = n\,p_s(y)$. A logarithm and a maximum
over $y$ give the $\alpha = \infty$ statement, which dominates every lower order; summing over
$y \in E$ gives the event form. Only the union bound is used, so the rule may consult the scores, the
strings, external randomness or nothing. The KL form is due to \citet{beirami2025bestofn}.
\end{proof}

The inequality is elementary and its KL form known; what it \emph{certifies} has not been stated. It
uses only that each draw's marginal is $p_s$, not their independence, and survives deterministic
post-processing and stopping early (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}); our draws are untruncated samples at the
stated temperature, so the reference is the anchor exactly as sampled. It covers rules with no score in
them:
\textbf{self-consistency \citep{wang2023selfconsistency} is an instance}, serving one of the draws, one with the
modal answer. The bound is tight: if $f$ has a unique maximiser $y^\star$ with $p_s(y^\star) = p$, then $q(y^\star) = 1-(1-p)^n \to np$. Over a web-trained model, as
self-consistency is usually run, the same bound certifies nothing about copyright: the guarantee is
relative to $p_s$ and is only as good as the anchor (Section~\ref{sec:leakage}).

\begin{proposition}[The vacuity threshold does not depend on the order]\label{prop:threshold}
Let $E$ be an event with $\Pr_{p_s}[E] = e^{-S}$, and for $\alpha \in [1,\infty]$ let
$b_\alpha(K) = \sup\{\Pr_q[E] : D_\alpha(q\,\|\,p_s) \le K\}$, the most a budget of $K$ nats at order
$\alpha$ can say about $E$. Then $b_\alpha(K) = \max\{a : d_\alpha(a\,\|\,e^{-S}) \le K\}$, with
$d_\alpha$ the R\'enyi divergence between Bernoulli laws, and $b_\alpha(K) = 1$ if and only if $K \ge S$,
at every order.
\end{proposition}

Data processing gives $d_\alpha(\Pr_q[E]\,\|\,\Pr_{p_s}[E]) \le D_\alpha(q\,\|\,p_s)$, a law that
reweights $p_s$ uniformly on $E$ and on its complement attains it, and $d_\alpha(1\,\|\,e^{-S}) = S$ at
every order (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). Below the threshold the orders part company: the pathwise bound
is $e^{K-S}$ and the KL bound falls only as about $K/S$, so a KL budget of half a work's surprisal still
permits the work half the time. For a work served at its own length $K = kT$ and $S(x) = s(x)T$ grow
together, so vacuity is decided by $k$ against $s(x)$ and the KL bound, about $k/s(x)$, does not improve
with length. A window is exposed sooner: $w$ tokens inside a $T_{\max}$-token response are unprotected
from $k = s(x)\,w/T_{\max}$, a quarter of $s(x)$ for $50$ tokens at $T_{\max} = 200$
(Table~\ref{tab:windowvacuity}). \citet{vyas2023provable} report their budget as a fraction of the safe
model's entropy; new here is the exact threshold, for a specific event and every order at once.

\paragraph{What one certificate covers, and for how many queries.}\label{sec:compose}
Table~\ref{tab:certificate} prices the exact window at its own position, whose probability under the
anchor has a closed form. Reproducing any near-verbatim variant of it has none, and a rights-holder can
bound it only by sampling the anchor: $N$ clean draws give $3/N$ at $95\%$, so selection certifies the
event at $3n/N$ per response, $0.75\%$ at $n=64$ from the $25{,}600$ clean draws of
Section~\ref{sec:leakage}, while a pathwise certificate of $K$ nats needs more than $3e^{K}$ draws to say
anything. Max-divergence adds exactly under adaptive composition, so $m$ responses at $n$ draws each are
certified at $m\log n$ however the queries were chosen (Appendix~\ref{app:compose}); per-query
access-freeness does not compose in general \citep{cohen2025blameless}. For one response to reproduce the
window the union bound gives $m\,n\,e^{-S_w}$, below $10^{-60}$ after $10^4$ queries at $n=64$, but a
transcript can carry the window piecewise, and there the certificate survives only $38$ queries at $n=64$
(Table~\ref{tab:certificate}). A scorer that colludes with the user can spend it: a keyed hash
realises $3.97$ of the $6$ bits a response at $n=64$ can carry (Appendix~\ref{app:channel}). A cruder
collusion, extending a memorised passage one token per query with the draw the memoriser ranks highest,
reconstructs none of $100$ windows after $50$ queries at $n=64$, though the certificate ($207.9$ nats)
is vacuous for $88$: the anchor's draws hold the true next token on $12.3\%$ of queries
(Appendix~\ref{app:prefixext}). That is looseness, not safety; a deployer who cannot vet the scorer
must cap each user's queries below $S/\log n$ for the shortest event it protects.
