\section{Selection anchoring}\label{sec:method}\label{sec:selection}

Fix a prompt. Write $p_s$ for a \emph{safe} model, trained without the protected work, and $p_r$ for the
\emph{risky} model a deployer would rather serve. A defence serves a law $q$ and certifies
$D(q\,\|\,p_s) \le K$ for some divergence $D$. For a protected work $x$ write $S(x) = -\log p_s(x)$ for
the safe model's surprisal of it and $s(x)$ for that surprisal per token. A \emph{selection rule} draws
$y_1,\dots,y_n \sim p_s$ i.i.d., scores them with any function $f$, and serves one. Every served token
was sampled from $p_s$; the served law departs from $p_s$ only through which draw survives, and that
choice is all the certificate charges.

\begin{proposition}[The budget of a selection rule]\label{prop:selection}
Let $y_1,\dots,y_n$ be i.i.d.\ $p_s$ and let $q$ be the law of \emph{any} rule that serves one of
them. Then $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ for every $y$, hence
$D_\infty(q \,\|\, p_s) \le \log n$ and so $D_\alpha(q\,\|\,p_s) \le \log n$ for every
$\alpha \in [1,\infty]$, and $\Pr_q[E] \le n \Pr_{p_s}[E]$ for every event $E$. If the rule is the
argmax of a score with no ties the KL is smaller still,
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \,\|\, p_s) \le \log n - (n-1)/n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $y$ is served then $y$ was drawn, so
$q(y) \le \Pr[\exists i : y_i = y] \le \sum_i \Pr[y_i = y] = n\,p_s(y)$. A logarithm and a maximum
over $y$ give the $\alpha = \infty$ statement, which dominates every lower order; summing over
$y \in E$ gives the event form. Only the union bound is used, so the rule may consult the scores, the
strings, external randomness or nothing. The KL form is \citet{beirami2025bestofn}, quoted not reproved.
\end{proof}

The inequality is elementary and its KL form known; what it \emph{certifies} has not been stated. It survives deterministic post-processing and stopping early (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}), and it covers
rules with no score in them:
\textbf{self-consistency \citep{wang2023selfconsistency} is an instance}, serving one of the draws, one with the
modal answer. The bound is tight: if $f$ has a unique maximiser $y^\star$ with $p_s(y^\star) = p$, then $q(y^\star) = 1-(1-p)^n \to np$. Over a web-trained model, as
self-consistency is usually run, the same bound certifies nothing about copyright: the guarantee is
relative to $p_s$ and is only as good as the anchor (Section~\ref{sec:leakage}).

\begin{proposition}[The vacuity threshold does not depend on the order]\label{prop:threshold}
Let $q$ be any law with $D_\alpha(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$ for some $\alpha \in [1,\infty]$, and let $E$
be the event that the output is $x$. For every such $\alpha$ the tightest bound on $\Pr_q[E]$
implied by the budget equals $1$ if and only if $K \ge S(x)$.
\end{proposition}

The proof (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}) applies one standard conversion per order: the change of measure
at $\alpha = \infty$, the R\'enyi-to-probability bound \citep{mironov2017renyi} and the binary-KL
inversion at $\alpha = 1$; all three break at $K = S(x)$. Below it the orders part company: the pathwise
bound falls as $e^{K-S(x)}$ and the KL bound only as about $K/S(x)$, so a KL budget of half a work's
surprisal still permits the work half the time. The risk is carried by $K/S(x)$, and a meter's
$kT/S(x) \to k/s(x)$ never improves with length while $\log n / S(x) \to 0$.
\citet{vyas2023provable} report their budget as a fraction of the safe model's entropy; new here is the
exact threshold, for a specific work and every order at once.

\paragraph{The certificate composes.}\label{sec:compose}
Max-divergence adds exactly under adaptive composition, so $m$ responses at $n$ draws each are certified
at $m\log n$ however the queries were chosen (Appendix~\ref{app:compose}); per-query access-freeness does
not compose in general \citep{cohen2025blameless}. Table~\ref{tab:certificate} prices this under the
$400$-nat per-user cap an earlier audit proposes, deployed nowhere: $192$ responses at $n=8$ against $2$
for a meter at $k=3$ that spends a measured $165.0$ nats per response, and none at the $600$ it is
certified at; the sharper KL bound of $1.204$ nats would give $332$, and we quote the pathwise $192$. The
same factor bounds a colluding scorer at $\log_2 n$ bits per response, of which a keyed-hash code
realises $3.97$ of $6$ at $n=64$ (Appendix~\ref{app:channel}).
