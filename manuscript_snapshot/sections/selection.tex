\section{Selection anchoring: a budget spent once}\label{sec:selection}

Fix a prompt and write $p_s$ for a \emph{safe} model, trained without the protected work, and $p_r$
for the \emph{risky} model a deployer would rather serve. An inference-time copyright defence serves
a law $q$ and certifies $D(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$, so a small $K$ means the served text is nearly as
unlikely to be the work as the safe model's own output is --- $k$-near-access-freeness
\citep{vyas2023provable}, charged by every deployed instance \emph{per decoded token}, a rate $k$
banked over $T$ steps so that $K = kT$ \citep{he2026anchored}. The budget is the right idea in the
wrong place.

\emph{Selection anchoring} puts it somewhere else. Draw $n$ completions $y_1,\dots,y_n \sim
p_s(\cdot)$, score each with any function $f$, and serve the highest scorer. Every token emitted is
drawn from $p_s$, so the per-step divergence is identically zero and there is nothing to meter; the
whole budget is spent once, on \emph{which draw to serve}. That the safe model is the only
\emph{generator} invites the objection that this is the safe model served well. It is not: the risky
model may act as the scorer, through the ranking rather than the sampling, its influence bounded at
$\log n$ however it is used.

\begin{proposition}[The budget of a selection rule]\label{prop:selection}
Let $y_1,\dots,y_n$ be i.i.d.\ $p_s$ and let $q$ be the law of $y^\star = \arg\max_i f(y_i)$ for any
score $f$, ties broken by any rule depending on the $y_i$ only through their scores. Then
$q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ for every $y$. Consequently
$D_\infty(q \,\|\, p_s) \le \log n$, hence $D_\alpha(q\,\|\,p_s) \le \log n$ for every
$\alpha \in [1,\infty]$, and $\Pr_q[E] \le n \Pr_{p_s}[E]$ for every event $E$. The KL is smaller
still: $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \,\|\, p_s) \le \log n - (n-1)/n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$q(y) = \sum_i \Pr[y_i = y,\ y_i \text{ served}] \le \sum_i \Pr[y_i = y] = n\,p_s(y)$, each summand
bounded by the marginal of its own draw. A logarithm and a maximum over $y$ give the
$\alpha = \infty$ statement, which dominates every lower order by monotonicity in $\alpha$; summing
over $y \in E$ gives the event form. Nothing is assumed about $f$, ties or $p_s$, and the bound is
tight (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}); the sharper KL bound is \citet{beirami2025bestofn}, quoted not
reproved.
\end{proof}

The inequality is elementary and the KL form known; what has not been said is what it
\emph{certifies}. Nothing in it mentions a reward, so \textbf{self-consistency is an instance}: the
deployer already running it has the better guarantee.

\paragraph{What $\log n$ certifies, and why it does not grow}
Put the budget beside the price of the work: write $S(x) = -\log p_s(x)$ for the safe model's
surprisal of a protected work $x$, the nats a mechanism must supply before it can be said to have
delivered that work rather than its own text.

\begin{proposition}[The vacuity threshold does not depend on the order]\label{prop:threshold}
Let $q$ be any law with $D_\alpha(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$ for some $\alpha \in [1,\infty]$, and let $E$
be the event that the output is $x$. For every such $\alpha$ the tightest bound on $\Pr_q[E]$
implied by the budget equals $1$ if and only if $K \ge S(x)$.
\end{proposition}

The proof (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}) is three standard conversions --- the $\alpha=\infty$ change
of measure, the R\'enyi-to-probability bound underwriting reconstruction robustness in R\'enyi
differential privacy \citep{mironov2017renyi}, and the binary-KL inversion. None is new; what
matters is that all three break in the same place. Raising $\alpha$ buys a strictly tighter bound
\emph{below} the threshold and moves it not at all, so the quantity carrying risk is $K/S(x)$ and
never the rate. \citet{vyas2023provable} already report their budget as a fraction of the safe
model's entropy; new here is the exact threshold, for a \emph{specific} work and every order at
once.

Selection anchoring meets this under the $\alpha = \infty$ charge --- the \emph{pathwise} budget,
which bounds every event rather than an average, and the strongest order in the family the deployed
meters charge in --- at exactly $\log n$ nats, where the same strengthening costs a metered decoder
dearly (Section~\ref{sec:orders}). The two also scale differently in the one variable a
rights-holder cares about: a metered budget has $K/S(x) \to k/s(x)$, a constant no length improves,
a selection budget $\log n / S(x) \to 0$. At $n=8$ that is $\log 8 = 2.08$ nats against a median
$S(x)$ of $849$, a margin above $400\times$ whatever the work's length.

Put as the amplification a rights-holder can be promised --- the factor by which the mechanism may
raise the safe model's own probability of emitting their work --- the two numbers are not of the
same kind. For selection at
$n=8$ it is exactly $8$, certified and realised alike, the bound being structural rather than a
measurement. For the metered decoder at $k=10$ the certificate is $K = kT_{\max} = 2000$ nats, so
the tightest factor its accounting implies is $e^{2000}$, while its measured spend implies
$e^{171.3}$, about $10^{74}$. Both are empty, and only the first is what a rights-holder is handed.

\textbf{It composes, at the order the certificate is written in.} Max-divergence adds exactly under
adaptive composition, so $m$ queries at $n=8$ cost $m \log 8$ with no advanced composition and no
assumption about how they were chosen --- not true of per-query access-freeness in general
\citep{cohen2025blameless}. Under a $400$-nat per-user odometer \citep{vijayavallabh2026audit} that
is $192$ queries, against $2$ for a metered decoder that spends a measured $165.0$ nats on a
$200$-token response, and none at all if the odometer is charged the $600$ nats that response is
certified at.
The sharper KL bound of $1.204$ nats gives $332$; we quote the pathwise $192$, the order
Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} advertises.

\paragraph{What the guarantee does not depend on}
The omissions are the engineering argument. Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} holds for \emph{any}
score --- a reward model, a classifier, a human or an adversary --- any tie rule and any $p_s$, so a
\emph{malicious} score multiplies the anchor's own probability of emitting $x$ by at most $n$ and
introduces nothing new. Unlike an $n$-gram blocklist it names no work, so paraphrase and unlisted
works are bounded on the same terms as exact quotation \citep{ippolito2023preventing}.

The price is compute, in a currency no nat measures: selection costs $7.7\times$ the metered
decoder's forward passes at $n=8$, where it first matches it, and $61.3\times$ at $n=64$, and a
smaller scorer does not buy it back (Appendix~\ref{app:frontier}).

The obvious objection is that the gain comes from leaving the decode loop rather than from where the
budget sits, so we granted a metered decoder the same $\log 8$ nats up front. Neither causal
placement buys anything --- $-0.008$ front-loaded, $-0.032$ uniform, $-0.019$ at ten times the
budget, against $+0.054$ off the axis at the same $2.08$ nats (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).
