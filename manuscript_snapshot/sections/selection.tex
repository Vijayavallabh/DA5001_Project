\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setting and notation}\label{sec:selection}

Fix a prompt and write $p_s$ for a \emph{safe} model, trained without the protected work, and $p_r$
for the \emph{risky} model a deployer would rather serve. An inference-time copyright defence serves
a law $q$ and certifies $D(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$, so a small $K$ means the served text is nearly as
unlikely to be the work as the safe model's own output is --- $k$-near-access-freeness
\citep{vyas2023provable}, charged by every deployed instance \emph{per decoded token}, a rate $k$
banked over $T$ steps so that $K = kT$ \citep{he2026anchored}. The budget is the right idea in the
wrong place.

\emph{Selection anchoring} puts it somewhere else. Draw $n$ completions $y_1,\dots,y_n \sim
p_s(\cdot)$, score each with any function $f$, and serve the highest scorer. Every token emitted is
drawn from $p_s$, so the per-step divergence is identically zero and there is nothing to meter; the
whole budget is spent once, on \emph{which draw to serve}. The safe model is the only
\emph{generator}, but not necessarily the only model: the risky one may act as the scorer, through
the ranking rather than the sampling, its influence bounded at $\log n$ however it is used.

\begin{proposition}[The budget of a selection rule]\label{prop:selection}
Let $y_1,\dots,y_n$ be i.i.d.\ $p_s$ and let $q$ be the law of \emph{any} rule that serves one of
them. Then $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ for every $y$, hence
$D_\infty(q \,\|\, p_s) \le \log n$ and so $D_\alpha(q\,\|\,p_s) \le \log n$ for every
$\alpha \in [1,\infty]$, and $\Pr_q[E] \le n \Pr_{p_s}[E]$ for every event $E$. If the rule is the
argmax of a score with no ties the KL is smaller still,
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \,\|\, p_s) \le \log n - (n-1)/n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $y$ is served then $y$ was drawn, so
$q(y) \le \Pr[\exists i : y_i = y] \le \sum_i \Pr[y_i = y] = n\,p_s(y)$. A logarithm and a maximum
over $y$ give the $\alpha = \infty$ statement, which dominates every lower order by monotonicity in
$\alpha$; summing over $y \in E$ gives the event form. The union bound is all that is used, so the
selection may consult the scores, the strings, external randomness or nothing at all, and the bound
is tight (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}); the sharper KL form is \citet{beirami2025bestofn}, quoted not
reproved, and needs the argmax structure the pathwise bound does not.
\end{proof}

The inequality is elementary and the KL form known; what has not been said is what it
\emph{certifies}. Because the hypothesis is only \emph{serve one of the draws}, the certificate
covers rules with no score in them: \textbf{self-consistency \citep{wang2023selfconsistency} is an
instance}, returning a draw from the modal answer class under whatever tie rule, so the deployer
already running it has the better guarantee.

\subsection{What $\log n$ certifies, and why it does not grow}
Put the budget beside the price of the work: write $S(x) = -\log p_s(x)$ for the safe model's
surprisal of a protected work $x$, the nats a mechanism must supply before it can be said to have
delivered that work rather than its own text.

\begin{proposition}[The vacuity threshold does not depend on the order]\label{prop:threshold}
Let $q$ be any law with $D_\alpha(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$ for some $\alpha \in [1,\infty]$, and let $E$
be the event that the output is $x$. For every such $\alpha$ the tightest bound on $\Pr_q[E]$
implied by the budget equals $1$ if and only if $K \ge S(x)$.
\end{proposition}

The proof (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}) is three standard conversions --- the $\alpha=\infty$ change
of measure, the R\'enyi-to-probability bound underwriting reconstruction robustness in R\'enyi
differential privacy \citep{mironov2017renyi}, and the binary-KL inversion. None is new; what
matters is that all three break in the same place. Below it they part company --- the pathwise
bound falls as $e^{K-S(x)}$, the deployed KL one only as about $K/S(x)$, so half the threshold
still permits the work half the time (Appendix~\ref{app:belowthreshold}) --- but no order moves
it, so the quantity carrying risk is $K/S(x)$ and never the rate. \citet{vyas2023provable} already report their budget as a fraction of the safe
model's entropy; new here is the exact threshold, for a \emph{specific} work and every order at
once.

Selection anchoring meets this under the $\alpha = \infty$ charge --- the \emph{pathwise} budget,
which bounds every event rather than an average, and the strongest order in the family the deployed
meters charge in --- at exactly $\log n$ nats, where the same strengthening costs a metered decoder
dearly (Section~\ref{sec:orders}). Figure~\ref{fig:selection}(a) is the consequence: $K/S(x)$ tends
to $k/s(x)$, a constant no length improves, and $\log n / S(x)$ to zero.

As the amplification a rights-holder is promised --- the factor by which the mechanism may raise
the safe model's own probability of emitting their work --- selection at $n=8$ spends
$\log 8 = 2.08$ nats and gives exactly $8$, a bound no smaller constant satisfies; the metered
decoder's $K = kT_{\max} = 2000$ at $k=10$ implies $e^{2000}$ and its spend $e^{171.3}$, about
$10^{74}$. Both empty, and the first is what is handed over.

\textbf{It composes, at the order the certificate is written in.} Max-divergence adds exactly under
adaptive composition, so $m$ queries at $n=8$ cost $m \log 8$ however they were chosen --- not true
of per-query access-freeness in general \citep{cohen2025blameless}. Against the $400$-nat per-user
odometer an earlier audit proposes \citep{vijayavallabh2026audit} --- none is deployed anywhere ---
that is $192$ queries
against $2$ for a metered decoder that spends a measured $165.0$ nats per $200$-token response, and
none at all charged the $600$ it is certified at. The sharper KL bound of $1.204$ nats gives $332$;
we quote the pathwise $192$ (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).

\subsection{What the guarantee does not depend on, and what it costs}
The omissions are the engineering argument. Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} holds for \emph{any}
score --- a reward model, a classifier, a human or an adversary --- any tie rule and any $p_s$, so a
\emph{malicious} score multiplies the anchor's own probability of emitting $x$ by at most $n$ and
introduces nothing new. That worst case is loose where it matters, and this is also the
only place the looseness can be measured: amplification needs a base rate to amplify, and a clean
anchor's is $0$. Against twelve deliberately memorised anchors, where it is not, an adversary
selecting on the memoriser's own likelihood over $64$ draws realises $1.0$ to $4.0$ of the $64$ the
bound permits (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}). Unlike an $n$-gram blocklist it names no work, so paraphrase and unlisted
works are bounded on the same terms as exact quotation \citep{ippolito2023preventing} --- measured
at a \emph{non-literal} event rather than assumed: the anchor reaches ROUGE-L $\ge 0.5$ on $0$ of
$100$ passages, where the memoriser reaches it on $47$.

The price is compute, and our own harness overstates it: at $61.3\times$ the metered decoder's
forward passes and $35.4\times$ its wall-clock, both include a load that is $61\%$ of the metered
arm's seconds, and our decoder forwards \emph{both} models every step whatever the budget is, so
the path we timed runs an $8.03$B model it discards. Separated out, a draw costs $0.35$ of that and
$n=64$ costs $21.8\times$ a metered decode per request; scoring is $9.3\%$ of it, so a free scorer
still leaves $32.1\times$ and the lever is $n$. \textbf{Held to the metered decoder's own compute
the mechanism still loses}: the matched cell is $n=2$ at $0.68\times$, gaining $-0.0330$
$[-0.0625, -0.0025]$, where the forward-pass count had put it at $n=4$ and $-0.0395$
$[-0.0720, -0.0065]$ (Appendix~\ref{app:costgrid}). Nor does the obvious alternative substitute --- a $3.9\times$ larger
safe model served alone gains $+0.015$ $[-0.0065, +0.0365]$ against selection's $+0.1045$, at
$86\times$ less compute (Appendix~\ref{app:biggeranchor}). Majority vote has no scorer term at all, $5.75\times$ at $n=32$, and where an answer is checkable
it dominates the pointwise reward at every scorer we ran up to $7.6$B --- but not at $14$B, which
overtakes it on both tasks (Appendix~\ref{app:scorerfree}). What is cheap is not what is best.

Nor is the gain an artefact of leaving the decode loop: the causal policy
Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse} permits, a budget granted up front and spent freely or
\emph{reserved}, was built and priced at three budgets and five placements and does not reach it
(Section~\ref{sec:orders}).
