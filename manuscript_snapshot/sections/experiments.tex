\section{Does it work?}\label{sec:experiments}

A certificate is worth nothing if the mechanism it certifies serves worse text. So we measure what
selection anchoring buys and what it leaks against the metered decoder, on its own workload and at
its own anchor (\texttt{TinyComma-1.8B}), temperature and cap: $500$ ordinary prompts, utility a
pairwise judgement against the \emph{unconstrained} risky model
(\texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}) scored $1/\tfrac12/0$, order randomised, arms nested. Judge~A is
\texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct}, judge~B \texttt{Phi-3.5-mini-instruct} and judge~C
\texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}; no arm is scored by the model that selected it, and \emph{judge~C is
the opponent's own checkpoint} --- a self-preference risk \citep{panickssery2024llm}, and one reason
we report only gains and never levels. Every band below was committed before the run that scores it,
and the first cost us a claim.

\paragraph{The scorer decides, and the obvious one fails}
The first scorer we pre-registered was the risky model's own per-token log-likelihood --- the
objective anchored decoding tilts toward --- and it fails, moving judged utility by $-0.006$ at
$n=8$ against a committed band of $+0.077$. A circular oracle over the same eight draws reaches
$+0.488$, so what fails is the objective and not the budget, and Section~\ref{sec:orders}'s AUC
diagnosis is measured on exactly these candidates (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).
Two scorers track quality, and the cheaper is better. Selecting by judge~A and scoring with judge~B
gains $+0.081$ $[+0.034, +0.130]$ at $n=8$; the deployable scorer
(Table~\ref{tab:selection}) never consults the risky model --- a \emph{pointwise} reward,
$\log p(\text{Yes}) - \log p(\text{No})$ from a third model on one fixed template, one forward pass
per candidate, monotone in $\log n$ (Spearman $+0.991$ over seven arms), with a second
non-selecting judge agreeing at $n=8$, $+0.073$ $[+0.027, +0.120]$; all pre-registered with refuting
bands. The comparison is not a tie but a reversal --- in \emph{gains over each arm's
own control}, because absolute judged levels do not survive the instrument check below. Head to
head, with every item shown in both orders and the two arms paired over the same $500$ prompts,
selection gains $+0.1045$ $[+0.082, +0.128]$ for $3.175$ nats and the metered decoder $+0.0400$
$[+0.014, +0.0655]$ for $171.3$: a difference of $+0.0645$ $[+0.030, +0.0995]$, $2.6\times$ the gain
for one fifty-fourth of the divergence, under the pathwise order rather than the KL. At the two
further pairs where it runs at all --- no other openly licensed anchor has a shared vocabulary with
the risky model --- the reversal repeats and widens: $+0.076$ for $1.204$ nats against its best
arm's $+0.056$ for $123.2$, and $+0.150$ at the closest anchor against a metered decoder that resolves at no
budget, $-0.019$ for $60.6$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).

\paragraph{The judge is dominated by position, and only the gains survive it}
Re-judging in \emph{both} orders --- $2{,}000$ calls on one arm and $4{,}000$ on the head-to-head,
bands committed first --- shows position bias \citep{zheng2023judging} dominating it: the same two
texts win $261$ of $500$ shown second and $24$ shown first --- about a third of items are tied
either way, which is why the two counts do not partition $500$ --- only $29.2\%$ of items get a
mutually consistent verdict, and the first slot wins $12.7\%$ of \emph{decided} comparisons. An
absolute judged level is therefore largely a statement about slot order, which is why nothing above
is quoted as one. The correction is \emph{not} symmetric between the two mechanisms, and we
pre-registered the wrong prediction about it: order-averaging the judge-A arm leaves $+0.0405$ $[+0.019, +0.063]$ against the $+0.081$ on record,
but it moves selection's head-to-head gain by a factor of $0.95$ and the metered decoder's by
$0.41$, so their difference grows rather than shrinks. The likely reason is that at $k=10$ the
metered decoder is nearly the risky model it is judged against, leaving the judge little to read but
slot order (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).

\paragraph{An adversarial scorer leaks nothing}
The sharpest test hands the selector to the attacker: we score the $n$ anchor samples by the
\emph{memorising} model's likelihood, on Section~\ref{sec:onset}'s $100$ passages and seeds, with
both baselines. Recall is $0.0000$ at every $n$ from $1$ to $64$ and at every one of the $100$
passages, against the memoriser's $0.3925$ mean and $0.8154$ maximum (Table~\ref{tab:extraction}),
and $0.0000$ at all six anchors --- $n$ multiplies the \emph{anchor's own} rate, and that the anchor
never saw the work is checkable rather than assumed: the check separates all eighteen models here,
every openly licensed anchor reproducing none of the $100$ passages and every model known to hold
the work at least half (Appendix~\ref{app:vetting}). \textbf{Nor is the memoriser ours.} Against \texttt{Llama-3.1-70B}, which
memorised \emph{Harry Potter} in pre-training, the unaided model recovers $0.2475$ on $50$ passages
at a $100$-token prefix and reproduces two \emph{in full}; selection scored by its own likelihood
recovers $0.0000$ (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).

\paragraph{Four anchors, and where the ceiling binds}
Nothing above is tied to the audited anchor, \texttt{TinyComma-1.8B}. The anchor set is constrained
by the premise rather than by convenience: a safe model must be trained without the protected work,
which rules out the open-\emph{data} families whose corpora contain books and leaves the openly
\emph{licensed} ones, the largest of the ten we could obtain being Comma-7B: the ceiling on this
axis is the licensing frontier, not our compute. Re-run unchanged at three of them --- Pleias-1.2B,
KL3M-1.7B and Comma-7B, a $6\times$ range in parameters, same prompts, judge and baseline ---
selection gains $+0.029$ $[-0.012, +0.068]$, $+0.039$ $[+0.005, +0.076]$ and
$+0.111$ $[+0.072, +0.148]$: two of three exclude zero on the pre-registered scorer, all three on
the second, against a band committed to cost us this claim had none. Across six anchors the gain
does not track the anchor's own level ($\rho = +0.543$). On \textbf{AlpacaEval}'s $805$ instructions \citep{dubois2024length} the audited anchor gains
$+0.031$ $[-0.001, +0.062]$ under judge~B and $+0.067$ $[+0.034, +0.101]$ under judge~C, Comma-7B
$+0.075$ $[+0.042, +0.108]$, all monotone in $\log n$, while \textbf{MT-Bench}'s $80$
\citep{zheng2023judging} resolve nothing at a half-width of $0.09$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}'s ceiling binds at the anchor and not within one: across domains
inside one anchor the same test is inseparable from a no-effect null
(Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}).

\paragraph{Off the judge's axis}
Every gain above is a judged preference and the judge is position-dominated; exact match is not. On
GSM8K's first $500$ test problems, $8$-shot, majority vote takes accuracy from $0.320$ to $0.546$
($+0.226$ $[+0.184, +0.270]$ at $n=32$) and the pointwise reward gains $+0.066$ $[+0.024, +0.108]$,
for $3.47$ and $4.16$ nats. On closed-book TriviaQA majority vote lifts
$4.2\times$ less, $+0.054$ $[+0.026, +0.082]$ at $n=64$, while the reward \emph{falls} with $n$,
$-0.038$ $[-0.068, -0.008]$ at $n=16$: across two tasks the rules differ by $3.4\times$ and then in
sign, and the ceiling is the scorer's.
