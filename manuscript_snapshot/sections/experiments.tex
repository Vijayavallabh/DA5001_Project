\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Setup.}\label{sec:setup} \textbf{Pairs.} The meter runs at the anchor its authors evaluate, \texttt{TinyComma-1.8B}
\citep{he2026anchored,kandpal2025commonpile}, trained only on openly licensed text and sharing the risky
model's tokenizer, which a token-level meter needs. The risky model is \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}
\citep{grattafiori2024llama3}, served as a text continuer as in an earlier audit's released logs
\citep{vijayavallabh2026audit} or through its chat template, or \texttt{Llama-3.1-70B} base, the authors'
own pair. Their byte-level decoder, AnchoredByte \citep{hayase2026bytesampler}, runs unmodified at their
recommended pair, Comma-7B with the $70$B base. Decoding is at temperature $1.0$ without repetition penalty, and again at the authors' $0.7$ and $1.1$, with $T_{\max}=200$. \textbf{Workload.} $500$ ordinary
prompts, none from the protected corpus: $200$ questions, $150$ WritingPrompts premises
\citep{fan2018writingprompts} and $150$ FactScore biography requests \citep{min2023factscore}
(Table~\ref{tab:promptset}). \textbf{Utility.} Each arm is judged against one fixed opponent, the
unconstrained risky model's own draw, in both presentation orders, scoring $1$, $\tfrac12$ or $0$, so
position is removed by construction and $0.5$ is parity with the risky model. Judge~B,
\texttt{Phi-3.5-mini-instruct} \citep{abdin2024phi3}, is pre-specified; judge~A,
\texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct} \citep{qwen2025qwen25}, supplies selection's pointwise reward,
$\log p(\text{Yes}) - \log p(\text{No})$ on one fixed template, and never scores an arm it selected.
\textbf{Leakage.} Near-verbatim recall of $50$-token windows on $100$ held-out protected passages: the
fraction of the reference's words inside in-order near-verbatim spans of at least $20$ words
\citep{ahmed2026extracting}.
Intervals are $95\%$ paired bootstraps over prompts, and every band was fixed before its run
(Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

\textbf{Where the meter's certificate says anything, selection matches or beats it; elsewhere the meter is the risky model} (Table~\ref{tab:served}). At $k=0.5$, the one budget whose certificate says anything about a
$50$-token window, the meter blends on half its steps and is judged indistinguishable from its own anchor
($-0.0030$ $[-0.0235, +0.018]$), while selection at $n=64$ improves on that anchor by $+0.1015$ $[+0.0765, +0.1260]$ for a bound of $3.175$ nats. At the authors' $0.7$ and $1.1$ the $8$B meter there gains $+0.0825$ $[+0.0605, +0.1045]$ over its anchor and the gains tie, meter minus selection $-0.0245$ $[-0.057, +0.0095]$, while at their $70$B pair the meter is its anchor again ($+0.018$ $[-0.0035, +0.040]$) and selection leads by $0.089$ $[0.0575, 0.122]$. From $k=1$ on the
certificate is vacuous for a window and the comparison is with the risky model itself, served unchanged
at $99.99\%$ of steps at $k=10$ for a measured $171.3$ nats. Continuing text at temperature $1.0$, that model loses: over its
anchor-alone control the meter gains $+0.0510$ $[+0.025, +0.076]$ at $k=10$, and the gains over each
arm's own control differ by $+0.0505$ $[+0.0155, +0.0860]$, at one fifty-fourth of the divergence, and by more at the authors' $70$B pair, whose base model is at this
temperature judged no better than the anchor, $+0.019$ $[-0.004, +0.042]$; at the authors' $0.7$ and $1.1$ the $8$B wins instead, $-0.081$ $[-0.113, -0.049]$, while their $70$B, now $+0.0585$ over the anchor, still loses, by $+0.0485$ $[+0.0145, +0.082]$ at $k=20$. Served through its chat
template the $8$B beats its own templateless text by $0.184$, and the $k=10$ meter, which \emph{is} that

\textbf{At $k=10$ the difference survives fresh draws and most judges, and not a stronger opponent,
instruction following, empty answers scored as losses or a scorer from another family} (Figure~\ref{fig:h2h}). Re-drawing selection,
then every sampled arm, moves it by at most $0.0055$; five of six judges exclude zero, and the sixth,
\texttt{Mixtral-8x7B}, straddles it at $-0.0015$; on the recorded text the reversal replicates under four
judges and is bounded below $0.03$ under the one clean frontier judge, whose interval over $850$ prompts,
$+0.0226$ $[-0.0018, +0.0476]$, has half-width $0.0247$. The largest reading, $+0.1210$, is \texttt{Qwen2.5-14B}'s, the scorer's family; without the two
Qwen judges three of four exclude zero. Scored instead by \texttt{gemma-2-27b-it}, which serves
shorter drafts (median $34$ words against $75$) and more empty ones ($71$ against $41$), selection gains
$+0.066$ $[+0.042, +0.0905]$ and the difference is $+0.015$ $[-0.0185, +0.0485]$ (pre-registered): the
headline depends on the scorer, not only on the judge. The reward prefers empty drafts and the judge scores an empty
answer near parity: counting empty answers as losses leaves $+0.0155$ $[-0.0245, +0.0555]$, since selection
serves $41$ empty texts and the meter none, while a rule that never serves an empty draw, still certified
at $\log n$, keeps $+0.056$ $[+0.0195, +0.093]$ (Appendix~\ref{app:empties}). From
\texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} upward every interval covers zero, and the difference belongs to prefix
completion: it holds on our prompts, public-domain books and BookMIA's unseen half, is unresolved on
CoTaEval's news questions and MT-Bench, and reverses on AlpacaEval, where the meter wins by $0.0329$
(Appendix~\ref{app:workload}).

\paragraph{Selection against its own anchor.}\label{sec:breadth} Selection runs at every openly licensed
anchor \citep{kandpal2025commonpile,langlais2026commoncorpus,bommarito2025kl3m}; the open-\emph{data}
families fail the vetting check of Section~\ref{sec:leakage}, which makes Comma-7B the largest admissible
anchor. At $n=8$ its gain excludes zero at four of six anchors in two families, largest at Comma-7B,
$+0.126$ $[+0.087, +0.164]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:breadth}), and does not track the anchor's own level
($\rho = +0.543$ on the two-judge mean). \textbf{With no judge}, majority vote over Comma-7B
lifts GSM8K exact match from $0.320$ to $0.546$ at $n=32$ ($3.47$ nats), $3.4\times$ the pointwise reward's
best lift, and lifts TriviaQA and MMLU, where the $7$B pointwise reward falls with $n$ on TriviaQA: the
scorer binds before the mechanism does (Figure~\ref{fig:judgefree}, Table~\ref{tab:judgefree}).

\subsection{Leakage}\label{sec:leakage}

Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} bounds any event by $n$ times the anchor's own rate, so a scorer can
only amplify leakage the anchor already has. Against an attacker who scores the $n$ draws by the
memorising model's own likelihood, and against a paraphrase event, near-verbatim recall is $0.0000$ at
every anchor, at every $n$ to $64$ and at $n=256$ (Figure~\ref{fig:safety}a), where the memoriser alone
reads $0.3925$ mean and $0.8154$ maximum on the same passages and the $70$B, unaided, $0.2475$,
reproducing two passages in full; on $100$ passages the zero bounds the per-passage rate below $3.0\%$
at $95\%$. The zero verifies the anchor, not the mechanism: all six
anchors read $0$ on a vetting screen at every prefix length from $20$ to $200$ tokens, while the
open-data \texttt{OLMo-2-7B} and \texttt{OLMo-2-13B} leak on up to $2$ and $6$ of $50$ passages
\citep{olmo2024olmo2} (Appendix~\ref{app:vetting}). Breaking the premise on purpose, with anchors
fine-tuned on the passages, the realised amplification at $n=64$ is $1.0$ to $7.0$ on one draw and $1.0$
to $3.0$ on a second (Figure~\ref{fig:safety}b): selection multiplies what the anchor leaks and is no
floor under it.

\subsection{Cost}\label{sec:cost}

Selection pays in draws (Table~\ref{tab:cost}, Appendix~\ref{app:sel-cost}). Counted in forward passes, $n=64$ costs $61.3\times$ the
meter, most of it the reward model's prefill; on the clock the reward pass is $9.3\%$ and the draws are
the price, so the lever is $n$ and not the scorer. On a server that runs only the anchor the draws cost
$21.8\times$ a metered decode, the throughput price. Batched, a request is one decode loop that never runs
the risky model, $0.22\times$ the meter's time at the authors' $70$B pair, $0.51\times$ at the $8$B one and
$0.061\times$ against their byte-level decoder;
a meter run as speculative decoding, the anchor drafting, might narrow that, which we did not try.
\textbf{Held to the meter's own compute, selection loses}: the matched cell is $n=2$ at $0.68\times$,
gaining $-0.0330$ $[-0.0625, -0.0025]$ against the meter, and on TriviaQA it reaches $0.112$ where the
meter, vacuous at $k=20$, reaches $0.618$. A $3.9\times$ larger safe model served alone is no substitute:
it gains $+0.015$ $[-0.0065, +0.0365]$ over the small one (Appendix~\ref{app:biggeranchor}). Majority
vote has no scorer, costs $5.75\times$ the meter's forward passes at $n=32$, and on checkable answers
dominates the pointwise reward at every scorer up to $7.6$B --- but not at $14$B, which overtakes four
draws of it on both tasks and every $n$ on TriviaQA (Appendix~\ref{app:scorerfree}).
