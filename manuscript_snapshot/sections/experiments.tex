\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}\label{sec:setup}

A certificate is worth nothing if the mechanism it certifies serves worse text, so we measure what
selection anchoring buys and what it leaks against the metered decoder, on its own workload and at
its own anchor, temperature and cap. The \emph{audited} anchor, the one He et al.\ evaluate, is
\texttt{TinyComma-1.8B}: trained only on openly
licensed text, so $p_s$ plausibly never saw the work, and sharing the risky model's tokenizer, so
the metered decoder can fuse the two at all. The workload is $500$ ordinary prompts
in three classes, none of them drawn from the protected corpus and all released with the artifact:
$200$ general-knowledge questions, $150$ FactScore biography requests, and $150$ creative-writing
prompts, the last two from public corpora rather than written here (Table~\ref{tab:promptset}). Utility is a pairwise judgement against the \emph{unconstrained} risky model
(\texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}) scored win/tie/loss as $1$, $\tfrac12$, $0$, order randomised, arms
nested so that every comparison is paired on the prompt. Judge~A is
\texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct}, judge~B \texttt{Phi-3.5-mini-instruct} and judge~C
\texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}; no arm is scored by the model that selected it, and \emph{judge~C is
the opponent's own checkpoint} --- a self-preference risk \citep{panickssery2024llm}, one reason we
report only gains. Every band below was fixed before the run that scores it, and the first cost us
a claim.

\subsection{Main results}\label{sec:main-results}
\textbf{The scorer decides, and the obvious one fails.} The first scorer we specified in advance was the risky model's own per-token log-likelihood --- the
objective anchored decoding tilts toward --- and it fails, moving judged utility by $-0.006$ at
$n=8$ against a band fixed in advance of $+0.077$. A circular oracle over the same eight draws reaches
$+0.488$, so what fails is the objective and not the budget (Appendix~\ref{app:currency}); and
handed the same reward on TriviaQA the meter gains nothing (Table~\ref{tab:parity}).
Selecting by judge~A and scoring with judge~B gains $+0.081$ $[+0.034, +0.130]$ at $n=8$; the deployable scorer (Figure~\ref{fig:breadth}) never
consults the risky model --- a \emph{pointwise} reward, $\log p(\text{Yes}) - \log p(\text{No})$
from a third model (\texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct}) on one fixed template, monotone in $\log n$ (Spearman $+0.991$ over seven arms), with a second non-selecting
judge agreeing at $n=8$, $+0.073$ $[+0.027, +0.120]$ and at $n=64$, $+0.123$ $[+0.076, +0.171]$. The certificate is $4.159$ nats
at $n=64$ under the pathwise order and $\log 64 - 63/64 = 3.175$ under the sharper KL one
\citep{beirami2025bestofn}; both are \emph{bounds}, attained for a tie-free score
(Appendix~\ref{app:realisedkl}), so the head-to-head sets selection's bound
against the meter's \emph{measured} spend: the conservative direction. We report \emph{gains over
each arm's own control}, never levels, for the reason below. Head to head, paired over the same $500$ prompts with every item shown in both orders, selection gains
$+0.1045$ $[+0.082, +0.128]$ for $3.175$ nats and the metered decoder $+0.0400$
$[+0.014, +0.0655]$ for $171.3$, a difference of $+0.0645$ $[+0.030, +0.0995]$ under that judge.
\looseness=-1 \textbf{It replicates across judges, not across opponents or workloads.} Five judges,
$3.8$--$72$B in four families, re-scored \emph{byte-identical} text: all five put the difference
positive: it \textbf{replicates under four judges and is bounded below $0.03$ under the one clean
frontier judge}; no family, size or consistency ordering explains it (Table~\ref{tab:h2hrepeat}). Swapping the \emph{opponent} dissolves it, as does moving off
prefix completion (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}). All five agree sixty-four draws buy \emph{at least} the meter's
utility for one fifty-fourth of its divergence, as does Figure~\ref{fig:judgefree}c with no judge
in it. The advantage is in nats and not in compute --- at the meter's own
compute selection loses, judged ($-0.0395$) and unjudged alike, the unjudged loss being the larger
(Appendix~\ref{app:judgefree}) --- and $+0.0400$ sits inside the
$0.04$--$0.07$ cross-pass floor measured below, so what carries the claim is the paired
\emph{difference} and not either level. \textbf{It rests on a protocol repair}: earlier arms judged under a prompt reconstructed as served
text minus generation, which differs across arms on $455$ of $500$ items; under the corpus prompt
the \emph{single-order} difference is $+0.013$, not $+0.070$, and what survives is the
order-averaged figure (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}). At the two further pairs where the meter runs at all
(Appendix~\ref{app:second-anchor}) --- no other
openly licensed anchor shares a vocabulary with the risky model --- the reversal repeats and widens,
$+0.076$ for $1.204$ nats against $+0.056$ for $123.2$, and $+0.150$ against a meter whose gain excludes zero
at no budget, $-0.019$ for $60.6$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).

\subsection{Ablations and robustness}\label{sec:ablations}

\textbf{The judge is dominated by position, and only the gains survive it.} Re-judging the same $500$ pairs in \emph{both} orders finds the same two texts winning $261$ of
$500$ shown second and $24$ shown first, so an absolute judged level is largely a statement about
slot order and none is quoted as one. The correction is \emph{not} symmetric --- it scales
selection's head-to-head by $0.95$ and the metered decoder's by $0.41$, so their difference grows
--- and a judged gain has a floor: a re-run at a different $n$-grid moves one by $0.04$ to $0.07$,
so a lone estimate of that size is not evidence. The head-to-head difference escapes the floor by
being paired within one pass (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).


\textbf{An adversarial scorer leaks nothing.} The sharpest test hands the selector to the attacker:
over nine arms --- six anchors, a paraphrase event, an attacker scoring the draws by the
\emph{memorising model's own likelihood}, and \texttt{Llama-3.1-70B} --- near-verbatim recall reads
$0.0000$ at every $n$ to $64$ and at $n=256$, on every passage and at all six anchors, against the
memoriser alone at $0.3925$ mean and $0.8154$ maximum. \textbf{Nor is that memoriser ours}:
\texttt{Llama-3.1-70B} memorised \emph{Harry Potter} in pre-training, unaided recovers $0.2475$ at
a $100$-token prefix and reproduces two \emph{in full}. The guarantee can fail only through the
anchor, whose own rate $n$ multiplies --- checkable rather than assumed
(Appendix~\ref{app:vetting}) --- and broken on purpose over twelve contaminated anchors the
realised factor at $n=64$ is $1.0$ to $7.0$, not the $64$ permitted (Figure~\ref{fig:safety},
Table~\ref{tab:extraction}).

\textbf{Six anchors, two more workloads, and three tasks with no judge at all.}
Figure~\ref{fig:breadth} is the whole of it, and the anchor set is constrained by the premise: a
safe model must be trained without the protected work, which rules out the open-\emph{data}
families and leaves the openly \emph{licensed} ones, the largest we could obtain being Comma-7B ---
the ceiling is the licensing frontier, not our compute. That
exclusion is measured: the check of Appendix~\ref{app:vetting} reads $0.000$ at all five licensed
anchors and above zero at both OLMo-2 models. Across the six admissible the gain does not track the
anchor's own level ($\rho = +0.543$ on the two-judge mean). \textbf{Two rows are not judged
preferences at all}: on GSM8K
majority vote takes exact match from $0.320$ to $0.546$ for $3.47$ certified nats, $3.4\times$ the
pointwise reward's lift, while on TriviaQA that reward \emph{falls} with $n$
(Figure~\ref{fig:judgefree}), and on MMLU, a \emph{forced choice}, both lift again. What saturates is the scorer, not the mechanism, which is why the
headline rule is the one with nothing to overoptimise against. Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}'s ceiling binds
across anchors, not within one: across domains inside a single anchor the same test is inseparable
from a no-effect null (Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}).
