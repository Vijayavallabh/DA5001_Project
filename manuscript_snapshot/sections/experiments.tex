\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Setup.}\label{sec:setup} \textbf{Pairs.} The meter runs at the anchor its authors evaluate, \texttt{TinyComma-1.8B}
\citep{he2026anchored,kandpal2025commonpile}, trained only on openly licensed text and sharing the risky
model's tokenizer. The risky model is \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}
\citep{grattafiori2024llama3}, served as a text continuer as in an earlier audit's released logs
\citep{vijayavallabh2026audit} or through its chat template, or the authors' \texttt{Llama-3.1-70B} base. Their byte-level decoder, AnchoredByte \citep{hayase2026bytesampler}, runs unmodified at their
recommended pair, Comma-7B with the $70$B base. Decoding is at temperature $1.0$ without repetition penalty, and again at the authors' temperature $0.7$ and penalty $1.1$ (\emph{their $0.7$ and $1.1$}), with $T_{\max}=200$. \textbf{Workload.} $500$ ordinary
prompts, none from the protected corpus: $200$ questions we wrote, $150$ WritingPrompts premises
\citep{fan2018writingprompts} and $150$ FactScore biography requests \citep{min2023factscore}; the first
two carry the released logs' \texttt{Complete the prefix:} header (Table~\ref{tab:promptset}).
\textbf{Utility.} Each arm is judged against one fixed opponent, the unconstrained risky model's own draw,
in both orders, scoring $1$, $\tfrac12$ or $0$: an arm's \emph{level} is its mean ($0.5$ is parity), its
\emph{gain} its level minus its own zero-budget control's (the anchor alone, or selection's single draw),
and a \emph{difference} two gains paired over prompts, free of position by construction. Judge~B, \texttt{Phi-3.5-mini-instruct}
\citep{abdin2024phi3}, is pre-specified, but its two verdicts on an item agree only $29$ to $37\%$ of the
time, so every registered difference is also read by judge~G, \texttt{gemma-2-27b-it}
\citep{gemma2024gemma2}, which agrees with itself on $78$ to $85\%$; judge~A,
\texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct} \citep{qwen2025qwen25}, supplies selection's pointwise reward,
$\log p(\text{Yes}) - \log p(\text{No})$ on one fixed template, and never scores an arm it selected.
\textbf{Leakage.} Near-verbatim recall on $100$ protected passages, the fraction of the reference's words in
in-order near-verbatim spans of at least $20$ words \citep{ahmed2026extracting}.
Intervals are $95\%$ paired bootstraps; every band was fixed in advance (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

continuing text at temperature $1.0$, reproducing the committed levels on $500$ of $500$ prompts. \textbf{Selection beats every meter at every matched certificate.} Best-of-$64$ gains
$+0.1015$ for a KL bound of $3.175$ nats, attained only for a tie-free score and unrepeated strings (Appendix~\ref{app:realisedkl}), while the meter at $k=0.5$, the one published budget that says anything

$-0.0030$ $[-0.0245, +0.0185]$, a null with $80\%$ power against $+0.0305$ and, by a two one-sided test, equivalent to zero within half of selection's gain; the same judge gives $+0.065$

the budget granted up front by $+0.0785$ $[+0.0445, +0.1115]$ and a meter capping every $50$-token span by

the per-token meters and the windowed meter at $\log 64$ only (Appendix~\ref{app:judgevalidity}). The windowed meter, the design Section~\ref{sec:uncovered} leaves open, climbs
with its budget and draws level only at $W = 125$ ($+0.022$ $[-0.0145, +0.0585]$; we had predicted selection
would still lead), where a memoriser served through it already reproduces near-verbatim text: $0.106$,
against $0.495$ alone and $0.000$ at every $W \le 40$.

\textbf{Where the risky model is far stronger than the anchor, the windowed meter wins, at a
certificate that covers exact windows only.} Served through its chat template the $k=10$ meter gains $+0.263$ over the anchor; a windowed meter at
$W = 125$ beats selection by $0.160$ $[0.122, 0.1985]$, and at $W=40$ it wins under judge~B, $-0.0525$
$[-0.0895, -0.0150]$, ties under judge~G, $-0.0295$ $[-0.0725, +0.0145]$, and leaks nothing through the same

\paragraph{Against the risky model.} \textbf{From $k=1$ on the meter's certificate is vacuous for a window
and the comparison is with the risky model itself} (Table~\ref{tab:served}), served unchanged at
$99.99\%$ of steps at $k=10$ for a measured $171.3$ nats. At the authors' $0.7$ and $1.1$ even $k=0.5$ is not
the anchor: the $8$B meter there gains $+0.0825$ $[+0.0605, +0.1045]$ and the gains tie, meter minus
selection $-0.0245$ $[-0.057, +0.0095]$, while at their $70$B pair the meter is its anchor again ($+0.018$
$[-0.0035, +0.040]$) and selection leads by $0.089$ $[0.0575, 0.122]$. Continuing text at temperature
$1.0$, the risky model loses: the meter gains $+0.0510$ $[+0.025, +0.076]$ at $k=10$, and the gains differ by $+0.0505$ $[+0.0155, +0.0860]$, at one fifty-fourth of the divergence ($+0.121$
$[+0.024, +0.222]$ on the $62$ prompts where both arms' verdicts are order-consistent), and by more at the
authors' $70$B pair, whose base model at this temperature, fluent but off task, is judged no better than the
anchor, $+0.019$ $[-0.004, +0.042]$; at the authors' $0.7$ and $1.1$ the $8$B wins instead, $-0.081$ $[-0.113, -0.049]$, while their $70$B, now $+0.0585$ over the anchor, still loses, by $+0.0485$ $[+0.0145, +0.082]$ at $k=20$. Served through its chat
template the $8$B beats its own templateless text by $0.184$, and the $k=10$ meter, that model at
$99.84\%$ of steps, wins by $0.138$; the meter's interval first lies above selection's at $k=3$,
whose $K/S_w = 3.75$ excludes nothing about a window, after a tie at $k=2$, and a memoriser served the same

(Appendix~\ref{app:served}); that model gains $+0.272$ over the anchor where selection
gains $+0.1065$, so selection closes less than half the gap (Appendix~\ref{app:tokenswap}). Handed the
same reward on TriviaQA, the meter gains nothing (Table~\ref{tab:parity}).


\textbf{At $k=10$ the difference survives fresh draws and the judges that read reliably, and not a stronger
opponent, instruction following, empty answers scored as losses or a scorer from another family}
(Figure~\ref{fig:h2h}). Re-drawing selection, then every sampled arm, moves it by at most $0.0055$. Five of six judges
exclude zero, among them both whose two verdicts on an item agree on over three quarters of items,

\paragraph{Selection against its own anchor.}\label{sec:breadth} Across the openly licensed anchors
\citep{kandpal2025commonpile,langlais2026commoncorpus,bommarito2025kl3m}, of which Comma-7B is the largest to
pass the vetting check of Section~\ref{sec:leakage}, selection's gain at $n=8$ excludes zero at four of six anchors in
two families, largest at Comma-7B, $+0.126$ $[+0.087, +0.164]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:breadth}), and does not
significantly track the anchor's own level over six anchors ($\rho = +0.543$ on the two-judge mean). \textbf{With no judge}, majority vote over Comma-7B
lifts GSM8K \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k} exact match from $0.320$ to $0.546$ at $n=32$ ($3.47$ nats), $3.4\times$ the pointwise reward's
best lift, and lifts TriviaQA and MMLU \citep{hendrycks2021mmlu}, where on TriviaQA the $7$B pointwise reward falls with $n$:
the scorer binds first (Figure~\ref{fig:judgefree}, Table~\ref{tab:judgefree}).

\subsection{Leakage}\label{sec:leakage}

A scorer can only amplify leakage the anchor already has (Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}). Against an attacker who scores the $n$ draws by the
memorising model's own likelihood, and against a paraphrase event, near-verbatim recall is $0.0000$ at
every anchor, at every $n$ to $64$ and at $n=256$ (Figure~\ref{fig:safety}a), where the memoriser alone
reads $0.3925$ mean and $0.8154$ maximum and the $70$B, unaided, $0.2475$, reproducing two passages in full;
the zero bounds the per-passage rate below $3.0\%$ at $95\%$. On the meters' own sweep protocol the best of $64$ anchor draws also reads $0.0000$, where a
memoriser served through the windowed meter reaches $0.01$ at $80$ nats a window, $125$ through the chat
template (Appendix~\ref{app:windowed}). The zero verifies the anchor, not the mechanism: all six
anchors read $0$ on a vetting screen at every prefix length from $20$ to $200$ tokens, where the open-data
\texttt{OLMo-2-7B} and \texttt{OLMo-2-13B} \citep{olmo2024olmo2} leak on up to $2$ and $6$ of $50$
(Appendix~\ref{app:vetting}). With anchors deliberately
fine-tuned on the passages, the realised amplification at $n=64$ is $1.0$ to $7.0$ on one draw and $1.0$
to $3.0$ on a second (Figure~\ref{fig:safety}b): selection multiplies what the anchor leaks and is no
floor under it.

\subsection{Cost}\label{sec:cost}

Selection pays in draws, and one number is the price: on a server that runs only the anchor the $64$ draws
cost $21.8\times$ a metered decode (Table~\ref{tab:cost}, Appendix~\ref{app:sel-cost}); the rest are views of it. Counted in forward passes $n=64$ costs $61.3\times$ the
meter, most of it the reward model's prefill, but on the clock the reward pass is $9.3\%$ and the draws are
the rest, so the lever is $n$ and not the scorer. Batched into one decode loop a request takes $0.22\times$
the meter's time at the authors' $70$B pair and $0.51\times$ at the $8$B one, because it never runs the risky model
($0.061\times$ against their byte-level decoder); a meter run as speculative
decoding might narrow that, which we did not try.
\textbf{Held to the meter's own compute, selection loses}: the matched cell is $n=2$ at $0.68\times$,
gaining $-0.0330$ $[-0.0625, -0.0025]$ against the meter, and on TriviaQA it reaches $0.112$ where the
meter, vacuous at $k=20$, reaches $0.618$. A $3.9\times$ larger safe model served alone is no substitute:
it gains $+0.015$ $[-0.0065, +0.0365]$ over the small one (Appendix~\ref{app:biggeranchor}). Majority
vote has no scorer, costs $5.75\times$ the meter's forward passes at $n=32$, and beats the pointwise reward
up to a $7.6$B scorer but not at $14$B (Appendix~\ref{app:scorerfree}).
