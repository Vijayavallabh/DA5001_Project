\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\textbf{Pairs.} The meter runs at the anchor its authors evaluate, \texttt{TinyComma-1.8B}
\citep{he2026anchored,kandpal2025commonpile}, trained only on openly licensed text and sharing the risky
model's tokenizer, which a token-level meter needs. The risky model is \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}
\citep{grattafiori2024llama3}, served as a text continuer as in an earlier audit's released logs
\citep{vijayavallabh2026audit} or through its chat template, or \texttt{Llama-3.1-70B} base, the authors'
own pair. Their byte-level decoder, AnchoredByte \citep{hayase2026bytesampler}, runs unmodified at their
recommended pair, Comma-7B with the $70$B base. Decoding is at temperature $1.0$ without repetition
penalty, where the authors use $0.7$ and $1.1$, with $T_{\max}=200$. \textbf{Workload.} $500$ ordinary
prompts, none from the protected corpus: $200$ questions, $150$ WritingPrompts premises
\citep{fan2018writingprompts} and $150$ FactScore biography requests \citep{min2023factscore}
(Table~\ref{tab:promptset}). \textbf{Utility.} Each arm is judged against one fixed opponent, the
unconstrained risky model's own draw, in both presentation orders, scoring $1$, $\tfrac12$ or $0$, so
position is removed by construction and $0.5$ is parity with the risky model. Judge~B,
\texttt{Phi-3.5-mini-instruct} \citep{abdin2024phi3}, is pre-specified; judge~A,
\texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct} \citep{qwen2025qwen25}, supplies selection's pointwise reward,
$\log p(\text{Yes}) - \log p(\text{No})$ on one fixed template, and never scores an arm it selected.
\textbf{Leakage.} Near-verbatim recall of $50$-token windows on $100$ held-out protected passages.
Intervals are $95\%$ paired bootstraps over prompts, and every band was fixed before its run
(Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

\textbf{The sign is set by how the risky model is served, and the budget never pays for the
difference} (Table~\ref{tab:served}). Where the risky model continues text, selection at $n=64$ is
judged better than the meter at every budget. At $k=0.5$, the one budget whose certificate says anything
about a $50$-token window, the meter binds on half its steps and is indistinguishable from its own anchor
($-0.0030$ $[-0.0235, +0.018]$); at $k=10$ it serves the risky model's distribution at $99.99\%$ of steps
and spends a measured $171.3$ nats. On the registered statistic, gains over each arm's own anchor-alone
control, selection gains $+0.1015$ $[+0.0765, +0.1260]$ for a bound of $3.175$ nats and the meter
$+0.0510$ $[+0.025, +0.076]$ for $171.3$ nats spent, a difference of $+0.0505$ $[+0.0155, +0.0860]$ at one
fifty-fourth of the divergence. Handed the same reward on TriviaQA, the meter gains nothing
(Table~\ref{tab:parity}). The authors' $70$B pair and their byte-level decoder repeat it at every
budget, and at this temperature the $70$B base is itself judged no better than the anchor,
$+0.019$ $[-0.004, +0.042]$. Served through its chat template the $8$B beats its own templateless text by
$0.184$, and there the $k=10$ meter, which \emph{is} that model at $99.84\%$ of steps, wins by $0.138$.
\textbf{Every cell the meter wins is certified at $K/S_w \ge 1.25$, that is at nothing, and wherever
$K/S_w < 1$ the meter is judged indistinguishable from its own anchor.}

\textbf{The difference survives fresh draws, judges and controls, and is scoped by the opponent and the
task} (Figure~\ref{fig:h2h}). Re-drawing selection, then every sampled arm, moves it by at most $0.0055$;
five of six judges exclude zero, and the sixth, \texttt{Mixtral-8x7B}, straddles it at $-0.0015$. On the recorded text it replicates under four judges and is bounded below $0.03$ under the one clean frontier judge, whose interval contains zero at a half-width of $0.0247$ over $850$ prompts (Appendix~\ref{app:h2hrepeat}). It
holds under a length control and with empty completions removed, both checks post hoc. It does not hold
against a stronger fixed opponent: from \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} upward every interval covers zero.
And it is a property of prefix completion: it holds on our prompts, on public-domain books and on
BookMIA's unseen half, is unresolved on CoTaEval's news questions and MT-Bench, and reverses on
AlpacaEval, where the meter wins by $0.0329$ (Appendix~\ref{app:workload}).

\label{fig:breadth}

Selection needs only its safe model, so it runs at every openly licensed anchor
\citep{kandpal2025commonpile,langlais2026commoncorpus,bommarito2025kl3m}; the open-\emph{data} families
are excluded because they fail the vetting check of Section~\ref{sec:leakage}, which makes Comma-7B the
largest admissible anchor (Figure~\ref{fig:breadth}). At $n=8$ the gain excludes zero at four of six
anchors in two families, largest at Comma-7B, $+0.126$ $[+0.087, +0.164]$, and does not track the
anchor's own level ($\rho = +0.543$ on the two-judge mean). \textbf{With no judge}, majority vote over
Comma-7B lifts GSM8K exact match from $0.320$ to $0.546$ at $n=32$ ($3.47$ nats), $3.4\times$ the
pointwise reward's best lift, and lifts TriviaQA and MMLU; on TriviaQA the $7$B pointwise reward instead
falls with $n$, so the scorer binds before the mechanism does. The ceiling is the anchor: on TriviaQA the
meter reaches $0.618$ against selection's $0.190$, but only at $k=20$, where it \emph{is} the risky model,
certified at $480$ nats for a $24$-token answer (Figure~\ref{fig:judgefree}, Table~\ref{tab:judgefree}).

\subsection{Leakage}\label{sec:leakage}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{figures/safety_envelope.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Selection reproduces no protected passage, and where its premise fails it amplifies
the anchor by a small constant, not by $n$.} \textbf{(a)} Near-verbatim recall, $50$-token windows,
$100$ held-out passages. Nine arms (six anchors, a paraphrase event, an attacker scoring the $n$ draws by
the \emph{memorising model's own likelihood}, and \texttt{Llama-3.1-70B}) read $0.0000$ at every $n$ to
$64$ and at $n=256$, on every passage. Dashed: the memoriser alone on the same passages and seeds,
$0.3925$ mean and $0.8154$ maximum, and the $70$B, which memorised the novel in pre-training, at $0.2475$
unaided, reproducing two passages in full. \textbf{(b)} Twelve anchors fine-tuned on the passages, attacker
selecting at $n=64$: the certificate permits multiplying the anchor's own rate by $64$; the realised factor
is $1.0$ to $7.0$ (filled) and $1.0$ to $3.0$ on a second, independent draw (hollow).}
\label{fig:safety}
\end{figure}

Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} bounds any event by $n$ times the anchor's own rate, so a scorer can
only amplify leakage the anchor already has. Against an attacker who scores the $n$ draws by the
memorising model's own likelihood, and against a paraphrase event, near-verbatim recall is $0.0000$ at
every anchor, at every $n$ to $64$ and at $n=256$ (Figure~\ref{fig:safety}a); on $100$ passages the zero bounds the
per-passage rate below $3.0\%$ at $95\%$. The zero verifies the anchor, not the mechanism: all six
anchors read $0$ on a vetting screen at every prefix length from $20$ to $200$ tokens, while the
open-data \texttt{OLMo-2-7B} and \texttt{OLMo-2-13B} leak on up to $2$ and $6$ of $50$ passages
\citep{olmo2024olmo2} (Appendix~\ref{app:vetting}). Breaking the premise on purpose, with anchors
fine-tuned on the passages, the realised amplification at $n=64$ is $1.0$ to $7.0$ on one draw and $1.0$
to $3.0$ on a second (Figure~\ref{fig:safety}b): selection multiplies what the anchor leaks and is no
floor under it.

Selection pays in draws (Table~\ref{tab:cost}). Counted in forward passes, $n=64$ costs $61.3\times$ the
meter, most of it the reward model's prefill; on the clock the reward pass is $9.3\%$ and the draws are
the price, so the lever is $n$ and not the scorer. Unbatched, on a server that runs only the anchor, the
draws cost $21.8\times$ a metered decode; that is the throughput price. Batched, a request is one decode
loop that never runs the risky model, and selection serves it in $0.22\times$ the meter's time at the
authors' $70$B pair and $0.51\times$ at the $8$B one. \textbf{Held to the meter's own compute,
selection loses}: the matched cell is $n=2$ at $0.68\times$, gaining $-0.0330$ $[-0.0625, -0.0025]$
against the meter. A $3.9\times$ larger safe model served alone is no substitute: it gains $+0.015$
$[-0.0065, +0.0365]$ over the small one, a seventh of selection's gain, at $86\times$ less compute
(Appendix~\ref{app:biggeranchor}). Majority vote has no scorer at all, $5.75\times$ the meter's forward
passes at $n=32$, and where an answer is checkable it dominates the pointwise reward at every scorer up to $7.6$B --- but not at $14$B, which overtakes four draws of it on both tasks and every $n$ on TriviaQA (Appendix~\ref{app:scorerfree}).
