\subsection{Why a per-token rate is vacuous, or sparse}\label{sec:frontier}

Where the risky model continues text, selection anchoring buys more utility than a metered decoder
for a fraction of the divergence, under a strictly stronger order. Can the meter be repaired to match it? The obstruction is one line of the
chain rule --- a statement about the \emph{shape} a per-token policy must take, not a lower bound on
what one could achieve.

\begin{proposition}[A bounded budget is sparse]\label{prop:sparse}
Let $q$ be any causal policy, $q(y) = \prod_{t \le T} q_t(y_t \mid y_{<t})$, with sequence budget
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$, and write
$N_\varepsilon = \#\{t \le T : D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t(\cdot \mid y_{<t}) \,\|\, p_{s,t}) > \varepsilon\}$.
Then $\mathbb{E}_q[N_\varepsilon] \le K/\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$.
\end{proposition}

The proof is the exact chain rule followed by Markov at each step, and it constrains the
\emph{shape} of a spend, not its size. Read forwards, a bounded budget is \emph{sparse}: the policy
coincides with the anchor at all but $O(K/\varepsilon)$ steps. Read backwards, a policy that moves the
distribution at a constant fraction of its steps --- as a per-token \emph{rate} does by construction
--- has a budget that is $\Omega(T)$. \textbf{The second horn is a shape, not a verdict}: selection's
served law factorises like any other, so at its own $3.175$ nats fewer than four steps of a
$204$-token completion may carry a whole nat, and it is judged above parity with the risky model,
at $0.555$ (Figure~\ref{fig:selection}b). What the bound forbids is \emph{spreading} a bounded budget, not
concentrating one, so it rules out a shape and not the class: a per-\emph{query} rule such as
CP-$\Delta$/CP-$k$ fusion \citep{abad2025cpfuse} is outside it, and whether a \emph{causal} policy can
concentrate well enough to match selection stays open (Section~\ref{sec:orders}).

\textbf{The class and the rates.} Making both horns measurable takes three quantities and no
attack. A \emph{bucket-metered decoder} serves, at each step, the largest tilt $\theta_t \in [0,1]$
along the geometric path $p_\theta \propto p_s^{1-\theta} p_r^{\theta}$ whose charge fits the
allowance $k_t$ of a token bucket of rate $k$ started at a debt $\delta$; the charge is the R\'enyi
divergence $c_\alpha(\theta) = D_\alpha(p_\theta \| p_s)$ of order $\alpha \in [1,\infty]$, closed
form in Eq.~\eqref{eq:renyi-path}. Its $\alpha \to 1$ limit is the deployed decoder's charge and its
$\alpha \to \infty$ limit is the pathwise charge selection anchoring satisfies for free, so the
family contains both published accounting rules. For a protected work $x$ of $N$ characters, with
$s_1,\dots,s_T$ the safe model's
per-token surprisals along $x$, $N_t$ the characters consumed after $t$ tokens and
$S(x) = \sum_t s_t$, three rates govern everything below:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{s(x) = \frac{S(x)}{N}}_{\text{surprisal rate}}, \qquad
\underbrace{k_{\mathrm{crit}}(x) = \max_{t \le T} \frac{\sum_{i \le t} s_i + \delta}{N_t}}_{\text{Loynes running maximum}}, \qquad
\underbrace{c_{\mathrm{use}} = \operatorname{med}_{y \sim p_r} \frac{\log p_r(y) - \log p_s(y)}{|y|}}_{\text{divergence rate of ordinary traffic}} .
\label{eq:rates}
\end{equation}
All three need only forward passes. Rates are nats per
\emph{character} where safe models with different vocabularies are compared, per \emph{token} where
a rate meets a budget (Appendix~\ref{app:opening}).

The other side of the dichotomy has a price too.
\begin{theorem}[The price of utility]\label{thm:nfl}
Let $q$ be \emph{any} law with $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$ --- in particular the output of
a decoder of the class above run to a per-trajectory budget $K$ under any charge $c_\alpha$,
$\alpha \in [1,\infty]$, every one of which dominates the KL. For every bounded utility $U$,
\begin{equation}
K \;\ge\; \Lambda^*_s\!\bigl(\mathbb{E}_q[U]\bigr), \qquad
\Lambda^*_s(u) = \sup_{\lambda}\;\bigl\{\lambda u - \log \mathbb{E}_{p_s}[e^{\lambda U}]\bigr\},
\label{eq:nfl}
\end{equation}
the Cram\'er rate function of $U$ under the safe model.
\end{theorem}

The proof is Donsker--Varadhan at the sequence level (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}); nothing about the
decoder enters, which is why it prices selection anchoring as readily as a meter. The optimum it
describes is the exponential tilt of $p_s$ toward $U$, the law KL-regularised alignment targets
\citep{korbak2022rl,rafailov2023dpo} and best-of-$n$ approaches
\citep{gui2024bonbon,yang2024asymptotics}, and it is not loose \citep{monteiropaes2026limits}. Read beside
Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} it settles the shape of the problem: reproducing a particular work
costs $S(x)$ nats and grows with its length --- $849$ for the median target
(Figure~\ref{fig:selection}a), and $159.8$ for the $50$-token window our extraction numbers are
actually scored on, which is vacuous at $k \ge 0.8$ (Appendix~\ref{app:windowvacuity}) --- while
buying mean utility $u$ costs $\Lambda^*_s(u)$, $O(1)$ \emph{however long the sequence} for any
utility the safe model itself sometimes attains. There is room for a mechanism both safe and useful,
and a rate cannot occupy it.

\begin{proposition}[Where its bucket is slack, a meter pays the imitation cost]\label{prop:imitation}
Let a decoder of the class run under the deployed charge $\alpha=1$, write
$Z_T = \sum_{t \le T} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\theta_t} \| p_{s,t})$ for its realised sequence divergence
and $\mathcal{S}$ for the steps at which the bucket is \emph{slack}, $|\mathcal{S}| = (1-\beta)T$.
Then $Z_T \ge \sum_{t \in \mathcal{S}} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{r,t} \| p_{s,t})
\ge (1-\beta) T \min_{t \in \mathcal{S}} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{r,t} \| p_{s,t})$, which does not depend
on the utility achieved. If moreover $\beta$ is bounded away from $1$, the slack steps carry
divergence bounded away from $0$, and $U$'s best outcome is one the safe model itself reaches at a
rate not vanishing in $T$, so that $\Lambda^*_s \le -\log \Pr_{p_s}[U = u_{\max}]$ is $O(1)$, then
the overhead $Z_T / \Lambda^*_s(\mathbb{E}_q[U])$ is $\Omega(T)$.
\end{proposition}

The third proviso is the substantive one: a utility only an exponentially rare sequence attains has
$\Lambda^*_s = \Theta(T)$ too and no separation survives (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). Winning a judged
comparison is not of that kind: under single-order judging the anchor's own draw wins over a quarter
of them, so winning \emph{every} one, at any length, would cost an optimal policy $1.30$ nats, and the
decoder spends $132$ times that to reach parity. Where the bucket is slack the largest feasible tilt
is $\theta_t = 1$, so the step is charged the full cost of \emph{imitating} the risky model, wanted or
not, and both factors are \emph{measured} over $16{,}500$ logged trajectories: $\beta$ falls from $0.954$ at $k=0.1$ to
$0.033$ at $k=3$ and $0.0000$ at $k=20$, the rate it multiplies saturates at $0.857$ nats per token,
and the running spend is linear in the step index at every budget (median $R^2 \ge 0.97$). Realised spend stalls at
$171.30$ nats, $4.3\%$ of the allowance, while the cap quadruples, and all four hold at three
further pairs, the authors' own $70$B among them, at rates $0.617$, $0.303$, $0.908$
(Table~\ref{tab:imitation-pairs}).

A second, weaker boundary is the Loynes running maximum \citep{loynes1962stability}: a budget
publishes a \emph{drift} where safety is a \emph{workload maximum} (Proposition~\ref{prop:outrun},
Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}). Over ten safe models the margin $s(x)/c_{\mathrm{use}}$ rises
monotonically with capability in every family (Appendix~\ref{app:scaling}): the raw material is
improving and a per-token meter cannot use it.
