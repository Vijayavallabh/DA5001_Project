\section{Why a per-token rate cannot be repaired}\label{sec:frontier}

Could a metered decoder be repaired to certify as little as selection does? One line of the chain rule
fixes the shape its spend must take.

\begin{proposition}[A bounded budget is sparse]\label{prop:sparse}
Let $q$ be any causal policy, $q(y) = \prod_{t \le T} q_t(y_t \mid y_{<t})$, with sequence budget
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$, and write
$N_\varepsilon = \#\{t \le T : D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t(\cdot \mid y_{<t}) \,\|\, p_{s,t}) > \varepsilon\}$.
Then $\mathbb{E}_q[N_\varepsilon] \le K/\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$.
\end{proposition}

The proof is the exact chain rule and Markov's inequality at each step (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). A
per-token allowance therefore has two options. If it grows with the work, $K = kT$, it is vacuous from
$k = s(x)$ on at every order (Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}). If it stays bounded, the policy departs
from the anchor by more than $\varepsilon$ at $O(K/\varepsilon)$ steps and is the anchor everywhere
else. The bound constrains the \emph{shape} of a spend, not the class: selection's served law factorises
like any other and satisfies it (at $3.175$ nats, fewer than four of its $204$ steps carry a whole nat)
because its budget is concentrated on a choice the decode loop does not contain. Whether a causal policy
can concentrate as well is open.

\begin{theorem}[The price of utility]\label{thm:nfl}
Let $q$ be \emph{any} law with $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$. For every bounded utility $U$,
\begin{equation}
K \;\ge\; \Lambda^*_s\!\bigl(\mathbb{E}_q[U]\bigr), \qquad
\Lambda^*_s(u) = \sup_{\lambda}\;\bigl\{\lambda u - \log \mathbb{E}_{p_s}[e^{\lambda U}]\bigr\},
\label{eq:nfl}
\end{equation}
the Cram\'er rate function of $U$ under the safe model.
\end{theorem}

The proof is Donsker--Varadhan at the sequence level (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}), and nothing about the
decoder enters. Reproducing a work costs $S(x)$, which grows with its length: $849$ nats for the median
protected target and $159.8$ for a $50$-token window. Buying mean utility $u$ costs $\Lambda^*_s(u)$,
which is $O(1)$ at any length for a utility the safe model itself sometimes attains. There is room for a
mechanism that is both safe and useful, and a rate cannot occupy it: where its bucket is slack, a metered
decoder is charged the full cost of imitating the risky model whatever utility that buys.

\begin{proposition}[Where its bucket is slack, a meter pays the imitation cost]\label{prop:imitation}
Let a bucket-metered decoder, which serves the largest tilt $p_{\theta_t} \propto
p_s^{1-\theta_t}p_r^{\theta_t}$ whose KL charge fits the bucket, run for $T$ steps, and let $\mathcal{S}$
be the steps at which the bucket is slack, $|\mathcal{S}| = (1-\beta)T$. Its realised sequence divergence
satisfies $Z_T \ge \sum_{t \in \mathcal{S}} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{r,t} \| p_{s,t})$, whatever the utility
achieved. If $\beta$ is bounded away from $1$, the slack steps carry divergence bounded away from $0$ and
$U$'s best outcome is one the safe model reaches with probability not vanishing in $T$, then
$Z_T / \Lambda^*_s(\mathbb{E}_q[U]) = \Omega(T)$.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{The deployed meter takes both horns (Figure~\ref{fig:horns}).} Where extraction begins is
where the certificate becomes vacuous: on nine pairs the onset lies between $0.88$ and $1.17$ of $s(x)$,
though one pair spans $0.88$ to $1.35$ when only its memoriser's fine-tuning length changes, so the level
is the claim and the ordering is not (Appendix~\ref{app:onset}). Where the budget certifies something the
meter binds and is judged indistinguishable from its own anchor (Table~\ref{tab:served}); the share of
steps at which it binds falls from $0.954$ at $k=0.1$ to $0.033$ at $k=3$ and $0.0000$ at $k=20$, and past
the imitation rate of $0.857$ nats per token it serves the risky model unchanged and spends $171.3$ nats,
$4.3\%$ of the $4000$ it certifies at $k=20$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:imitation}). None of this contradicts
the mechanism's own guarantee: no per-trajectory violation occurs in more than $100{,}000$ budgeted trajectories.
The gap is between what the budget bounds and what $k$ is taken to mean.

The repairs a meter has left each land on a horn or fall short of selection (Table~\ref{tab:repairs}).
What they share is the currency they buy with, likelihood under $p_r$. As a criterion for choosing
among the anchor's own draws it predicts judged quality no better than the answer's length does; as
the first scorer we registered it moved judged utility by $-0.006$ against a band of $+0.077$, where a
circular oracle reaches $+0.488$ (Appendix~\ref{app:currency}); as a direction to tilt generation it
buys the risky model's quality only by becoming the risky model.
