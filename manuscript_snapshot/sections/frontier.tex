\section{What a per-token rate can certify}\label{sec:frontier}

Could a metered decoder be made to certify as little as selection does? For a budget on the whole
sequence, one line of the chain rule fixes the shape its spend must take.

\begin{proposition}[A bounded budget is sparse]\label{prop:sparse}
Let $q$ be any causal policy, $q(y) = \prod_{t \le T} q_t(y_t \mid y_{<t})$, with sequence budget
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$, and write
$N_\varepsilon = \#\{t \le T : D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t(\cdot \mid y_{<t}) \,\|\, p_{s,t}) > \varepsilon\}$.
Then $\mathbb{E}_q[N_\varepsilon] \le K/\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$.
\end{proposition}

The proof is the exact chain rule and Markov's inequality at each step (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). A
per-token allowance on the whole sequence therefore has two options. If it grows with the work, $K = kT$, it is vacuous from
$k = s(x)$ on at every order (Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}). If it stays bounded, the policy departs
from the anchor by more than $\varepsilon$ at $O(K/\varepsilon)$ steps and is the anchor everywhere
else. The bound constrains the \emph{shape} of a spend, not the class: selection's served law satisfies it
(at $3.175$ nats, fewer than four of its $204$ steps carry a whole nat) because its budget sits on a choice
the decode loop does not contain; whether a causal policy can concentrate as well is open.

\begin{theorem}[The price of utility]\label{thm:nfl}
Let $q$ be \emph{any} law with $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$. For every bounded utility $U$,
\begin{equation}
K \;\ge\; \Lambda^*_s\!\bigl(\mathbb{E}_q[U]\bigr), \qquad
\Lambda^*_s(u) = \sup_{\lambda}\;\bigl\{\lambda u - \log \mathbb{E}_{p_s}[e^{\lambda U}]\bigr\},
\label{eq:nfl}
\end{equation}
the Cram\'er rate function of $U$ under the safe model.
\end{theorem}

The proof is Donsker--Varadhan at the sequence level (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}), and nothing about the
decoder enters. Reproducing a work costs $S(x)$, which grows with its length: $849$ nats for the median
protected target and $159.8$ for a $50$-token window. Buying mean utility $u$ costs $\Lambda^*_s(u)$,
which is $O(1)$ at any length for a utility the safe model itself sometimes attains. There is room for a
mechanism that is both safe and useful; a meter that tilts toward the risky model does not occupy it,
because where its bucket is slack it is charged the full cost of imitation whatever utility that buys.

\begin{proposition}[Where its bucket is slack, a meter pays the imitation cost]\label{prop:imitation}
Let a bucket-metered decoder serve at each step the largest tilt $p_{\theta_t} \propto
p_s^{1-\theta_t}p_r^{\theta_t}$ whose KL charge fits its bucket, and call a step slack when
$\theta_t = 1$, and let $I_T$ sum $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{r,t}\,\|\,p_{s,t})$ over the slack steps. Then
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\,\|\,p_s) \ge \mathbb{E}_q[I_T]$, whatever the utility achieved. If
$\mathbb{E}_q[I_T] \ge \gamma T$, and a bounded utility $U$ that the decoder does not lower attains its
maximum under $p_s$ with probability at least $\pi > 0$, then
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\,\|\,p_s) \ge \Lambda^*_s(\mathbb{E}_q[U])\cdot\gamma T/\log(1/\pi)$.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{The deployed meter shows both options (Figure~\ref{fig:horns}).} By the time extraction begins
the certificate is void: on nine pairs the onset lies between $0.88$ and $1.17$ of $s(x)$, near the
whole-target threshold, and one pair spans $0.88$ to $1.35$ when only its memoriser's fine-tuning length
changes, so the level is the claim and the ordering is not; two mechanistic predictions of the onset
failed (Appendix~\ref{app:onset}). Where the budget certifies something the meter binds and is
judged indistinguishable from its own anchor (Table~\ref{tab:matched}); the share of steps its bucket
constrains falls from $0.954$ at $k=0.1$ to $0.033$ at $k=3$ and $0.0000$ at $k=20$, and past the
imitation rate $\gamma = 0.857$ nats per token it serves the risky model unchanged and spends $171.3$ nats,
$4.3\%$ of the $4000$ it certifies at $k=20$. The anchor wins both presentation orders on $2.2\%$ of
prompts, so $\log(1/\pi) = 3.82$ and Proposition~\ref{prop:imitation} puts that spend at no less than $44.9$
times what winning every comparison requires, a coarse, prompt-averaged figure, silent in one prompt class;
at the utility the meter reaches, $\Lambda^*_s$ is $0.023$ nats and the ratio $7{,}384$
(Appendix~\ref{app:frontierratio}). No per-trajectory violation occurs in over $100{,}000$ budgeted
trajectories; the gap is between what the budget bounds and how $k$ is read.

The repairs a meter has left each land on one of the two options or fall short of selection
(Appendix~\ref{app:repairs}, Table~\ref{tab:repairs}): a sharper charge ($\alpha = 8$) cuts oracle recall
from $0.0965$ to $0.0012$ only by binding on $99.6\%$ of steps against $0.4\%$, a lower cap ($k=0.5$) is its
anchor, a better schedule buys $3$ to $13\%$ more fidelity at $k \le 1$ and under $1.4\%$ at $k=3$, and a
budget granted up front gains $+0.0545$ $[+0.0315, +0.0775]$ at $64$ nats against selection's $+0.1045$. They
share a currency, likelihood under $p_r$: to choose
among draws it predicts judged quality no better than the answer's length, moving judged utility
by $-0.006$ against a band of $+0.077$ where a circular oracle reaches $+0.488$
(Appendix~\ref{app:currency}), and to tilt generation it buys the risky model's quality only
by becoming the risky model.

\paragraph{What the dichotomy does not cover, built and measured.}\label{sec:uncovered} Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}
constrains a budget on the whole sequence. A meter can instead cap each step's max-divergence by $W$ minus
the largest realised log-ratio of a recent span, so every span of at most $w$ tokens is certified at $W$
given the text before it. Ordinary text's realised log-ratio per $50$-token window has median
$40.3$ nats (Appendix~\ref{app:windowlr}). We built it ($w=50$; Section~\ref{sec:matched},
Appendix~\ref{app:windowed}):
it certifies the \emph{exact} window, the median one at $e^{W-159.8}$, yet a memoriser served through it
reproduces near-verbatim spans from $W=80$, because a near-verbatim variant is another event, whose anchor
probability has no closed form and at $W$ nats could be bounded only with more than $3e^{W}$ clean draws
(Table~\ref{tab:certificate}). Selection spent in installments, $n$ draws per block of $L$ tokens, is
certified by the same composition at $(\lceil w/L\rceil + 1)\log n$ for any $w$-token span given what
precedes it, and it buys more: at $64$ draws a block, $10$-token installments beat one choice by $+0.0415$
$[+0.0185, +0.0645]$, reproduced on a fresh draw, at $25$ nats a window, while held
to one whole-output $\log 64$ they lose (Appendix~\ref{app:blockwise}). At $1{,}000$ tokens one choice among $64$
still gains $+0.063$ $[+0.043, +0.083]$, and installments of $8$ draws a block lose to it (Appendix~\ref{app:long}). Both sample a bounded tilt of the
anchor, $p_s h$ with $h \le e^{\lambda}$ at every length (Appendix~\ref{app:blockwise}); whether a causal
policy that consults $p_r$ can realise one without paying the imitation cost is the question
Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse} leaves open. What separates the mechanisms is not whether the budget is charged once or per token but what
it buys: a choice among the anchor's own draws, or the risky model's tokens.
