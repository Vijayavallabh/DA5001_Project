\section{What a per-token budget certifies, and what it buys}\label{sec:frontier}

Every mechanism in this family shares one design: a charge is levied per decoded token, accumulated
in a token bucket, and capped. This section says what such a cap certifies, what it can buy, and how both scale with the length of
the work.

\paragraph{The class and the rates}
Fix a prompt and write $p_s$ for the safe model, $p_r$ for the risky model, and
$p_\theta \propto p_s^{1-\theta} p_r^{\theta}$ for the geometric path between them, so that
$\log p_\theta = \log p_s + \theta \ell - \log Z(\theta)$ with $\ell = \log p_r - \log p_s$ and
$Z(u) = \mathbb{E}_{p_s}[e^{u\ell}]$. A \emph{bucket-metered decoder} serves the largest $\theta_t \in [0,1]$ with $c(\theta_t) \le k_t$,
for $k_t$ the allowance of a token bucket of rate $k$ started at a debt $\delta$. The charge is the
R\'enyi divergence of order $\alpha \in [1,\infty]$,
\begin{equation}
c_\alpha(\theta) \;=\; D_\alpha(p_\theta \,\|\, p_s)
 \;=\; \frac{\log Z(\alpha\theta) - \alpha \log Z(\theta)}{\alpha - 1},
\label{eq:renyi-path}
\end{equation}
whose $\alpha \to 1$ limit is $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_\theta \| p_s)$, the charge of the
deployed decoder, and whose $\alpha \to \infty$ limit is $\max_v \log p_\theta(v)/p_s(v)$, the
pathwise charge, so the family contains both published accounting rules and interpolates between a
guarantee about an average and one about every event. Over $T_{\max}$ tokens the published budget is
$K = k\,T_{\max}$, and it is this product, not $k$, the certificate is written against. For a
protected work $x$ of $N$ characters, with $s_1,\dots,s_T$ the safe model's per-token surprisals
along $x$, $N_t$ the characters consumed after $t$ tokens and $S(x) = \sum_t s_t$, three rates
govern everything below:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{s(x) = \frac{S(x)}{N}}_{\text{surprisal rate}}, \qquad
\underbrace{k_{\mathrm{crit}}(x) = \max_{t \le T} \frac{\sum_{i \le t} s_i + \delta}{N_t}}_{\text{Loynes running maximum}}, \qquad
\underbrace{c_{\mathrm{use}} = \operatorname{med}_{y \sim p_r} \frac{\log p_r(y) - \log p_s(y)}{|y|}}_{\text{divergence rate of ordinary traffic}} .
\label{eq:rates}
\end{equation}
All three need only forward passes --- no decoding, no attack, no access to the mechanism --- so a
deployer can compute them in advance. Rates are nats per \emph{character} where safe models with
different vocabularies are compared and per \emph{token} where a rate meets a budget; both
denominators appear in Appendix~\ref{app:opening}.

\begin{proposition}[The vacuity threshold does not depend on the order]\label{prop:threshold}
Let a decoder of the class above run to a sequence budget $K$ under the charge $c_\alpha$, and let
$E$ be the event that it emits $x$. For every $\alpha \in [1,\infty]$ the tightest bound on
$\Pr[E]$ implied by the budget equals $1$ if and only if $K \ge S(x)$.
\end{proposition}

The proof (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}) is three standard conversions --- the $\alpha=\infty$ change
of measure, the R\'enyi-to-probability bound underwriting reconstruction robustness in R\'enyi
differential privacy \citep{mironov2017renyi}, and the binary-KL inversion. None is new; the
contribution is that all three break in the same place. Raising $\alpha$ buys a strictly tighter
bound \emph{below} the threshold --- at $S=200$, $K=100$ the cap falls from $0.50$ to $e^{-100}$ ---
and moves it not at all, so the quantity carrying risk is $k/s(x)$, never $k$.
\citet{vyas2023provable} already report their budget as a fraction of the safe model's entropy; new
here is the exact threshold, for a \emph{specific} work and every order at once.

A second, weaker boundary sits above it: the rate needed to emit $x$ at all is the Loynes running
maximum of the surprisal process \citep{loynes1962stability}, so a budget publishes a \emph{drift}
where safety is a \emph{workload maximum} (Proposition~\ref{prop:outrun}). The two bracket a regime
where the decoder protects the work and no budget in the family can say so --- a median $4.25$ to
$6.10$ in units of $s$, collapsing to $1.32$--$1.48$ once an adversary supplies one genuine prefix
token (Appendix~\ref{app:opening}).

\begin{theorem}[The price of utility]\label{thm:nfl}
Let a decoder of the class above run to a per-trajectory sequence budget $K$ under any charge
$c_\alpha$, $\alpha \in [1,\infty]$, and write $q$ for the law of its output. For every bounded
utility $U$,
\begin{equation}
K \;\ge\; \Lambda^*_s\!\bigl(\mathbb{E}_q[U]\bigr), \qquad
\Lambda^*_s(u) = \sup_{\lambda}\;\bigl\{\lambda u - \log \mathbb{E}_{p_s}[e^{\lambda U}]\bigr\},
\label{eq:nfl}
\end{equation}
the Cram\'er rate function of $U$ under the safe model.
\end{theorem}

The proof is Donsker--Varadhan at the sequence level (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). It is a converse,
holding for the optimal policy, and that optimum is not loose: the maximum reward purchasable at a
fixed KL budget has a closed form governed by a Jeffreys divergence rather than the
$\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}}$ of earlier analyses \citep{monteiropaes2026limits}.

\begin{proposition}[A per-token meter pays the imitation cost, not the utility cost]\label{prop:imitation}
Let a decoder of the class run under the deployed charge $\alpha=1$ and write
$Z_T = \sum_{t \le T} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\theta_t} \| p_{s,t})$ for its realised sequence divergence.
If the bucket constraint binds on a fraction $\beta$ of steps then
$Z_T \ge (1-\beta)\sum_{t} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{r,t} \| p_{s,t})$, which is linear in $T$ and does not
depend on the utility achieved. Since $\Lambda^*_s(u) \le -\log \Pr_{p_s}[U = u_{\max}]$ for every
bounded $U$, the overhead $Z_T / \Lambda^*_s(\mathbb{E}_q[U])$ is $\Omega(T)$ whenever $\beta$ is
bounded away from $1$.
\end{proposition}

The proof is two lines (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}): where the constraint is slack the largest
feasible $\theta_t$ is $1$, so the step is charged the full $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{r,t}\|p_{s,t})$; and
$\Lambda^*_s$ is capped at its value on the best outcome. Both factors are \emph{measured, not
assumed}, over $16{,}500$ logged trajectories: $\beta$ falls from $0.954$ at $k=0.1$ to $0.033$ at
$k=3$ and $0.0000$ at $k=20$, while the rate it multiplies saturates at $0.857$ nats per token and
the running spend is linear in the step index at every budget (median $R^2 \ge 0.97$).
Realised spend therefore stalls at $171.30$ nats, $4.3\%$ of the allowance the certificate is
written against, while the cap quadruples. All four hold at three further pairs, the authors'
own $70$B among them, at rates $0.617$, $0.303$, $0.908$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).

\paragraph{The dichotomy}
Read the theorem beside Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} and one comparison does all the work.
Reproducing a particular work costs $S(x)$ nats, growing with its length --- $849$ for a protected
target in Section~\ref{sec:onset} --- while buying mean utility $u$ costs $\Lambda^*_s(u)$, which
for bounded $U$ is $O(1)$ \emph{however long the sequence}. A certificate and a useful mechanism are
therefore not intrinsically in conflict. What puts them there is the per-token axis, and the reason
is one line of the chain rule.

\begin{proposition}[An affordable causal policy is the anchor almost everywhere]\label{prop:sparse}
Let $q$ be any causal policy, $q(y) = \prod_{t \le T} q_t(y_t \mid y_{<t})$, with sequence budget
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \,\|\, p_s) \le K$, and write
$N_\varepsilon = \#\{t \le T : D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t(\cdot \mid y_{<t}) \,\|\, p_{s,t}) > \varepsilon\}$.
Then $\mathbb{E}_q[N_\varepsilon] \le K/\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$.
\end{proposition}

The proof is the exact chain rule
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|p_s) = \sum_t \mathbb{E}_q[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_t\|p_{s,t})]$ followed by Markov at
each step; its force is the contrapositive, that a policy moving the distribution at a constant
fraction of its steps has a budget that is $\Omega(T)$. Either $K$ grows with the work ---
\emph{vacuous} --- or the decoder is the anchor at all but $O(1)$ steps --- \emph{trivial}. Nothing
here forbids a causal policy from \emph{concentrating} a bounded budget; it forbids one from
spreading it, which is what a rate does by construction.

Both rates are measurable without any mechanism and are separating --- over ten safe models and
three openly licensed corpora the margin $s(x)/c_{\mathrm{use}}$ rises monotonically with capability,
correcting an earlier audit~\citep{vijayavallabh2026audit} that read the protected rate alone
(Appendix~\ref{app:scaling}). The raw material is improving; a per-token meter cannot use it.
