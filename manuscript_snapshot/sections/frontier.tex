\section{The frontier of a metered decoder}\label{sec:frontier}

\paragraph{The class}
Fix a prompt and write $p_s$ for the safe model, $p_r$ for the risky model, and
$p_\theta \propto p_s^{1-\theta} p_r^{\theta}$ for the geometric path between them, so that
$\log p_\theta = \log p_s + \theta \ell - \log Z(\theta)$ with $\ell = \log p_r - \log p_s$ and
$Z(u) = \mathbb{E}_{p_s}[e^{u\ell}]$. A \emph{bucket-metered decoder} serves, at each step $t$, the
largest $\theta_t \in [0,1]$ whose charge satisfies $c(\theta_t) \le k_t$, where $c$ is nondecreasing
with $c(0)=0$ and $k_t$ is the allowance of a token bucket of rate $k$ started at a debt
$\delta$. The charge we consider is the R\'enyi divergence of order $\alpha \in [1,\infty]$,
\begin{equation}
c_\alpha(\theta) \;=\; D_\alpha\!\left(p_\theta \,\|\, p_s\right)
 \;=\; \frac{\log Z(\alpha\theta) - \alpha \log Z(\theta)}{\alpha - 1},
\label{eq:renyi-path}
\end{equation}
whose $\alpha \to 1$ limit is $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_\theta \| p_s)$, the charge of the audited decoder,
and whose $\alpha \to \infty$ limit is $\max_v \log p_\theta(v)/p_s(v)$, the pathwise charge. The
family therefore contains both published accounting rules and interpolates between a guarantee about
an average and a guarantee about every event.

\paragraph{The three rates}
For a protected work $x$ of $N$ characters, let $s_1,\dots,s_T$ be the safe model's per-token
surprisals along $x$, $N_t$ the characters consumed after $t$ tokens, and $S(x) = \sum_t s_t$. Define
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{s(x) = \frac{S(x)}{N}}_{\text{surprisal rate}}, \qquad
\underbrace{k_{\mathrm{crit}}(x) = \max_{t \le T} \frac{\sum_{i \le t} s_i + \delta}{N_t}}_{\text{Lindley running maximum}}, \qquad
\underbrace{c_{\mathrm{use}} = \operatorname{med}_{y \sim p_r} \frac{\log p_r(y) - \log p_s(y)}{|y|}}_{\text{divergence rate of ordinary traffic}} .
\label{eq:rates}
\end{equation}
These are nats per \emph{character}, because §\ref{sec:scaling} compares ten safe models with
three vocabularies and only a per-character rate is comparable across them. The choice is
immaterial where it could matter: Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} concerns the \emph{total}
$S(x)$, which has no rate units, and $k_{\mathrm{crit}}/s(x)$ is a ratio, reported under both
denominators in Appendix~\ref{app:opening}. Where a rate meets a \emph{budget} rather than another
rate --- §\ref{sec:onset} --- both are per token, since the mechanism meters $K = k\,T_{\max}$ in
tokens.

\begin{proposition}[The vacuity threshold does not depend on the order]\label{prop:threshold}
Let a decoder of the class above run to a sequence budget $K$ under the charge $c_\alpha$, and let
$E$ be the event that it emits $x$. For every $\alpha \in [1,\infty]$ the tightest bound on
$\Pr[E]$ implied by the budget equals $1$ if and only if $K \ge S(x)$.
\end{proposition}

The proof (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}) is three standard conversions --- the $\alpha=\infty$
change of measure, the R\'enyi-to-probability bound that underwrites reconstruction robustness in
R\'enyi differential privacy \citep{mironov2017renyi}, and the binary-KL inversion. None is new;
the contribution is that all three break in the same place.
Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} is the reason the failure cannot be repaired by strengthening the
accounting. Raising $\alpha$ buys a strictly tighter bound \emph{below} the threshold --- at
$S = 200$, $K = 100$ the cap falls from $0.50$ at $\alpha=1$ to $e^{-100}$ at $\alpha=\infty$ ---
and moves the threshold not at all. A deployer who finds the certificate uninformative at a given
$k$ has exactly one remedy inside this class, namely a smaller $k$, and that is the remedy
§\ref{sec:orders} prices.

\begin{proposition}[Protection outruns certification]\label{prop:outrun}
Under the banking rule, if the decoder emits each token of $x$ with probability at least $1-\eta$
then $k \ge (1-\eta)\,k_{\mathrm{crit}}(x) - \log 2$. Moreover
$k_{\mathrm{crit}}(x) \ge s(x) + \delta/N$ for every work, with equality if and only if the
surprisal accumulates at a constant rate.
\end{proposition}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}; it needs only that the charge dominates the KL, which
every order $\alpha \ge 1$ does, so the protection boundary is a property of the family and not of
one accounting rule. Neither is $k_{\mathrm{crit}}$ our invention. It is the Loynes running maximum
that represents the workload of a queue \citep{loynes1962stability}, and equivalently the smallest
rate of a $(\sigma,\rho)$ arrival curve that admits the surprisal process
\citep{cruz1991calculus,leboudec2001network}. Naming it that way is what makes the diagnosis
concrete: a budget publishes a drift where safety is a workload maximum, and the queueing
literature has known for sixty years that the two come apart.

Together the two propositions bracket a regime. Below $s(x)$ the certificate says something and the
decoder is heavily constrained. Above $k_{\mathrm{crit}}(x)$ the work is reproducible. On
$[s(x), k_{\mathrm{crit}}(x))$ the decoder still protects the work and no budget in the family can
say so: the certificate reports the drift of the surprisal process and safety is governed by its
running maximum. This is the same distinction a token bucket makes between a mean rate and a
burst, and it is why a scalar budget is the wrong primitive rather than merely a loose one.

\paragraph{How wide the uncertified regime is, and who owns it}
% analysis/opening_effect.py -> results/opening_effect{,_tinycomma18b,_pleias3b,_kl3m37b}{,_token}.csv
Measured on 758 reference passages under four safe models from three openly licensed corpora,
$k_{\mathrm{crit}}/s$ has a median of $4.25$ to $6.10$, so the uncertified interval is wide. It is
also almost entirely an artifact of where a generation starts: the running maximum is attained at
the very first token in $87.7\%$ to $90.5\%$ of cases, because the bucket must pay for the opening
out of one step's allowance, and an adversary who supplies a single genuine prefix token collapses
the ratio to $1.32$--$1.48$, where it stays for $5$, $10$ or $20$ tokens
(Appendix~\ref{app:opening}). The protection is real, and it belongs to the prompt rather than to
the budget.

\paragraph{What utility actually costs, and what the decoder actually pays}
Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} prices one particular event, $\{\text{output} = x\}$, at $S(x)$.
The budget is a single number, so whatever else the decoder buys is drawn from the same account.
The exchange rate for utility is classical.

\begin{theorem}[The price of utility]\label{thm:nfl}
Let a decoder of the class above run to a per-trajectory sequence budget $K$ under any charge
$c_\alpha$, $\alpha \in [1,\infty]$, and write $q$ for the law of its output. For every bounded
utility $U$,
\begin{equation}
K \;\ge\; \Lambda^*_s\!\bigl(\mathbb{E}_q[U]\bigr), \qquad
\Lambda^*_s(u) = \sup_{\lambda}\;\bigl\{\lambda u - \log \mathbb{E}_{p_s}[e^{\lambda U}]\bigr\},
\label{eq:nfl}
\end{equation}
the Cram\'er rate function of $U$ under the safe model.
\end{theorem}

The proof is Donsker--Varadhan at the sequence level (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}); the budget bounds
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|p_s)$ by the chain rule, and the rate function bounds that in turn. It is a
converse, holding for the optimal policy, and that optimum is not loose --- the maximum reward
purchasable at a fixed KL budget has a closed form governed by a Jeffreys divergence rather than the
$\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}}$ of earlier analyses \citep{monteiropaes2026limits}.

\paragraph{The bound is satisfied by four orders of magnitude, and that is the finding}
It would be convenient to read Eq.~\eqref{eq:nfl} as forcing a deployer to spend past the vacuity
threshold to get anything useful. It does not, and we checked rather than assumed. For a bounded
utility the rate function is $O(1)$ nats however long the sequence, while reproducing a work costs
$S(x)$, which is hundreds. Measured on the judged arms, with $U$ the judge's verdict scored
$1/\tfrac12/0$ and its law under $p_s$ read off the anchor-only arm:

\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$k$ & realised spend & $\mathbb{E}_q[U]$ & gain & $\Lambda^*_s$ & spend\,/\,$\Lambda^*_s$ \\
\midrule
$0.5$ & $79.0$  & $0.334$ & $+0.011$ & $0.0003$ & $2.8{\times}10^{5}$ \\
$1$   & $134.6$ & $0.420$ & $+0.097$ & $0.023$  & $5900$ \\
$3$   & $165.0$ & $0.469$ & $+0.146$ & $0.052$  & $3200$ \\
$20$  & $171.3$ & $0.483$ & $+0.160$ & $0.061$  & $2800$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The decoder spends between $79$ and $171$ nats of realised sequence divergence --- not its budget
cap, which it never exhausts --- to buy a utility gain an optimal policy could have bought for
hundredths of a nat. Bootstrapping over judged pairs and taking the \emph{weakest} end of each
interval still leaves the ratio between $1.3\times10^{3}$ and $8.6\times10^{3}$.
So the mechanism's cost is not information-theoretic necessity: it is approximation overhead, and it
is three to four orders of magnitude. A decoder spends divergence on every token of every response,
and almost none of that spend is what makes the output better. Two readings of that are available
and only one is safe. Eq.~\eqref{eq:nfl} is a lower bound attained by \emph{some} distribution, and
nothing says the attaining one is a model anybody would want to serve --- a tilt that games the
judge would also achieve the mean cheaply. What the gap licenses is the negative claim, that the
budget a metered decoder must publish is not set by how much better its output is; it does not
license the constructive claim that a cheap decoder with the same quality exists.

The obvious objection is that a three-valued judge is too blunt a utility to price. The objection
runs the wrong way: bluntness \emph{caps} the rate function. For any bounded $U$,
$\Lambda^*_s(u_{\max}) = -\log \Pr_{p_s}[U = u_{\max}]$, so here winning \emph{every} judged
comparison --- the ceiling of this utility, far above anything the decoder reaches --- would cost an
optimal policy $1.19$ nats, and the decoder spends $144$ times that. A utility can only be expensive
in this sense when its best outcome is exponentially unlikely under the safe model, which is exactly
what an atom $\{\text{output}=x\}$ is and why Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} prices it at $S(x)$.
Extraction and utility differ not because one is measured badly but because one is a rare event and
the other is a bounded average.

What pushes the budget past $s(x)$ is therefore not the price of utility but the price of
\emph{being the risky model}, and a scalar budget cannot separate the two. Closing the gap is the
paper's open problem; one route is to stop decoding greedily against the bucket, as
\citet{tomasi2026primaldual} do with Lagrangian multipliers updated by mirror descent. Until then the measured crossover of
§\ref{sec:orders} is the operative number and Eq.~\eqref{eq:nfl} is a floor far beneath it; the same
overhead is visible per step (§\ref{sec:price}).
