\section{Limitations in full}\label{app:limitations}

\paragraph{One protected corpus}
Every measurement runs on sixteen English genre novels, from CopyBench and BookMIA. The robustness
cells in Appendix~\ref{app:scaling} are nine re-analyses of those same sixteen works, so
$p = 3.1\times10^{-5}$ is simply the smallest value sixteen paired items can produce and should be
read as ``the trend holds for every novel we have'' rather than as independent replication.
Non-literal reproduction, other languages and other genres are untested.

\paragraph{The onset ratio takes two values, and we can name one cause but not the other}
$s(x)$ spans $1.87\times$ across the seven pairs in tokenizer-free units, still short of the dynamic
range a data collapse normally rests on, and at the crossing budgets only one to four works in a
hundred leak. In units of each pair's own $s(x)$ the bootstrap intervals are $[0.84, 1.22]$,
$[0.79, 0.96]$, $[0.84, 1.07]$, $[0.86, 1.08]$, $[0.80, 1.09]$ for the five pairs at about four
characters per token, and $[1.14, 1.41]$ and $[1.02, 1.24]$ for the two KL3M pairs at about two. The
first five overlap freely around $0.90$; both KL3M intervals exclude $1$.

The KL3M pairs differ from the rest in three ways at once --- the tokenizer, targets twice as long
in tokens, and, for one of them, much the weakest memoriser --- so pair six was built to remove one
of the three. It shares the tokenizer and the target length with pair five and memorises
thoroughly, $s_r/s_s = 0.09$ against $0.35$, which places it inside the $0.06$--$0.11$ band of the
four; its ratio is nevertheless $1.053$. Memorisation strength is therefore not what puts a pair on
the far side of the vacuity point, though it does modulate the ratio within a family, and in the
direction opposite to Eq.~\eqref{eq:req}. That leaves the tokenizer confounded with everything else the tokenizer determines, and for a fixed
corpus those are not separate variables. Each benchmark item is a prompt prefix followed by the
protected passage, so a tokenizer that cuts the same text more finely changes three things at once:
the adversary's seed, which is a fixed number of \emph{tokens} and therefore buys $7.3$ words on the
KL3M pairs against $13.0$--$14.4$ on the rest; the number of decode steps before the passage is
reached, $435$ against $195$--$230$; and the target length. All three split the seven pairs exactly
and without overlap, in the same place the onset ratio splits.

\paragraph{A control we ran, and have withdrawn}
We previously reported that truncating the KL3M target to the four-character pairs' step count left
the ratio at $1.091$, and concluded that the step count was not the mechanism. That control was
invalid and the conclusion does not stand. The protected passage begins at token $455$ of the KL3M
tokenisation, and truncating the target to $276$ tokens ends it at token $296$: the passage was
absent from the target for $100$ of $100$ passages, and an adversary reproducing that target
perfectly would have scored a near-verbatim recall of $0.000$. Whatever the run measured, it was not
extraction of the protected passage. The attack script now refuses any configuration in which no
target contains its passage, and reports the covered fraction on every run. We report the
withdrawal rather than quietly dropping the paragraph, because the number appeared in a previous
version of this manuscript.

Pair seven removes the next candidate. Vocabulary size is the obvious reading of ``granularity'',
and the two are confounded across the first six pairs: the fine tokenizer has $32{,}768$ types and
the coarse ones $64{,}000$ to $128{,}256$. Phi-3.5-mini breaks that. Its vocabulary is $32{,}011$
types, smaller than KL3M's, and it still cuts these passages at $3.8$ characters per token; across
every anchor we hold, vocabulary size and characters per token are decorrelated enough that
kl3m-002-170m ($32{,}768$ types, $1.98$ characters) and Phi-3.5-mini ($32{,}011$, $3.78$) differ by
$1.91\times$ in granularity at the same vocabulary scale. Its onset ratio is $0.926$, with the
coarse family. Its memoriser is also the most thorough of the seven, $s_r/s_s = 0.003$, so the
memorisation control repeats at its limit. The pair's own bootstrap interval, $[0.80, 1.09]$, does
not by itself exclude the KL3M values; the claim it supports is the negative one, that vocabulary
size is not the variable. Which property of the tokenizer does move the ratio remains open, and no
anchor we can reach falls between the two granularity groups --- the gap from $2.4$ to $3.4$
characters per token is empty across all fourteen models in our cache, so the next test needs a
tokenizer trained for it rather than chosen from what exists.

We report the proportionality as a unit that locates the onset within about a fifth, not as a law. The third pair was re-measured at $458$
passages after its $100$-passage curve proved too flat through the crossing for a bootstrap to
resolve: a third of resamples never reached the threshold on a grid that stopped at $k = 3.6$, and
extending it to $4.2$ brought that to $0.1\%$ while moving the onset from $3.18$ to $3.27$. The
other six pairs are still $100$ passages each. Building more of them needs a memoriser strong
enough that the \emph{unconstrained} risky model reproduces the passages: a first $350$M attempt
reached $0.71$ near-verbatim recall greedily but only $0.022$ under the sampling the attack uses,
and entered the analysis only after retraining to $0.91$. We admit a pair only if its $k=-1$ recall
exceeds the onset threshold by an order of magnitude.

\paragraph{The no-free-lunch theorem is a converse}
It lower-bounds the budget any policy needs, including the optimal one. Our decoder is causal and
meters with a token bucket, and we do not bound the gap between the two. That gap is large and
measurable --- a factor of $5$ to $32$ between the divergence on the risky model's own rollout and
the divergence paid on a budgeted one --- and closing it is the clearest open problem this paper
leaves.

\paragraph{One judge, one workload}
Utility is scored by a single instruction-tuned model on the benchmark's own prompt classes rather
than deployment traffic. We measure the instrument's resolution rather than assume it --- the same
decoder reached by two different solvers differs by $0.71\sigma$ --- but a second judge would be
better, and $n=150$ resolves only about four points. The order comparison is at matched budget, not
matched utility.
