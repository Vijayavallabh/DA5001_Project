\section{Limitations in full}\label{app:limitations}

\paragraph{One protected corpus}
Every measurement runs on sixteen English genre novels, from CopyBench and BookMIA. The robustness
cells in Appendix~\ref{app:scaling} are nine re-analyses of those same sixteen works, so
$p = 3.1\times10^{-5}$ is simply the smallest value sixteen paired items can produce and should be
read as ``the trend holds for every novel we have'' rather than as independent replication.
Non-literal reproduction, other languages and other genres are untested. The caveat is not uniform
across the paper. Both the order results in Appendix~\ref{app:matched} and the onset itself were
re-run on a second, disjoint protected corpus --- 600 excerpts of 50 public-domain books, cut to the
same shape --- with the anchor, the architecture, the settings, the seed and the grid held fixed and
only the protected work changed. No order cell changed sign and the pair ranking survived. For the
onset, three anchors were swept on the full grid with both baselines, with the bands committed
first: the ratios move by at most $0.05$ from their CopyBench twins ($1.053 \to 1.102$,
$0.878 \to 0.895$, $0.926 \to 0.949$), the fine-tokenizer pair is again the only one above $1$ and
its interval again excludes it, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:req} places the onset to within
$0.85$--$1.04$ of the measurement, no worse than on the corpus it was derived on. The seven-pair
measurements themselves, and every intervention in Appendix~\ref{app:seed}, still rest on the
sixteen novels.

\paragraph{More passages do not narrow the onset interval, and that is why several arms are undecided}
The bootstrap interval on onset\,/\,$s(x)$ has a mean width of $0.256$ across the pairs at
$100$ passages each. Quadrupling the passages does not shrink it: measured on the same pair with
the same metric, `Pleias-350M' gives a width of $0.219$ at $n=100$ and $0.215$ at $n=458$, where
independent sampling noise would predict $0.102$. The variance is between \emph{works}, not between
passages drawn from them, so it is bounded below by the sixteen novels and not by how many windows
we cut from each. That has a direct consequence for the interventions in
Section~\ref{sec:onset}: their pre-registered bands are about $0.1$ wide, narrower than an interval
this method can deliver, so several arms land in a committed ``undecided'' region even when the
measured shift is large and in the predicted direction. Where an arm decides nothing we say so, and
the quantity that would fix it is more distinct works, not more windows.

\paragraph{The onset ratio runs over a range, and we can name one cause but not the other}
$s(x)$ spans $1.87\times$ across the nine pairs in tokenizer-free units, still short of the dynamic
range a data collapse normally rests on, and at the crossing budgets only one to four works in a
hundred leak. In units of each pair's own $s(x)$ the bootstrap intervals are $[0.84, 1.22]$,
$[0.79, 0.96]$, $[0.84, 1.07]$, $[0.86, 1.08]$, $[0.80, 1.09]$ for the five pairs at about four
characters per token, $[0.97, 1.48]$ and $[0.96, 1.07]$ for the two at $2.7$, and $[1.14, 1.50]$ and
$[1.02, 1.24]$ for the two KL3M pairs at about two. The first five overlap freely around $0.90$ and
both KL3M intervals exclude $1$, but the eighth pair's is the widest of all and excludes nothing ---
its own control, the ninth, is the narrowest: the \emph{ordering}
is what nine pairs establish, not the level of any one of them.

The KL3M pairs differ from the rest in three ways at once --- the tokenizer, targets twice as long
in tokens, and, for one of them, the weakest memoriser of the first seven --- so pair six was built
to remove one of the three. It shares the tokenizer and the target length with pair five and memorises
thoroughly, $s_r/s_s = 0.09$ against $0.35$, which places it inside the $0.06$--$0.11$ band of the
four; its ratio is nevertheless $1.053$. Memorisation strength is therefore not what puts a pair on
the far side of the vacuity point, though it does modulate the ratio within a family, and in the
direction opposite to Eq.~\eqref{eq:req}. That leaves the tokenizer confounded with everything else the tokenizer determines, and for a fixed
corpus those are not separate variables. Each benchmark item is a prompt prefix followed by the
protected passage, so a tokenizer that cuts the same text more finely changes three things at once:
the adversary's seed, which is a fixed number of \emph{tokens} and therefore buys $7.5$ words on the
KL3M pairs against $13.9$--$15.0$ on the rest; the number of decode steps before the passage is
reached, $445$ against $197$--$230$, with the two open-calm pairs between at $314$; and the target length. All three order the nine pairs
the way the onset ratio is ordered, and the two open-calm pairs sit between the groups on all of
them.

\paragraph{Target length, controlled by truncation}
Truncation separates the target's length from the tokenizer. Decoding the KL3M-520M pair on the
first $276$ tokens of each target matches the four-character pairs' step count with the tokenizer
unchanged and moves $s(x)$ by $1.3\%$; scored on the longest common substring in words, an
absolute count that a shortened target cannot inflate, the ratio is $1.091$ with a bootstrap
interval of $[1.04, 1.24]$, against $1.032$ for the same pair untruncated and $0.866$--$0.893$ for
the four-character pairs. The step count is not the mechanism. Recall is scored against the decoded
target, and each benchmark item is $930$ characters of the novel followed by its continuation, so a
truncated target is still protected text: the unconstrained model reaches $0.696$ near-verbatim
recall on it. The test runs in one direction only, since the four-character pairs cannot be
lengthened to $580$ tokens without longer references than the benchmark provides.

Pair seven removes the next candidate. Vocabulary size is the obvious reading of ``granularity'',
and the two are confounded across the first six pairs: the fine tokenizer has $32{,}768$ types and
the coarse ones $64{,}000$ to $128{,}256$. Phi-3.5-mini breaks that. Its vocabulary is $32{,}011$
types, smaller than KL3M's, and it still cuts these passages at $3.8$ characters per token; across
every anchor we hold, vocabulary size and characters per token are decorrelated enough that
kl3m-002-170m ($32{,}768$ types, $1.98$ characters) and Phi-3.5-mini ($32{,}011$, $3.78$) differ by
$1.91\times$ in granularity at the same vocabulary scale. Its onset ratio is $0.926$, with the
coarse family. Its memoriser is also the most thorough of the nine, $s_r/s_s = 0.003$, so the
memorisation control repeats at its limit. The pair's own bootstrap interval, $[0.80, 1.09]$, does
not by itself exclude the KL3M values; the claim it supports is the negative one, that vocabulary
size is not the variable. Which property does move it is answered in part by
Appendix~\ref{app:seed}: five interventions on the adversary's seed move a pair in both directions,
one of them across its own vacuity threshold, but none reaches the other family's band, so the
context accounts for part of the split and not all of it. One further candidate was pre-registered
and refuted: the per-pair burstiness $k_{\mathrm{crit}}/s(x)$, which is
Proposition~\ref{prop:outrun}'s own quantity, ranks the pairs at $\rho = +0.036$ over the seven it
was committed on and $-0.050$ over nine (Appendix~\ref{app:collapse}). What did resolve part of it
was an anchor in the gap. Across $21$ ungated, openly licensed models the granularity
distribution leaves the region sparse rather than empty --- sixteen at $3.49$--$4.18$ characters per
token, four at $1.22$--$2.36$ --- and exactly one falls between, at $2.71$. Built into a pair and swept
against bands committed beforehand, it lands at $1.027$, between the groups and within $0.02$ of
what the context relation fitted on the other seven predicted; a second anchor on the same tokenizer,
run to control the first one's weak memoriser, lands at $0.993$ on a memoriser five times stronger.
The clusters were a gap in the anchors that exist. What remains unresolved is causation, not the shape: seed words is
$20\times$ characters per token by construction, so granularity and context cannot be separated by
choosing anchors, only by the interventions in Appendix~\ref{app:seed} --- which, applied to all
nine pairs at once, close $61\%$ of the spread and leave $39\%$ standing. That residue is the
honest remainder of this section: the two KL3M pairs are still the top of it at a matched context,
at $0.979$ and $0.939$ against the others' $0.866$--$0.926$, and nothing we can vary in the
evaluation brings them down to it.

We report the proportionality as a unit that locates the onset within about a fifth, not as a law. The Pleias-350M pair was re-measured at $458$
passages after its $100$-passage curve proved too flat through the crossing for a bootstrap to
resolve: a third of resamples never reached the threshold on a grid that stopped at $k = 3.6$, and
extending it to $4.2$ brought that to $0.1\%$ while moving the onset from $3.18$ to $3.27$. The
other six pairs are still $100$ passages each. Building more of them needs a memoriser strong
enough that the \emph{unconstrained} risky model reproduces the passages: a first $350$M attempt
reached $0.71$ near-verbatim recall greedily but only $0.022$ under the sampling the attack uses,
and entered the analysis only after retraining to $0.91$. We admit a pair only if its $k=-1$ recall
exceeds the onset threshold by an order of magnitude.

\paragraph{The no-free-lunch theorem is a converse}
It lower-bounds the budget any policy needs, including the optimal one. Our decoder is causal and
meters with a token bucket, and we do not bound the gap between the two. That gap is large and
measurable --- a factor of $5$ to $32$ between the divergence on the risky model's own rollout and
the divergence paid on a budgeted one --- and closing it is the clearest open problem this paper
leaves.

\paragraph{What selection anchoring cannot do}
The mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:selection} is the paper's constructive claim and carries six
limits, of which the first is not a defect but the guarantee seen from the other side.

\emph{The support ceiling.} $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ says a served string must be one the safe model
would have drawn, so the certificate and the quality ceiling are the same inequality: no $n$ produces
text outside $p_s$'s support, and a deployer whose safe model cannot perform the task at all gains
nothing. That is the exact price of a bound that survives an adversarially chosen scorer, and it is
the structural difference from a metered decoder, which can serve text the anchor would never
produce and pays for the privilege in a currency indexed to the protected work. At the scale we
measure it is not what binds: the oracle arm reaches $u = 0.807$ among the same eight candidates
where the deployed scorers reach $0.52$, so the pool holds far more judged utility than any scorer
we tried extracts, and the binding constraint at $n=8$ is the scorer rather than the anchor.

\emph{Part of any gain could be a degeneracy filter, and is not.} Best-of-$n$ never selects an
empty completion, and the audited anchor emits one on $6.8\%$ of the $500$ prompts --- above the
$5\%$ its own pre-registered entry gate allows, a failure recorded rather than exempted. Recomputed
on the $466$ prompts whose $n=1$ completion is non-empty, the gain is $+0.0536$
$[+0.0075, +0.0966]$ against $+0.0540$ on all $500$; across the four anchors every gain moves by
less than $0.003$ and every interval that excluded zero still does. The empty fractions are
$0.0\%$, $0.2\%$, $3.0\%$ and $6.8\%$, and the anchor with the most of them is not the one with the
largest gain.

\emph{The ceiling binds at the anchor, and we could not measure it within one.} On AlpacaEval's
$805$ instructions the audited anchor gains $+0.067$ $[+0.034, +0.101]$ under one judge and
$+0.031$ $[-0.001, +0.062]$ under the other, and MT-Bench's $80$ prompts resolve nothing in either
direction at a paired half-width of $0.09$. Two pre-registered tests of the support ceiling
followed, on two different axes, and they do not agree.

\emph{Across anchors} it is confirmed. Comma-7B on the same $805$ prompts, the same unconstrained
opponent and the same judges gains $+0.075$ $[+0.042, +0.108]$ and $+0.167$ $[+0.134, +0.201]$,
monotone in $\log n$ in both --- roughly double the audited anchor's, with judge~B's interval taken
off zero, and the committed band asked for an excess of $0.03$
(\texttt{results/onset\_prediction\_alpaca\_comma7b.md}). At the capable anchor the standard
benchmark is not harder than our own prompts: on judge~C its gain slightly exceeds the in-house
$+0.155$, so the pre-registered PROMPT-SET EFFECT reading does not fire either.

\emph{Within one anchor} it is not measurable. Across AlpacaEval's five constituent sources and two
MT-Bench families committed before the split was computed, the gain does not track the anchor's
control level in either judge, and the $-0.79$ and $-0.70$ it does show are not separable from a
within-prompt exchangeability null in which selection does nothing, which already gives
$-0.36 \pm 0.34$ and $-0.34 \pm 0.35$ over $20{,}000$ draws, at $P = 0.09$ and $0.17$. Seven cells cannot resolve this, and
we do not claim they do. The consistent reading is that the ceiling is a property of the anchor's
overall capability rather than of the domain it is asked about, which is also the direction of
Spearman $+1.000$ between anchor-alone level and gain over the four anchors --- four points, and
registered as descriptive.

\emph{Overoptimisation.} As $n$ grows the argmax is optimised harder against the scorer's own errors,
which for reward models is known to turn the gain over rather than saturate it
\citep{gao2023scaling}. Our extraction arm reaches $n=64$ and our judged arm, as committed, reaches
$n=8$; whether judged utility keeps rising to $n=64$ is measured in
\texttt{results/onset\_prediction\_selection\_scaling.md}, pre-registered with bands that partition
the line into a growing, a saturating and an overoptimising reading. Nothing in
Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} prevents the third.

\emph{The scorer is a second model.} It cannot break the certificate --- that is the content of the
proposition --- but it can degrade utility arbitrarily, and a compromised or badly calibrated scorer
turns the mechanism into an expensive way to serve the worst of $n$ anchor samples. The failure is
visible in exactly the place a deployer would look, which is the served utility, and not in the
certificate, which is why the two must be monitored separately. The compute is $n$ draws and $n$
scoring passes against the two forward passes per token a metered decoder makes; neither dominates in
general, and at the sizes used here the two are within a small factor.

\emph{The bound is relative to $p_s$.} $\Pr_q[E] \le n \Pr_{p_s}[E]$ is a statement about the safe
model's own probability of emitting the work, so a safe model that has itself seen the work carries
its contamination straight through, multiplied by $n$. This is inherited from near-access-freeness
and is not specific to selection, but it is worth restating where the numbers are small enough to
look reassuring: the $0.0000$ of Section~\ref{sec:experiments} is $n$ times a base rate that is zero
\emph{because the anchor never saw these novels}, and it would not be zero for an anchor that had.

\paragraph{Two judges, one workload}
Utility is scored by instruction-tuned models on the benchmark's own prompt classes rather than
deployment traffic. Two judges at $600$ comparisons per arm agree on the sign everywhere and on
neither endpoint's magnitude: at $k=1$ one reads $-3.66\sigma$ against the anchor and the other
$+0.11\sigma$, at $k=10$ $-6.97\sigma$ against $-3.29\sigma$. The null arm calibrates that gap
rather than leaving it to argument --- served the \emph{unconstrained} risky model, the first judge
separates it from the anchor at $-6.09\sigma$ and the second at $-2.49\sigma$, so the second is
the coarser instrument and its smaller separations are partly its own resolution. That calibration
is itself noisy: the same $500$ comparisons judged in an earlier run gave $-5.47\sigma$ and
$-1.49\sigma$, so about a $\sigma$ of run-to-run drift is the floor beneath which no separation
reported here should be read. What survives
both is the pair of endpoints: neither separates the decoder from the anchor at $k=0.5$, and both
do at $k\ge10$. The order comparison in Table~\ref{tab:orders} is at matched budget and its judged
arms carry $n=150$; Appendix~\ref{app:matched} repeats it at matched \emph{utility} without a
judge on twelve pairs in seven families, where the ranking is non-monotone in $\alpha$, reverses
on nine of them, and is predicted by nothing measured.

\paragraph{The two leakage measurements cannot be put on one axis, and we tried}
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports the metered decoder's extraction onset and selection
anchoring's zero recall in two places with two pipelines, and a reader of the released CSVs will
notice that the two pipelines disagree about the one quantity they share. The same memorising
model, the same $100$ passages, the same $20$-token seeds and the same temperature read $0.4921$ in
\texttt{composition\_8b\_kl.csv} and $0.3925$ in \texttt{selection\_extraction.csv}. We
pre-registered a gate to decide whether a joint table could honestly be built
(\texttt{results/onset\_prediction\_leakage\_headtohead.md}): re-run both arms at one generation
length, and require their two memoriser-alone rows to agree within $0.02$.

They do not. At a matched $300$ tokens the two read $0.4921$ and $0.4428$, a residual of $0.0493$,
so \textbf{no joint table is built} and no ratio is quoted across the two files. What the arm does
establish is that neither pipeline is wrong. Generation length accounts for half the original
discrepancy exactly as predicted --- lengthening the selection arm from $200$ to $300$ tokens moved
its control from $0.3925$ to $0.4428$, because at $200$ tokens against a full-length reference
near-verbatim recall is capped near $0.77$ by construction --- and the residual has two candidate
causes the arm cannot separate: \texttt{generate} consumes the sampler's randomness per step, so
changing the token budget changes the draw as well as the length, and the metered arm decodes each
passage's own target length while the selection arm decodes a flat $300$. Both measure a sampled
memoriser correctly and they sample different trajectories.

The consequence for the paper is narrow and we prefer to state it than to have it inferred: every
leakage number here is read against a control produced by \emph{its own} pipeline, the two
mechanisms' leakage is never divided one by the other, and the comparison this paper makes between
them is about what they \emph{spend}, which is measured on one axis, and not about what they leak,
which is not.
