\section{Limitations in full}\label{app:limitations}

\paragraph{One protected corpus.}
Every extraction measurement runs on sixteen English genre novels from CopyBench and BookMIA, plus the
four French and German works of the multilingual arm. The margin's sign test, $p = 3.1\times10^{-5}$, is
the smallest value sixteen paired items can produce: it says the trend holds for every novel we have, not
that it was independently replicated. Non-literal reproduction and other genres are untested. On two
further corpora the level of the onset survives, every onset within about $1.3\times$ of $s(x)$, and the
ordering of pairs does not (Appendix~\ref{app:collapse}), so an onset ratio is reportable to its order of
magnitude and no more finely. Varying only a memoriser's fine-tuning length moves one pair's ratio from
$0.8756$ to $1.3477$, $1.64\times$ the nine-pair spread; re-seeding moves it by $0.033$, $0.066$ and $0.080$
where the fine-tune converged and by $0.260$ over three seeds and $0.347$ over four where it did not; and
neither intervention that would make convergence a cause could be built (a loss floor of $0.0222$ against
a $0.02$ stop-loss; a memoriser that reproduces nothing after a $17\%$ change in learning rate). The
nine-pair table's memorisers are matched on none of training length (a factor of $5.1$ in sampled $k=-1$
recall), batch size, accumulation, stopping rule or convergence. The seed-word gradient $\rho = -0.958$ keeps
its sign in every re-draw under the largest re-seed noise measured and $p<0.05$ in $92.3\%$ of them, at a
median of $-0.849$: a rank ordering, not a coefficient.

\paragraph{More passages do not narrow the onset interval.}
The bootstrap interval on onset\,/\,$s(x)$ has a mean width of $0.285$ across the pairs at $100$ passages
each; on one pair under one metric it is $0.219$ at $n=100$ and $0.215$ at $n=458$, where independent
sampling noise predicts $0.102$. The variance is between works, not windows, so several registered bands
about $0.1$ wide land undecided even where the shift is large and in the predicted direction. More distinct
works would fix it; more windows will not.

\paragraph{The onset's range has one cause we can name and one we cannot.}
$s(x)$ spans $1.87\times$ across the nine pairs, short of the range a data collapse normally rests on. Both
KL3M pairs exclude $1$ and differ from the rest in tokenizer, target length in tokens and, for one, memoriser
strength; a pair built to remove the last memorises thoroughly ($s_r/s_s = 0.09$ against $0.35$) and still
reads $1.053$. The tokenizer remains confounded with what it moves at once: the adversary's seed ($7.5$
words against $13.9$--$15.0$), the decode steps before the passage ($445$ against $197$--$230$) and the
target length.

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl} is a converse.}
It lower-bounds the budget any policy needs; we do not bound the gap between that optimum and a causal
token-bucket decoder, which is a factor of $5$ to $32$ between the divergence on the risky model's own
rollout and the divergence paid on a budgeted one.

\paragraph{What selection anchoring cannot do.}
\emph{The support ceiling.} $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ is also a quality ceiling: no $n$ produces text outside the
anchor's support. At $n=8$ on the judged workload it does not bind: the oracle among the same eight
candidates reaches $u = 0.807$ where the deployed scorers reach $0.52$. Across anchors it is confirmed on
AlpacaEval: the audited anchor gains $+0.074$ $[+0.042, +0.108]$ and $+0.020$ $[-0.011, +0.052]$ under two
judges, Comma-7B $+0.075$ $[+0.044, +0.106]$ and $+0.152$ $[+0.119, +0.185]$; MT-Bench's $80$ prompts
resolve nothing at a paired half-width of $0.10$. Within one anchor, across AlpacaEval's five sources and
two MT-Bench families, the correlations of $-0.79$ and $-0.70$ between gain and control level are not
separable from a null in which selection does nothing ($-0.36 \pm 0.34$ and $-0.34 \pm 0.35$, $P = 0.09$
and $0.17$).

\emph{Empty drafts.} The audited anchor's first draw is empty on $14.6\%$ of prompts in its $n=8$ arm and
$12.8\%$ in its $n=64$ arm, against $0.0\%$ at both Pleias anchors, $0.2\%$ at KL3M-1.7B, $18.4\%$ at
Comma-7B and $31.6\%$ at Comma-7B-1T, all three Comma anchors above the $5\%$ the admission criterion
allows, recorded rather than exempted. On the $427$ prompts whose $n=1$ draw is non-empty the audited
anchor's $n=8$ gain is $+0.079$ $[+0.028, +0.128]$ against $+0.073$ on all $500$, and every interval that
excluded zero still does but one, Comma-1T's under judge~B, whose lower end was $+0.001$.

\emph{Overoptimisation.} To $n=512$ no judged ladder turns over, against registered bands that allowed it
to; under a task metric the $7$B reward turns over on TriviaQA and CoTaEval (Table~\ref{tab:scorerladder}),
and nothing in Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} prevents it on a judged workload at larger $n$.

\emph{The scorer is a second model and a stated requirement.} It cannot break the certificate but can
degrade utility arbitrarily, so served utility and the certificate must be monitored separately. Every
reward turn-over here is measured at \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct} and none survives a larger scorer, so on
knowledge-intensive tasks selection under a pointwise reward carries a scorer-size requirement, and a
deployer who ignores it can be worse off at every $n$ than serving the anchor once. On the judged workload
the two larger scorers lift nothing ($-0.0285$ and $-0.0085$ against the $7.6$B rung), and no latency arm
ran above $7$B.

\emph{The bound is relative to $p_s$.} A safe model that has seen the work carries its contamination
through, multiplied by $n$ (Table~\ref{tab:contam}); the $0.0000$ of Section~\ref{sec:experiments} is $n$
times a base rate that is zero because the anchor never saw these novels.

\paragraph{The metered decoder as its authors run it.}
\citet{he2026anchored} pair base risky models (\texttt{Llama-3.1-70B}, \texttt{Qwen2.5-72B},
\texttt{Llama-4-Scout}) with openly licensed anchors, decode books at temperature $0.7$ with a repetition
penalty of $1.1$, fuse mismatched vocabularies through AnchoredByte, and score utility by Prometheus-2
fluency and FActScore precision. We ran their token-level pair at temperature $1.0$ with no penalty, the
released logs' $8$B-Instruct without its chat template (and, separately, with it), AnchoredByte only at
their recommended pair, and their two metrics with open models in place of \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini}. None of
this changes a certificate or a leakage number; each can change a judged one. No human rated anything and no
lawyer assessed any output.

\paragraph{Two judges, one workload.}
Two judges at $600$ comparisons per arm agree on the sign everywhere and on neither endpoint's magnitude:
at $k=1$ one reads $-3.66\sigma$ against the anchor and the other $+0.11\sigma$, at $k=10$ $-6.97\sigma$
against $-3.29\sigma$. Served the unconstrained risky model, the first separates it at $-6.09\sigma$ and the
second at $-2.49\sigma$, and an earlier run of the same $500$ comparisons gave $-5.47\sigma$ and
$-1.49\sigma$, so about a $\sigma$ of run-to-run drift is the floor beneath which no separation here should
be read.

\paragraph{The two leakage measurements are not on one axis.}
The meter's onset and selection's zero come from two pipelines whose memoriser-alone rows disagree ($0.4921$
against $0.3925$; at a matched $300$ tokens $0.4921$ against $0.4428$, a residual of $0.0493$ against a
registered gate of $0.02$). The residual has two causes the arm cannot separate: changing the token budget changes the draw as well as the length, and the meter decodes each passage's own target length where selection decodes a flat $300$. No joint table is built, every leakage number is read against a control from
its own pipeline, and the comparison between the mechanisms is about what they spend, not what they leak.
