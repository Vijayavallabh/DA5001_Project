\section{An Audit Protocol That Can Fail}\label{sec:protocol}

Each check below can be run by a deployer, a rights-holder, or a third party against the decoder's logs, and each names the observation that would falsify the claim it tests. C1--C5 test the certificate as stated; C7--C10 test the three changes that would make it say more; C11 and C12 test what the certificate and its price are relative to. The protocol avoids the natural alternative, a concentration bound on sampled spends, because Eq.~\eqref{eq:chain} makes that bound true by construction.

\emph{Accounting} (C1). Recompute $a_t$ from the served and anchor distributions and check $a_t \le k_t + \varepsilon$ at every step and $Z \le \max\{0,B\} + \varepsilon$ at the end, with $\varepsilon = 10^{-3}$ absorbing the solver's tolerance. One violation falsifies the accounting; utilisation concentrated near zero shows the budget never bound. \emph{Regime} (C2). Report the fraction of steps with $\theta_t = 0$, $0 < \theta_t < 1$, and $\theta_t = 1$, the fraction of outputs identical to the unconstrained model on the same seed, and the leading tokens the anchor writes, $\lfloor \delta_{\mathrm{init}}(x)/k \rfloor$. \emph{Certificate strength} (C3). For each protected passage compute $S(x)$ with one forward pass of the anchor and report $\mathrm{cap}(K,S)$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:cap} and how often it equals one; a rights-holder can run this without the risky model. \emph{Event-level leakage} (C4). Report the distribution of $L(y)$, the fraction of trajectories with $L(y) > K$, and the largest single-step ratio, using the empirical-Bernstein confidence sequence of Waudby-Smith and Ramdas~\cite{waudbysmith2024estimating} for rates, so the auditor may stop as soon as the interval settles the claim.

\emph{Composition} (C5). Run the attacks of Section~\ref{sec:attacks} with query access only and report reconstruction against $k$ and the number of queries, with every query's spend inside its own budget. Reconstruction is scored by near-verbatim recall, the fraction of the target's words covered by in-order matches of at least twenty words~\cite{ahmed2026extracting}, and by the longest exactly reproduced span. Recovering a substantial part of a passage under compliant queries is the failure. \emph{Natural memorisation} (C7). Repeat C3 and C5 with the deployer's own risky model on passages it memorised in pre-training, following Cooper et al.~\cite{cooper2025extracting}: raw passage seeds with no instruction, greedy decoding from the risky model alone as the reference, and the deployment's own decoding settings. Reconstruction at the deployed $k$ that approaches the risky model's own is the failure. \emph{Certificate form} (C8, C9). Run the decoder with the realised log-ratio as the charged quantity and check $L(y) \le \max\{0,B\}$ per trajectory (Proposition~\ref{prop:pathwise}); for the KL form, report the Freedman bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:conc} next to the empirical exceedance, so the concentration assumption of Lemma~2.3 of Vyas et al.\ is stated and tested. \emph{Accounting across queries} (C10). Replay the per-query logs against a per-user budget and report reconstruction and the fraction of users refused as functions of that budget.

Two checks concern what the rest are relative to. \emph{Anchor dependence} (C11). Recompute $S(x)$ under a second, independently trained permissive anchor. Because the two anchors need not share a tokenizer, compare the total surprisal of a passage in nats rather than a per-token rate. A vacuity fraction that moves with the anchor is a fact about the reference model, not about the budget. \emph{Utility} (C12). Price every variant on ordinary prompts with a measure external to the models under test, against the unconstrained model on the same prompts, and against the same comparison performed between two independent samples of the unconstrained model itself. Without that null a win rate cannot be read, because two draws from one decoder already disagree.

Every number traces to one summary table that one script generates from the logs, and the baselines, checks and raw logs are released. Deployers should publish, next to $K$, the C2 activity statistics and the C3 vacuity fraction on a reference set of protected passages, which tell a rights-holder whether the certificate applies to their work at the deployed $k$; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} extends this to a certificate card.
