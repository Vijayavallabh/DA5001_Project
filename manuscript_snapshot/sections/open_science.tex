\section*{Open Science}
All code, prompt sets, per-trajectory and per-query logs, and the summary tables behind every number in this paper are released under a permissive licence in an anonymised artifact, linked from the submission form and kept unchanged for the duration of the review; the archived record follows at camera-ready. The artifact contains the audited decoder with the pathwise constraint and the bank cap as options; the two experiment drivers; the reanalysis, certificate-strength, regime-sweep, composition-attack, natural-memorisation, warped-anchor, latent-leakage, budget-path, prefix-debt, odometer, bank-cap, concentration, pathwise-price, extraction-cost, anchor-robustness, length-scaling, separation, utility and second-mechanism scripts, each of which regenerates one summary table from the logs; the CopyBench, general-knowledge, FactScore, and creative-writing prompt sets with the exact sampling caps and seeds; the recipe and training script for the memorising risky model, whose merged weights are not redistributed because they reproduce copyrighted passages, with a SHA-256 manifest so that a re-run can be checked against ours; the $2.4$\,GB of raw trajectory logs from the released runs~\cite{vijayavallabh2026audit}; and the per-query logs of every attack in this paper. Every figure is produced by one command from the CSV files, and a setup script verifies the environment and runs the test suite before any experiment. The anchor, the second anchor used for the robustness check, the 8B risky model and the 70B risky model are public checkpoints identified by repository name and revision in the artifact documentation. The two shard-trained models behind the second-mechanism audit are built by the released recipe with a shard flag; like the memorising model their merged weights are withheld and their recipe is not.

\section*{LLM usage considerations}
Large language models are the object of study. The risky models (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-70B base~\cite{grattafiori2024llama3}), the two anchors (TinyComma-1.8B and Comma~v0.1-7B), the judge used for the utility evaluation (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct~\cite{qwen2025qwen25}), and the memorising models derived from the 8B model by low-rank fine-tuning are all built on openly released weights, and their identities, revisions, decoding settings, and seeds are reported, so no result depends on a closed model. LLMs were used for editorial purposes in this manuscript, and all outputs were inspected by the authors to ensure accuracy and originality. An LLM-based coding assistant was also used, under the authors' direction, to write and refactor the analysis scripts and to draft prose. The assistant is a closed model, but everything it produced is either released code, whose behaviour the test suite and the manifest pin down, or text that the authors revised, so reproducibility does not depend on it. Every quantitative claim was produced by the released scripts, every reference was checked against the publisher's or arXiv's metadata, and no experimental result, citation, or number was accepted from the assistant without such verification. The experiments used A100 80\,GB GPUs: the single-GPU runs total about $48$ GPU-hours across the small-budget sweeps, the composition attacks, the pathwise and concentration sweeps, the ablations, the anchor and length-scaling passes, the utility judging and the second-mechanism audit, and the 70B runs about $9$ GPU-hours on two GPUs sharded in bf16, for $56.5$ GPU-hours in all, three low-rank fine-tunes of $75$, $15$ and $15$ minutes included. The per-job wall times behind these figures are released with the artifact. Query volume was kept small by reanalysing the $26{,}999$ previously released trajectories~\cite{vijayavallabh2026audit} rather than regenerating them, by running each sweep on $900$ prompts with three trajectories each, and by attacking $100$ passages with the fine-tuned model, $58$ with the 70B model and $60$ with the second mechanism, and by scoring the utility evaluation on generations the sweeps had already produced rather than decoding again.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
The protected passages are the book split of CopyBench, a public benchmark on which the mechanism's authors also evaluate, and the audit involves no human subjects or personal data. The memorising risky model exists only to test the defence; its merged weights are not released, because they reproduce copyrighted text on request, while the fine-tuning recipe is, because the audit cannot be reproduced without it and it adds nothing beyond ordinary supervised fine-tuning on a public dataset. The 70B experiments extract passages that Cooper et al.\ already showed the public checkpoint to have memorised, and the per-query logs release only what the benchmark itself publishes. Every attack query was issued to our own copy of the decoder; no deployed product was queried. In the terms of the Menlo report~\cite{dittrich2012menlo}, the beneficiaries are rights-holders and deployers who would otherwise read the certificate as a guarantee it does not give, and the residual risk, a recipe for a model that reproduces 608 already-public benchmark passages, is small against that benefit. Nothing in the audit contradicts the mechanism's theorem, the repairs of Section~\ref{sec:certificates} are offered as additions to it, and the findings and code will be shared with the mechanism's authors.
