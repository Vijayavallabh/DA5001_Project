\subsection{The deployed instance, and every escape it has left}\label{sec:orders}

\input{sections/onset}



Four escapes remain, all measured in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}: sharpen the charge, lower the cap,
schedule the spend better, or grant the budget up front and spend it sparsely --- the one causal policy
Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse} positively permits. \citet{he2026anchored} ablate the first and the
last themselves, as their $\infty$-R\'enyi decoder and their Global variant; we price both against
selection, each with a refuting band fixed in advance. \textbf{The $80\times$ of sharpening is real
and a rights-holder is right to want it}, but then the constraint binds at $99.6\%$ of ordinary
steps against $0.4\%$; a lower cap buys nothing two judges separate from the safe model; and the
budget granted up front, the fair test of whether a \emph{causal} rule can concentrate, reaches
$+0.0545$ $[+0.0315, +0.0775]$ at $64$ nats, about half of selection's gain in the same pass at
$\log 64 = 4.16$: an empirical shortfall for the best construction we could build, not a proof that
none matches. What the four share is the currency they buy with: likelihood under $p_r$. As a criterion for
choosing among the anchor's own draws it predicts judged quality no better than the answer's length
does (Appendix~\ref{app:currency}); as a direction to tilt generation it buys the risky model's
quality only by becoming the risky model (Figure~\ref{fig:judgefree}c), which is the vacuous horn.
