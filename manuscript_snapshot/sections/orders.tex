\section{The deployed instance, and the three repairs it has left}\label{sec:orders}

Two questions follow for the deployed instance, anchored decoding \citep{he2026anchored}. Does the
vacuity threshold correspond to anything a rights-holder would recognise? And can its three
remaining degrees of freedom --- charge, cap, schedule --- recover what
Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse} says they cannot? All four answers were pre-registered, and the
interesting part of the three failures is \emph{where} each fails.

\paragraph{Repair 1: sharpen the accounting}
Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} says a stronger divergence cannot move the threshold, so the
question is what it does underneath. Four orders ran the same attack at the authors' own budget
$k=3$, where all four publish $K = 3T$ and the certificate is vacuous for $100\%$ of the $758$
protected passages and $98.7\%$ of $9{,}870$ across a hundred books --- indistinguishable to a
rights-holder (Table~\ref{tab:orders}) --- while underneath they span two orders of magnitude in
what an adversary recovers, $0.0965$ at $\alpha=1$ against $0.0012$ at $\alpha=8$, and at
\emph{matched utility} run from $10^{6}$ times more dangerous to $10^{7}$ times safer
(Appendices~\ref{app:bookmia},~\ref{app:matched}).

\paragraph{Repair 2: lower the budget}
This is a walk onto the trivial horn, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:nfl} prices the walk: the decoder spends
$79$ to $171$ nats to buy gains an optimal policy could have bought for $0.0003$ to $0.077$, a ratio
that never falls below $2200$; bluntness \emph{caps} the rate function, so winning \emph{every}
comparison would cost an optimal policy $1.30$ nats and the decoder spends $132$ times that to win a
third of them. The certified and the useful regions then fail to meet. Under two
instruction-tuned judges outside the audit, $600$ comparisons per budget, neither separates the
decoder from its own safe model at $k=0.5$ ($-1.03\sigma$, $+1.52\sigma$), where all $758$ protected
passages are still covered; both do only at $k \ge 10$ ($-6.97\sigma$, $-3.29\sigma$), where none is
(Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).

\paragraph{Repair 3: spend the budget better}
Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse} bounds this one before it is run: a greedy decoder is not short of
allowance but of somewhere useful to put it. Optimal offline reallocation of its own realised spend
is a ceiling no causal scheduler can beat \citep{tomasi2026primaldual}, and buys $3$ to $13\%$ more
utility at $k \le 1$ and under $1.4\%$ at $k=3$ (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).

\paragraph{What the three have in common}
The budget is not spent inefficiently on utility; it is spent \emph{efficiently on a target that is
not utility}. A metered decoder exists to move the served distribution toward $p_r$, so its budget
buys likelihood under $p_r$ --- a currency that, measured on these candidates, predicts judged
quality worse than the answer's length does (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). A wrong currency on the
wrong axis is not repaired by a better meter.
