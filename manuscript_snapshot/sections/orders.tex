\section{The published budget determines neither the protection nor the price}\label{sec:orders}

Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} says every order of the charge becomes vacuous at the same budget,
so a deployer who publishes $k$ publishes the same certificate whichever order the decoder uses. We
built the family --- $\alpha \to 1$ is the audited decoder, $\alpha \to \infty$ the pathwise one,
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:renyi-path} is closed form on the geodesic, so one bisection serves all of them
--- and ran the same attack and workload through four members at the budget the mechanism's authors
use. Figure~\ref{fig:orders} in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} shows what the certificate says across the family;
Table~\ref{tab:orders} shows what the decoders do.

Table~\ref{tab:orders} is the point of this paper in one row group. Holding the published budget
fixed, and the certificate with it --- vacuous throughout --- the four decoders span two orders of
magnitude in what a compliant adversary recovers, $0.097$ of a passage down to $0.001$, and more
than two in what they cost, from touching $0.4\%$ of ordinary decode steps to $90.1\%$. A number
that determines neither the protection nor the price is not a summary of the mechanism but of one
accounting convention inside it.

The family is cheap at one end and not in the middle. Moving from $\alpha=1$ to $\alpha=2$ nearly
halves oracle-window recall, $0.097$ to $0.054$, for intervention rising from $0.4\%$ to $2.4\%$ of
steps and no measurable loss of diversity --- distinct-3 moves from $0.9910$ to $0.9924$, the wrong
way for a cost. One more step does not continue that trade: at $\alpha=4$ oracle recall falls to
$0.004$, but the decoder serves the risky model unchanged at $8.7\%$ of steps instead of $91.4\%$
and intervenes at $83.0\%$, already most of the way to $\alpha=8$. The order is a sharp knob, not a
dial, and a deployer choosing $\alpha$ on the published budget alone cannot tell which system they
have bought.

\paragraph{Judged utility, and the resolution of the instrument}
Activity counts are not utility, so we also score the arms with a judge outside the audit
(Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, order randomised, $150$ pairs per order arm). A win rate is meaningless
without a null, and there are two: the unconstrained model judged against \emph{itself} at a
different seed loses $46.4\%$ of the time, and the anchor served alone loses $62.7\%$. Every arm is
a position between them.

Against those: $\alpha=1$ sits $1.92\sigma$ from the anchor, $\alpha=4$ $1.49\sigma$ and $\alpha=2$
$1.05\sigma$ --- separations of $0.35\sigma$ and $0.70\sigma$ from $\alpha=1$, which $150$ pairs
cannot resolve, and a second judge reorders them completely. \emph{Activity is not price either.}
The $\alpha=4$ decoder overrides the risky model at $90\%$ of decode steps and leaks $24\times$ less
than $\alpha=1$ in oracle windows ($0.004$ against $0.097$), and neither judge can tell it from
$\alpha=1$: the published budget determines neither the protection nor the price, and neither does
the intervention rate. Only at $\alpha=8$ does the first judge find a cost, and there it is total
--- it loses $62.7\%$, the anchor's own rate to the decimal ($0.00\sigma$), with the anchor's
leakage. Turning the order up far enough converges on the safe model, which was always available
without a mechanism, at matched budget rather than matched utility
(Appendix~\ref{app:collapse}).
