\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Language models reproduce text they were trained on, and some of that text is copyrighted. Near-access-freeness (NAF)~\cite{vyas2023provable} offers a mechanism-level remedy: a model whose output distribution stays within a divergence budget of a \emph{safe} model that never saw a protected work cannot, the argument goes, be much more likely than the safe model to reproduce it. Anchored Decoding~\cite{he2026anchored} is the first implementation of this idea at inference time for an arbitrary risky model. It fuses the risky model with a permissively trained anchor token by token, charges each step's KL divergence against a banked allowance, and certifies a sequence-level budget of $K = k\,T_{\max}$ nats per prompt. The certificate holds for any risky model and comes with a proof.

This paper asks what the certificate is worth to the party it is meant to protect. A rights-holder cares about an event, the reproduction of a specific passage, and about a system that answers many prompts; the certificate bounds an expectation, for one prompt, in KL divergence. We audit the distance between the two in the way the differential-privacy community audits a claimed $\varepsilon$~\cite{jagielski2020auditing,nasr2023tight,steinke2023privacy}: by constructing measurements that could contradict it. Every audit step has a failure mode, and we report which ones fired.

The accounting is right. On all 26{,}999 trajectories of the released runs~\cite{vijayavallabh2026audit}, and on more than $100{,}000$ trajectories and attack queries of our own, the per-step and per-trajectory invariants hold with a largest overshoot of zero (Section~\ref{sec:accounting}). What the guarantee means at the budgets of the released runs is another matter. At $k=3$ and $k=5$ the constraint is active on $0.2\%$ and $0.1\%$ of decode steps on the book prompts and $57\%$ of generations are identical between the two budgets (Section~\ref{sec:regime}); the bound the certificate implies on the probability of reproducing a passage is vacuous for all 758 CopyBench passages at $k \ge 3$ and becomes informative only at $k \le 0.5$, where the anchor writes the first tokens of every answer (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}); and because the certificate bounds an expectation, at $k=1$ the realised log-likelihood ratio of an output exceeds $K$ for $8\%$ of trajectories, with single tokens served at $e^{26}$ times their anchor probability (Section~\ref{sec:tails}). Neither finding is an artefact of the particular anchor or of short passages. A second permissive anchor four times the size assigns the same passages fewer total nats, so it is vacuous \emph{more} often at $k=1$; and while a longer work does raise $S(x)$ above $K$, the certificate an adversary actually faces covers one window at a time and is vacuous at every work length for $k \ge 5$.

Whether any of this is exploitable depends on the risky model, so we audit two that memorised the passages: the 8B instruct model of the released logs fine-tuned until it reproduces them, the choice the guarantee leaves to the adversary, and Llama~3.1~70B base, the risky model the mechanism's authors evaluate, which memorised \emph{Harry Potter} and \emph{1984} in pre-training~\cite{cooper2025extracting}. Both give the same answer (Sections~\ref{sec:attack-results} and~\ref{sec:natural}): at $k \le 3$ the budget binds and a query recovers a few percent of a passage, although the certificate for that passage is vacuous, and by $k=20$, a budget the authors also evaluate, the decoder is the identity, where compliant windowed queries in the spirit of Cohen's composition theorem~\cite{cohen2025blameless} recover $86\%$ of a passage from the fine-tuned model and the 70B model's own extraction rate. In between, most of the protection is the prefix debt, a charge on the prompt that the guarantee does not analyse: switched off, it lets a single query recover $40\%$ of a passage at $k=5$ instead of $9\%$, and at $k=20$ it makes no difference at all, so the component the theorem omits is decisive on exactly the interval where the mechanism is usable (Section~\ref{sec:prefixdebt}).

Each gap admits a repair inside the decoder, and Section~\ref{sec:certificates} implements and prices three. Charging the realised log-ratio instead of the KL divergence yields the $\Delta_{\max}$ form of NAF, which bounds every event, at the cost of intervening on three times as many steps at $k=1$ and on almost none at $k \ge 5$. A Freedman bound on the decoder's own logged variances turns the KL form into a concentration certificate, but not at the budgets that need one, since it says nothing about the outputs that already exceed $K$. And a per-user budget bounds composition without separating the parties: legitimate traffic and reconstruction are metered in the same nats, so the budget that holds an adversary to a tenth of a work admits a reader less than one ordinary answer (Proposition~\ref{prop:sep}). Prices throughout are measured by a judge outside the audit rather than by the risky model's own log-loss, which cannot report on a distribution it moved. Since the decoding settings and the anchor's exposure to famous text also change what a published $K$ means, we propose a certificate card that records them.

\paragraph{Contributions}
The audit runs twelve checks, each with a stated failure mode and each released with the code, logs, and prompt sets that produced it (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}, C6). Grouped by what they establish: the implementation is exact, with no invariant failure in $5.21$ million released decode steps or in any run of our own, and at the audited budgets it is nearly inactive, measured against the risky model alone and the anchor alone (C1, C2); the certificate it publishes is uninformative wherever the mechanism is usable, whether read as a per-passage cap on reproduction or as the tail of the realised log-ratio under anytime-valid confidence sequences, and that verdict survives a second anchor, a longer work, and the deployer's decoding settings (C3, C4, C11); the protection that does exist comes from the prefix debt rather than from the certified budget, under adaptive attacks with a memorising 8B model and with the authors' own Llama~3.1~70B on books it memorised in pre-training, including the first empirical instantiation of the composition theorem against an inference-time NAF mechanism (C5, C7); and three repairs inside the decoder are proved and priced by a judge outside the audit, namely the $\Delta_{\max}$ form under the same banking rule, a concentration certificate from quantities the unchanged decoder already logs, and per-user accounting with Proposition~\ref{prop:sep} (C8--C10, C12).
