\section{What the evaluation hands the adversary}\label{app:seed}

\paragraph{Three quantities move together, and none of them is the tokenizer}
Each benchmark item is a prompt prefix followed by its continuation, both from the same novel, and
the attack seeds the adversary with \texttt{--seed-tokens 20}: a fixed number of \emph{tokens}. For one
fixed piece of text a finer tokenizer therefore changes three things at once --- the words the seed
buys, the decode steps before the continuation is reached, and the target's length in tokens --- and
all three order the nine pairs the way the onset ratio is ordered:

these; only an intervention can.

\paragraph{Nine interventions, five on the seed and four on the anchor}
Every arm was committed before it ran, with the band that would refute it
(\texttt{results/onset\_prediction\_seed.md} and
\texttt{results/onset\_prediction\_temperature.md}). The seed arms change
\texttt{--seed-tokens} alone: the tokenizer, the anchor, the memoriser, the corpus, the passages,
the budget grid and the metric are held fixed, and $s(x)$ is recomputed at the matching seed because
the surprisal rate is measured over the target given the seed. The temperature arms change the warp
applied to both logit vectors before the KL solve, which moves $s(x)$ and holds the seed fixed.

The primary metric is the longest common sub\emph{string} in words at a threshold of four --- a
contiguous run, the CopyBench exact-match metric, and an absolute
count that a changed target length cannot inflate; near-verbatim recall at $0.01$ is reported
alongside. Intervals are bootstrapped over passages. The last column is the fraction of bootstrap
resamples in which the mean curve never crosses the threshold; it is reported for every arm because
it, and not the interval, is what catches a grid whose top end is a ceiling.

\paragraph{What the seed arms do and do not establish}
Arm B moves a pair from one family's band across the vacuity threshold into the other's, and its
interval is disjoint from its control's; the pre-registered band for ``the seed is the mechanism''
was $\ge 1.00$ and the measurement is $1.004$. Arm A moves the converse pair in the converse
direction, from $1.032$ to $0.939$, and Arm A2 replicates that direction on a second, larger anchor
of the same family, $1.155$ to $0.979$. Both of the latter land in ranges the pre-registration had
already declared undecided between two accounts --- seed matching, which predicted $0.85$--$0.95$
for Arm A, and a token-bucket account on which the seed acts by choosing which token the adversary
must generate first --- so we report them as undecided rather than assigning them. Neither direction
reaches the other family's band, so the adversary's context is not the whole of the difference
between the families.

The dose-response, swept across a factor of seven on KL3M-520M, is not monotone: $1.163$ at $4.0$
words, $1.032$ at $7.5$, $0.939$ at $14.8$ and back to $1.056$ at $29.6$. The turn was predicted by
neither registered account. Two things push in its direction and neither was designed in: at $80$
seed tokens the remaining target is $12\%$ shorter than the control's ($520$ against $590$ tokens),
which on an absolute word count makes the threshold harder to reach; and the seed-$10$ arm's
unconstrained recall is $0.227$ against the control's $0.519$, which is why that arm was
pre-committed to be reported with its baseline attached and excluded from any fit. We claim the
effect over the range an evaluation actually varies the seed, $4$ to $14$ words, and not beyond it.

Two checks that the seed arms measure what they claim. In four of the five the unconstrained model
reproduces its target exactly as well as its control does --- $k=-1$ recall $0.520$, $0.546$,
$0.894$ and $0.386$ against $0.519$, $0.519$, $0.909$ and $0.411$ --- so the intervention changes
what the adversary is given and not how well the memoriser knows the text; the exception is the
seed-$10$ arm noted above, at $0.227$, which is excluded from any fit for exactly that reason. The
anchor alone leaks nothing in any of the runs. And
the residual difference in target length points the wrong way to explain Arm B: its target is
$3.7\%$ longer than its control's, which on an absolute word count can only make the threshold
easier to reach and the onset \emph{lower}, while the measured onset rose by $17.7\%$.

\paragraph{Grid ceilings, and why the no-crossing fraction is reported}
Two arms first came back with an implausibly tight interval and a large no-crossing fraction: the
seed-$80$ arm at $2.1\%$ and Arm A2 at $38.0\%$, against $0.0$--$0.9\%$ among the seed arms. Both had
the same cause. The mean $\texttt{lcs\_word}$ curve rose past four and then flattened just above it
at the top of the grid --- Arm A2 holds at $4.7$--$5.0$ from $k = 2.2$ to $k = 3.0$ --- so resamples
that shifted the plateau a little below four never crossed at all, and the interval computed from
the resamples that did cross is conditioned on crossing. It is narrow for the same reason it is
wrong. The pre-registration commits to extending the grid whenever the fraction rises materially
above the others and to reporting both grids. Extending Arm A2 to $k \in \{3.5, 4.0, 5.0\}$, where
the curve reaches $6.71$, $8.61$ and $21.52$, takes the fraction to $0.0\%$, leaves the point
estimate at $0.979$ and widens the interval from $[0.96, 0.98]$ to $[0.96, 1.67]$. The seed-$80$
extension behaved the same way. The rule was written for these arms and, on a later reading of the
column above, applied to the one run in the whole paper that also triggers it: the KL3M-1.7B control,
at $3.4\%$ on a grid topping out at $k = 3.2$ with a bootstrap upper end of $3.13$, $0.07$ below the
ceiling. The same three budgets take it to $0.0\%$, leave the ratio at $1.155$ and widen the interval
from $[1.14, 1.39]$ to $[1.14, 1.47]$ --- and, in near-verbatim recall, the main text's
Section~\ref{sec:onset} interval from $[2.52, 3.13]$ to $[2.52, 3.32]$ with the onset unmoved at
$2.578$. In every case the widening is upward and the lower end does not move, which is why the
claim that both KL3M intervals exclude $1$ is unaffected. Only the extended grids are used above.

\paragraph{The temperature arms, and the one confound in them}
The $\tau = 0.4$ arms move $s(x)$ by $67\%$ and $62\%$ with everything the seed arms hold fixed
still fixed, which is the only design here that varies the budget's unit within a single pair. The
elasticity $d\log(\text{onset})/d\log s(x)$ is $+0.72$ $[+0.41, +0.84]$ and $+0.61$ $[+0.40, +0.96]$:
both exclude $0$, refuting a budget quoted in a constant number of nats, and both exclude $1$, so
the onset does not simply scale with the rate either.

The confound is that the decoder warps \emph{both} logit vectors before the KL solve, so $\tau = 0.4$
sharpens the risky model too and its unconstrained recall rises, $0.519$ to $0.904$ on KL3M-520M and
$0.909$ to $0.964$ on Pleias-1.2B. A sharper memoriser reaches the threshold at a lower budget, so
the warped arm's onset is biased down and the elasticity toward $0$ --- toward the null being
refuted. Restricting both arms to the passages the memoriser reproduces in \emph{both} at $k=-1$
(near-verbatim recall $\ge 0.7$, a threshold fixed for power before any onset was computed) matches
unconstrained strength by construction:

Dropping $62$ of $100$ passages from the KL3M pair and closing a strength gap of $0.519$ against
$0.904$ changes the estimate in the third decimal, although the onsets it is built from do move
($2.492 \to 2.462$ and $3.603 \to 3.550$; Pleias $2.780 \to 2.748$ and $3.740 \to 3.694$). Whatever
the warp does to the onset, it does not do it through the memoriser. What matching costs is power:
on $38$ and $86$ passages the upper ends reach $1.00$ and $1.03$, so sub-proportionality is a
full-sample statement and is reported as one, while the part that refutes the null survives both.

The $\tau = 0.7$ arms were run as a dose check and are uninformative by the rule committed before
either was scored: they move $s(x)$ by $0.09$--$0.11$ log units against the $\tau = 0.4$ arms'
$0.62$--$0.67$, so the elasticity divides by a lever six times too small. KL3M-520M returns $+0.81$
$[-0.53, +1.23]$ and Pleias-1.2B $-0.18$ $[-1.07, +2.58]$; both intervals contain $0$, $1$ and both
$\tau = 0.4$ estimates. Their point estimates straddle, one agreeing with the decisive arms and one
not, and neither fact is evidence. They are reported for completeness and carry no weight in either
direction.

\paragraph{The same relation across the nine pairs}
Ranking the nine by the words their adversary holds gives a Spearman correlation with the onset
ratio of $-0.958$ (exact permutation test over all $9!$ orderings, $p = 0.0002$), and the trend
continues inside the five-pair coarse family alone, across a range of $1.1$ words, at $-0.800$
($p = 0.13$ at $n=5$). At seven pairs it read $-0.919$ at $p = 0.007$; the eighth was placed
between the groups on purpose and \emph{strengthened} it, and so did the ninth, which was built to
control the eighth's weak memoriser and shares its tokenizer and therefore its seed exactly. The two
sit at $1.027$ and $0.993$, apart by less than the width of either cluster. This is confounded with granularity by
construction and cannot stand on its own; what it adds is that the relation is not a two-group
artifact, and that it is visible among five pairs built for unrelated reasons.

\paragraph{The intervention applied to all nine pairs at once}
The five interventions above move one pair at a time. The same knob can be turned on every pair
together: hand each adversary the same number of \emph{words} instead of the same number of tokens,
and ask how much of the spread in onset$/s(x)$ survives. Seven of the nine arms needed for that are
already on record --- the two KL3M pairs at \texttt{--seed-tokens 40} ($14.8$ words), and five pairs
whose $20$-token seed already buys $13.9$--$15.0$ --- so the only new runs are the two open-calm
pairs at \texttt{--seed-tokens 30}, which buys them $14.63$ words. Bands, grid rule, entry gate,
primary metric and four excluded alternatives were committed before either was swept
(\texttt{results/onset\_prediction\_matched\_context.md}); the recomputation of the seven and the
prediction of the two are separated there, because only the second is blind.

The primary metric is the longest common substring in words, for the reason the interventions above
use it: a longer seed shortens the target, and an absolute count cannot be inflated by that. The
near-verbatim ratio is computed in the same pass and reported beside it.

land inside their committed $[0.85, 0.96]$, the 1B at its upper edge at $0.959$. Their entry gates

Two things make this more than a restatement of the ranking. First, equalising a variable that
merely \emph{correlates} with an outcome need not shrink the outcome's spread: if granularity acted
on the onset through the vocabulary, the target's length, or the tokenizer's fit to English prose,
lengthening the seed would leave the ratio where it was, because none of those changes. Second, the
mechanism is the one the single-pair arms already showed --- $s(x)$ moves by $0.5$--$0.7\%$ under
the longer seed while $k_{\mathrm{crit}}$ falls about $11\%$, so the seed is starting the target at
a cheaper point on a profile it does not otherwise touch.

What survives is $0.113$ of spread, with the two KL3M pairs still on top at $0.979$ and $0.939$.
The claim scored here is a fraction and not a closure, and the pre-registration said so before the
numbers existed: $61\%$ of the nine-pair spread is the benchmark's seed convention and $39\%$ is
not.

\paragraph{What the context costs the units claim, and what conditioning on it recovers}
The claim that $s(x)$ is the right unit for a budget has a null a referee will reach for: quote a
constant number of nats and do not rescale at all. Over the nine pairs it nearly holds. Predicting
a held-out pair's onset as $c\,s(x)$, with $c$ the mean ratio of the other eight, gives a mean
absolute error of $0.245$ nats per token; predicting the other eight's mean onset in nats gives
$0.388$. Across an $s(x)$ range of $1.61\times$, rescaling buys $1.6\times$.

Conditioning on the adversary's context is what separates them, and it now does so on both sides.
Among the five pairs handed $13.9$--$15.0$ words, over an $s(x)$ range of $1.49\times$ --- nearly
the whole range --- the same leave-one-out gives $0.070$ nats against the constant's $0.364$; among
the four handed fewer than ten it gives $0.195$ against $0.563$. Pooled, the ratio's coefficient
of variation is $10.0\%$; within the groups it is $2.4\%$ and $7.1\%$:

A five-of-nine subgroup is the shape a chance finding takes, so the multiplicity check is an exact

subsets of eight and $p = 0.048$ over the $21$ of seven. It is still not a test of the grouping
rule, which was read off these same measurements. What tests it are the interventions above, which
move a single pair across the boundary in both directions with everything else fixed, and the eighth
pair, whose ratio was predicted from the other seven before its anchor was fine-tuned.
