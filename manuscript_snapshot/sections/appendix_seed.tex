\section{What the evaluation hands the adversary}\label{app:seed}

\paragraph{Three quantities move together, and none of them is the tokenizer}
Each benchmark item is a prompt prefix followed by its continuation, both from the same novel, and
the attack seeds the adversary with \texttt{--seed-tokens 20}: a fixed number of \emph{tokens}. For one
fixed piece of text a finer tokenizer therefore changes three things at once --- the words the seed
buys, the decode steps before the continuation is reached, and the target's length in tokens --- and
all three split the seven pairs exactly where the onset ratio splits, with no overlap in any of
them:

Seed length is measured on the passages each run used, not inferred from an average. Because a
twenty-token seed is exactly twenty times the characters per token, no choice of pairs can separate
these; only an intervention can.

\paragraph{Two interventions, in opposite directions}
Both were committed before they ran, with the bands that would refute them. Each changes
\texttt{--seed-tokens} alone: the tokenizer, the anchor, the memoriser, the corpus, the passages, the
budget grid and the metric are held fixed, and $s(x)$ is recomputed at the matching seed because the
surprisal rate is measured over the target given the seed.

The primary metric is the longest common subsequence in words at a threshold of four, an absolute
count that a changed target length cannot inflate; near-verbatim recall at $0.01$ is reported
alongside. Intervals are bootstrapped over passages.

\paragraph{What the two arms do and do not establish}
Arm B moves a pair from one family's band across the vacuity threshold into the other's, and its
interval is disjoint from its control's; the pre-registered band for ``the seed is the mechanism''
was $\ge 1.00$ and the measurement is $1.004$. Arm A moves the converse pair in the converse
direction, from $1.032$ to $0.939$, but its interval overlaps its control's and it lands in a range
the pre-registration had already declared undecided between two accounts --- seed matching, which
predicted $0.85$--$0.95$, and a token-bucket account on which the seed acts by choosing which token
the adversary must generate first, predicting $0.96$ from a $7.3\%$ fall in $k_{\mathrm{crit}}$.
We report it as undecided rather than assigning it, and neither arm reaches the other family's band,
so the adversary's context is not the whole of the difference between the families.

Two checks that the arms are measuring what they claim. The unconstrained model reproduces either
target in every arm --- $k=-1$ recall $0.909$ against $0.894$ for the Pleias pair and $0.519$
against $0.520$ for the KL3M pair --- so the intervention changes what the adversary is given, not
how well the memoriser knows the text, and the anchor alone leaks nothing in any of the four runs.
And the residual difference in target length points the wrong way to explain the result: Arm B's
target is $3.7\%$ longer than its control's, which on an absolute word count can only make the
threshold easier to reach and the onset \emph{lower}, while the measured onset rose by $17.7\%$.

\paragraph{The same relation across the seven pairs}
Ranking the seven by the words their adversary holds gives a Spearman correlation with the onset
ratio of $-0.919$ (exact permutation test over all $7!$ orderings, $p = 0.007$), and the trend
continues inside the five-pair coarse family alone, across a range of $1.4$ words, at $-0.800$
($p = 0.13$ at $n=5$). This is confounded with granularity by construction and cannot stand on its
own; what it adds is that the relation is not a two-group artifact, and that it is visible among
five pairs built for unrelated reasons.
