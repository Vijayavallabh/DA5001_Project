\section{The metered decoder, audited}\label{app:onset}

\paragraph{Both factors of Proposition~\ref{prop:imitation}.}
Table~\ref{tab:imitation} measures them at the audited pair. Below the imitation rate the bucket binds
almost everywhere and the decoder spends most of its allowance; above it the bucket goes slack, the
realised rate stops at $0.857$ nats per token, and the certificate at $k=20$ is written against a quantity
the decoder uses $4.3\%$ of. The crossover is the pair's mean $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{r,t}\|p_{s,t})$. $\beta$ counts every step the
bucket touches, including the prefix-debt opening where the anchor writes the token outright;
strictly interior blends alone are $0.9\%$ of steps at $k=3$. On the
$3{,}300$ protected-passage trajectories the rate is higher, $0.911$ nats per token: the meter charges most
where the risky model is most distinct from the anchor, which is where memorisation lives. The spend is
spread, not concentrated: the busiest $1\%$ of steps carry $5.8\%$ of it and covering $90\%$ takes $64\%$ of
the sequence. At $k=20$ the decoder serves $p_{r,t}$ unchanged at every step, so $N_\varepsilon = \Theta(T)$
for every $\varepsilon$ below the imitation rate, the vacuous horn in Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}'s own
currency. Table~\ref{tab:imitation-pairs} repeats the measurement at four pairs: the rate saturates (ratio
against a band of $1.10$ fixed in advance), realised spend converges on it to within $0.14\%$, and at $k=20$
the decoder serves $p_r$ unchanged at $\ge 99.95\%$ of steps. The level orders by how far the risky model
has been \emph{tuned} from the anchor, not by its size: a $70$B base model reads $0.8330$ against the $8$B
instruction-tuned model's $0.8278$ at $k=3$, where we had predicted it higher. The same-family prediction
(instruct rate below half the base rate) passed narrowly, by $2.2\%$ of its threshold.

\paragraph{What a windowed meter would spend.}\label{app:windowlr}
Section~\ref{sec:uncovered} asks what a meter certifying every $50$-token window would pay to serve the
risky model. The per-step log of the risky model's own draws records $p_r$ and $p_s$ of every served
token, so the realised log-ratio $\sum_t \log(p_{r,t}(y_t)/p_{s,t}(y_t))$ of every window is a sum over the
log: $1{,}500$ trajectories on the $500$ ordinary prompts, $299{,}636$ steps before end-of-text,
$226{,}136$ windows (\texttt{results/\allowbreak window\_\allowbreak logratio.csv}). Its median is $40.3$
nats, its $90$th and $99$th percentiles $63.9$ and $92.5$, its maximum $188.7$; the per-step KL summed
over the same windows, what serving $p_r$ costs in expectation, reads $40.7$, $59.6$ and $85.8$. A
trajectory's largest window has median $63.2$ nats and exceeds $125.3$ on $1\%$ of trajectories, and
$0.011\%$ of windows reach the protected window's median surprisal of $159.8$. A sliding-window meter
with a budget near $125$ nats would rarely bind on ordinary text and would certify the median protected
window at $e^{125-159.8}$; it certifies no event whose probability under the anchor must be sampled
(Section~\ref{sec:compose}).

\paragraph{Selection in installments.}\label{app:blockwise}
Section~\ref{sec:uncovered}'s installment rule draws $n$ continuations of up to $L$ tokens from the anchor
alone, scores each by the committed reward of the candidate completed to $200$ tokens by one anchor
rollout, serves the argmax and repeats until $200$ tokens or an end-of-text; at $L = 200$ it is
whole-output selection through the same code, on fresh draws. Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} holds per
block, so $B$ blocks certify the output at $B\log n$ and any $50$-token window at
$\min(\lceil 50/L\rceil + 1, B)\log n$ (Table~\ref{tab:blockwise}; registered in
\texttt{results/\allowbreak onset\_\allowbreak prediction\_\allowbreak blockwise.md}). At $64$ draws per block,
installments of $10$ and $25$ tokens beat one choice among $64$ whole drafts, by $+0.0415$
$[+0.0185, +0.0645]$ and $+0.0245$ $[+0.001, +0.0475]$, against our registered prediction of a tie, and
installments of $50$ tie it; their window certificates, $24.95$ and $12.48$ nats, stay far below the
protected window's $159.8$. Held instead to one whole-output $\log 64$, spent as $2$, $3$ or $6$
installments of $8$, $4$ or $2$ draws, they lose to one choice among $64$ by $0.0635$, $0.082$ and $0.088$:
a decoder steered by the same reward with its budget of $\log 64$ fixed up front, the closest we ran to a
token-level reward tilt at that budget, does worse than selection spent once. Scored on the prefix alone,
without the rollout, the rule serves $23$ words at the median and loses to the rollout by $0.0395$
$[0.02, 0.059]$. The risky model as planner, scoring each $10$-token block by $\log p_r - \log p_s$ over the
same $64$ draws, gains $+0.077$ $[+0.054, +0.1005]$ over the anchor, $+0.026$ $[+0.001, +0.051]$ more than
the meter does at $k=10$ (post hoc), and loses to the reward by $0.0895$ $[0.064, 0.115]$: the same choice
buys more when the reward makes it than when the risky model does. The memoriser choosing twenty times among $64$ ten-token continuations
reads near-verbatim recall $0.0000$ on all $100$ passages it memorised (alone it reads $0.3925$), and
ROUGE-L $0.1168$ against whole-output selection's $0.1073$. With the prompt and the served text each in
its own slot of the reward's template, every rule serves more empty answers than the committed pool's $41$ ($103$ once, $104$ to $110$ in
installments of $64$ draws, $113$ to $192$ at one $\log 64$), and the planner none.

\emph{Two refined predictions, both refuted.} The rate a decoder must permit before it can pay for $x$
at all,
\begin{equation}
r(x) \;=\; s_s(x) - s_r(x),
\label{eq:req}
\end{equation}
predicts an onset ratio of $1 - s_r/s_s$ that must move with memorisation. Committed for the last five
pairs before any was swept, it is refuted under all three registered rules: every miss low, held-out errors
of $0.365$, $0.507$ and $0.296$ nats, and a directional claim that inverts. Eq.~\eqref{eq:req} is right
about what a decoder must \emph{afford} (Table~\ref{tab:imitation}'s crossover) and wrong about where
leakage \emph{begins}. The running maximum of Proposition~\ref{prop:outrun} fails in the complementary
direction: warping the anchor's logits to $\tau = 0.4$ moves $s(x)$ by $67\%$ and $62\%$ on two pairs and
the onset follows at an elasticity of $+0.72$ $[+0.41, +0.84]$ and $+0.61$ $[+0.40, +0.96]$, while
$k_{\mathrm{crit}}$ misses by $21.9\%$ against a no-change null's $12.3\%$.

\emph{The level is the claim, not the ordering.}\label{app:collapse} Holding one pair, its corpus, anchor
and grid fixed and varying only the memoriser's fine-tuning length moves the onset ratio from $0.8756$ to
$1.3477$; re-seeding at a fixed epoch count moves it by $0.033$ to $0.080$ where the fine-tune converged
and by $0.2597$ where the memoriser is marginal. \textbf{The within-pair spread is $1.64\times$ the entire
between-pair spread} of $0.8784$ to $1.1658$, and whether a pair's interval excludes $1$ flips inside that
single pair, from $[0.68, 0.98]$ to $[1.01, 1.59]$. On two further corpora (Table~\ref{tab:gutenberg}) two
of three pairs vary by $0.088$ and $0.066$, inside their own bootstrap widths of $0.227$ and $0.287$, and
the third by $0.436$ against $0.171$; on the third corpus the ordering inverts, Pleias-1.2B reading $1.314$
above the fine-tokenizer pair, whose interval no longer excludes $1$. Every onset in these arms lands
between $0.80$ and $1.35$ of $s(x)$, which is what Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} claims; which pairs
begin above $1$ is confounded with training variables the table does not hold fixed.

\paragraph{The uncertified interval is the opening.}\label{app:opening}
On the $758$ passages under four safe models, $k_{\mathrm{crit}}/s$ has a median of $4.25$ to $6.10$, but
the running maximum in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rates} is attained at the \emph{first} token of the work in $87.7\%$
to $90.5\%$ of cases, because the bucket must pay for the opening out of one step's allowance. An adversary
who supplies one genuine prefix token removes it: the ratio falls to $1.32$--$1.48$ and stays there at $5$,
$10$ and $20$ supplied tokens. Per token instead of per character the whole-work median is $3.88$--$5.98$
and the ratios after a supplied prefix are unchanged ($1.35$--$1.41$). This is why oracle-prefix windows
recover $0.0965$ of a passage at $k=3$ against $0.0116$ for a single query (Table~\ref{tab:orders}).

\paragraph{Vacuity on a hundred books.}\label{app:bookmia}
The vacuity verdict needs no memoriser, only forward passes of the anchor, so we re-measured it on BookMIA
\citep{shi2024detecting} with the $758$ passages as a positive control (Table~\ref{tab:bookmia}). $65$ of
$4{,}935$ seen passages are surprising enough that a $600$-nat budget does not cover them, which is the
weaker reading registered rather than a replication; the unseen half and the control read $1.0000$. The
halves are not exchangeable under an anchor that saw neither (medians $6.0\%$ apart against a specified
$5\%$), so no arm treats them as a matched pair. A first pass scored vacuity as $k \ge s(x)$ per token and
read $0.326$ on the control: that criterion agrees with $K \ge S(x)$ only when the cap and the work have
the same length, and a deployment caps at $200$ tokens against a protected span near $60$.

\paragraph{A pair where memorisation is the only variable.}\label{app:second-anchor}
The earlier audit recorded a vocabulary mismatch ($64{,}000$ against $128{,}256$ tokens) as the reason no
second anchor could be fused with the risky model. The obstacle was an implementation check that the
embedding table have exactly $\mathrm{len}(\text{tokenizer})$ rows, which the Common Pile $7$B checkpoint
fails by padding $64{,}000$ tokens to $64{,}256$ rows; masking the untrained rows out of the solve removes
it. We fine-tune that $7$B model until it reproduces the protected passages ($0.915$ recall on its
training split, $0.000$ held out) and fuse it against the unmodified model it came from, so anchor and
risky model share every weight but those memorisation moved. Across $7$ budgets, both baselines, $5$
strategies and $31{,}738$ budgeted queries there is no invariant violation. The anchor alone reproduces
$0.0000$ under every strategy; at $k=20$ the decoder is the identity (single-query recall $0.7189$, equal
to the unconstrained model's to four decimals); recall is $0.000$ at every budget up to $k=1$, $0.048$ at
$k=3$ and $0.197$ at $k=5$, beginning near $s(x) = 2.39$ and far below $k_{\mathrm{crit}} = 11.32$.

\paragraph{A better anchor widens the margin.}\label{app:scaling}
Ten openly licensed safe models ($0.17$B to $7$B, three corpora) score the same $758$ passages and the same
$300$ ordinary risky-model generations, in nats per character so no shared vocabulary is needed. Both the
passages' rate $s(x)$ and the ordinary output's divergence $c_{\mathrm{use}}$ fall as the anchor improves,
and $c_{\mathrm{use}}$ falls faster, so the margin $s(x)/c_{\mathrm{use}}$ rises with capability within
every family, for all sixteen novels individually (Table~\ref{tab:marginspan}), and with training tokens at
fixed size ($4.63$ to $5.02$ from the $1$T to the $2$T checkpoint of one $7$B model). Pooled across
families it is not monotone in parameter count, because the families sit at different levels.

\paragraph{A lower cap.}
At $k=0.5$ neither outside judge separates the meter from its own safe model over $600$ comparisons each
($-1.03\sigma$, $+1.52\sigma$); both separate only at $k \ge 10$ ($-6.97\sigma$, $-3.29\sigma$).

\paragraph{A better schedule.}
On the geodesic the charge and the fidelity bought are closed forms in $\psi(u) = \log Z(u)$,
\begin{equation}
C(\theta) = \theta\psi'(\theta) - \psi(\theta), \quad C'(\theta) = \theta\psi''(\theta), \qquad
G(\theta) = \theta m - \psi(\theta), \quad G'(\theta) = m - \psi'(\theta),
\label{eq:charge-gain}
\end{equation}
with $m = \psi'(1)$, so $C'(0) = 0$ while $G'(0)$ is the Jeffreys divergence between the two models
\citep{monteiropaes2026limits}: the first nat bought at a step is worth the most. The bucket takes whatever
price $G'/C'$ its allowance lands on; the best allocation of the same total equalises it. Computed from two
teacher-forced passes over $30$ targets of three kinds (memorised passages, a held-out novel, the risky
model's own samples), the optimal reallocation buys $3$ to $13\%$ more fidelity at $k \le 1$ and under
$1.4\%$ at $k=3$, against an offline optimum no causal scheduler can beat \citep{tomasi2026primaldual}; the
three target kinds agree to within two points. The bucket already captures $46.7$--$54.5\%$ of the
$\theta = 1$ fidelity at $k=0.25$ and $92.7$--$98.9\%$ at $k=3$, so three orders of magnitude are not there
to recover.

\paragraph{The budget granted up front.}
The one causal policy Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse} positively permits spends a budget granted at the
start. Judged order-averaged against the committed opponent it gains $-0.0015$ $[-0.0200,+0.0170]$ at
$\log 8$ nats, $+0.0215$ $[+0.0015,+0.0415]$ at $\log 64$ and $+0.0545$ $[+0.0315,+0.0775]$ at $64$ nats:
the causal horn is not empty, but at matched budget selection leads by $+0.083$ $[+0.052,+0.1135]$ and no
budget on the grid reaches selection's $+0.1045$ at $\log 64$. Choosing \emph{where} to spend does not help:
a rule that spends at step $t$ only if the full-tilt demand reaches $\tau$ nats reserves as designed (median
spending step $3 \to 17$) and gains $+0.0215$, $+0.0150$, $+0.0110$, $+0.0045$ and $+0.0070$ at
$\tau = 0,1,2,4,8$, falling except for a tick at the last, because a higher bar releases the budget less
often ($0\%$ to $86.7\%$ of trajectories never spend) \emph{and} buys less when it does. An earlier arm,
judged single-order and not compared with these, gave the front-loaded rule $-0.008$ $[-0.056, +0.041]$
at $\log 8 = 2.0794$ nats against selection's $+0.054$ at the same budget.

\paragraph{Two anchors closer to the risky model.}\label{app:frontier3}
Neither anchor here is a safe model, so no certificate, leakage or $s(x)$ number is taken from either; the pairs test whether the reversal is the mechanism's or the anchor's. A token-level meter needs a shared vocabulary, and no other openly licensed anchor has one with this risky model. At Llama-3.2-1B the meter is useful,
$+0.051$, $+0.056$ and $+0.055$ under judge~B at $k=1,3,20$ for $88.1$ to $123.4$ nats, and selection at
$n=8$ gains more, $+0.076$ $[+0.031, +0.122]$ and $+0.154$ $[+0.104, +0.200]$ on two judges, for $1.204$
nats. At Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, the closest meterable anchor and the one the protocol named the hardest
case, the meter resolves at no budget on either judge (all eight point estimates negative, spends $54.8$ to
$60.6$ nats over a $40\times$ range of caps) while selection gains $+0.150$ $[+0.106, +0.194]$ and $+0.273$
$[+0.227, +0.323]$. A close anchor flattens the meter onto the anchor. The arms each pass holds twice (the
meter's $k=0$ and selection's first draw) differ by at most $0.034$, and the same size appears independently
at Comma-7B ($0.039$), so \textbf{the cross-pass floor on a judged gain is about $0.04$}, and gains from
different passes are never compared here.
