\section{Where extraction begins, and why there}\label{sec:onset}

Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} locates the budget at which the certificate stops saying anything,
for every order. It does not say leakage begins there; this section asks where it does.

\paragraph{Seven pairs}
The first is the audited one, a $1.8$B Common Pile anchor against a memorising Llama-3.1-8B. The
other six are built so that memorisation is the only difference: we fine-tune a model until it
reproduces the passages and fuse it against the \emph{unmodified} model it came from, so the two
share an architecture, a corpus, a tokenizer and every weight the memorisation did not move
(Appendix~\ref{app:second-anchor}). Three are deliberately unlike the rest: two KL3M anchors whose
tokenizer emits about two characters per token where the others emit four, differing from each
other in memorisation alone ($s_r/s_s = 0.09$ against $0.35$), and a Phi-3.5-mini anchor with
KL3M's vocabulary size but the others' granularity. A pair enters only if the \emph{unconstrained}
risky model reproduces the passages, $k=-1$ recall above $0.10$: the seven reach $0.41$ to $0.91$,
and one earlier memoriser failed that screen (Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}).

\paragraph{The onset tracks the vacuity threshold, to within about a fifth}
Measured as the budget at which mean single-query recall first reaches $0.01$, interpolated inside
its bracket:

Rates here are nats per \emph{token}, unlike Section~\ref{sec:scaling}, because the decoder meters
one charge per decoded token; the ratio is unaffected, numerator and denominator being charged
alike. Across pairs sharing no anchor, no tokenizer and no risky model, $s(x)$ spans $1.61\times$
per token and $1.87\times$ per character, and every onset falls between $0.88$ and $1.17$ of it.
\emph{The fraction is not fixed, and it is not noise either.} The five pairs whose tokenizers emit
$3.6$ to $4.2$ characters per token sit at $0.878$--$0.926$; the two KL3M pairs, at about two, sit
at $1.053$ and $1.166$, with bootstrap intervals whose lower end exceeds $1$. On those two, leakage
begins \emph{after} the certificate has gone vacuous.

The split is not about how well the model memorised. The two KL3M pairs were built to test that:
they share a tokenizer and a target length and differ in memorisation, $s_r/s_s = 0.09$ against
$0.35$, and the thorough one still sits at $1.053$ while its $s_r/s_s$ falls between two pairs that
sit at $0.892$ and $0.920$. Nor is it the length of the target: for a fixed corpus that is the same
variable as the tokenizer, so we decoded the KL3M pair on the first $276$ tokens of each target
instead, matching the coarse pairs' step count with the tokenizer unchanged, and the ratio stayed at
$1.09$ (Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}). Nor is it the size of the vocabulary, the reading one reaches for
first: Phi-3.5-mini carries $32{,}011$ types against KL3M's $32{,}768$ but cuts these passages at
$3.8$ characters per token against $2.0$, and it lands at $0.926$, with the coarse family and not
with the pair whose vocabulary it matches. Its interval, $[0.80, 1.09]$, is too wide to exclude the
KL3M values on its own, so what it settles is negative: vocabulary size is not the variable. Nor is
it the recipe: a Llama-3.1-70B base that memorised \emph{Harry Potter} in
pretraining~\citep{cooper2025extracting} leaks at the built pairs' rate (Appendix~\ref{app:collapse}).

It is, in large part, what the evaluation hands the adversary. The attack seeds a fixed number of
\emph{tokens}, so the words that buys are the tokenizer's business: $7.3$ on the KL3M pairs against
$13.0$--$14.4$ on the other five, an exact split with no overlap (Appendix~\ref{app:seed}). Two
interventions, each pre-registered with its refuting band, change nothing but that number. Giving Pleias-1.2B the KL3M seed --- $6.9$ words instead of
$13.7$ --- moves it from $0.866$ $[0.72, 0.95]$ to $1.004$ $[0.99, 1.23]$, intervals that do not
overlap. Giving KL3M-520M the others' --- $14.3$ words, against TinyComma's $14.4$ --- moves it the
other way, from $1.032$ $[1.01, 1.23]$ to $0.939$ $[0.88, 1.17]$, in the predicted direction but
with an interval too wide to carry the claim alone. Neither arm reaches the other family's band, so
the seed is not the whole of the difference; it is enough, in both directions, to move a pair across
the budget at which its own certificate goes vacuous.

This is not a quirk of our harness: extractability is itself defined ``with $k$ tokens of
context''~\citep{carlini2023quantifying} and rises with the context supplied --- $33\%$ at $50$
tokens against $65\%$ at $450$ there. Two such measurements under different tokenizers at one token
budget differ in a quantity that drives what they measure.

\paragraph{A refinement we derived, pre-registered, and had to reject}
The geometry supplies a second candidate, which we pre-registered and must reject. On the geometric
path $\psi_t(1) = \log \sum_v p_s(v) e^{\ell_t(v)} = 0$ identically, so at full tilt the per-token
charge along a target $x$ is $\frac{1}{T}\sum_t D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{r,t}\,\|\,p_{s,t})$ on $x$'s own
prefixes; when the risky model is near-deterministic on a memorised $x$ that collapses onto the
realised token and becomes a difference of surprisals. Define
\begin{equation}
r(x) \;=\; s_s(x) \;-\; s_r(x),
\label{eq:req}
\end{equation}
the budget rate a decoder must permit before it can pay for $x$ at all; it needs no decoding and no
attack. It also predicts an onset ratio of $1 - s_r/s_s$, which must \emph{move} with memorisation:
$0.997$, $0.940$, $0.925$, $0.911$, $0.908$, $0.886$ and $0.647$ here.

Predictions for the last five pairs were committed before any was swept. Three rules were on the
table --- Eq.~\eqref{eq:req} at the median of $r(x)$ with nothing fitted, the same at $q_{25}$, and
a fitted constant $c\,s(x)$ --- and the two KL3M pairs decide against all three, every miss low and
all six predictions outside the measured intervals, for held-out errors of $0.352$, $0.251$ and
$0.481$ nats. The directional claim does not merely fail, it inverts: the pair with by far the most
residual surprisal has the highest ratio of all, and the rank correlation between predicted and
measured ratio is $-0.18$ where the derivation requires it positive. Eq.~\eqref{eq:req} is a correct
statement about what a decoder must \emph{afford} and a wrong one about where leakage
\emph{begins} --- at the onset budget it is satisfied for $27\%$ and $26\%$ of works on the two
pairs where we can check it while $0\%$ and $1\%$ leak (Appendix~\ref{app:collapse}).

Neither quantity screens an individual book: as a classifier for ``which passage leaks at budget
$k$'', $r(x)$ reaches a mean AUC of $0.678$ and $s(x)$ alone $0.751$. Either locates the budget at
which a \emph{class} of works becomes exposed; for one book neither suffices.
