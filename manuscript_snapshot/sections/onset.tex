\section{Where extraction begins, and why there}\label{sec:onset}

Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} locates the budget at which the certificate stops saying anything
and shows no order of the charge moves it. It does not say leakage begins there. This section
derives where leakage begins, from the two models and the work alone.

\paragraph{Four pairs}
The first is the audited one, a $1.8$B Common Pile anchor against a memorising Llama-3.1-8B. The
other three are built so that memorisation is the only difference: we fine-tune a model until it
reproduces the passages and fuse it against the \emph{unmodified} model it came from, so the two
share an architecture, a corpus, a tokenizer and every weight the memorisation did not move
(Appendix~\ref{app:second-anchor}). They run $7$B, $1.2$B and $350$M across two corpora, and a
weaker memoriser keeps more residual surprisal on the text it memorised --- $s_r$ from $0.18$ to
$0.37$ --- which is what separates the two readings below. A pair enters only if the
\emph{unconstrained} risky model reproduces the passages, single-query recall at $k=-1$ above
$0.10$: the four reach $0.72$, $0.72$, $0.88$ and $0.91$. An earlier $350$M memoriser reached
$0.71$ greedily but $0.022$ under the sampling the attack uses, and is excluded.

\paragraph{The onset is a fixed fraction of the vacuity threshold}
Measured as the budget at which mean single-query recall first reaches $0.01$, interpolated inside
its bracket:

Rates here are nats per \emph{token}, unlike Section~\ref{sec:scaling}, because the decoder meters
one charge per decoded token and each pair shares a vocabulary by construction. That cannot affect
the ratio, whose numerator and denominator are charged in the same units, but it does inflate the
dynamic range: across pairs sharing no anchor, no tokenizer and no risky model, $s(x)$ spans
$1.49\times$ per token and $1.33\times$ per character, and the onset spans the same factor. The
ratio runs from $0.878$ to $0.920$, mean $0.894$, standard deviation $0.016$. Rescaling the budget by $s(x)$ collapses the recall curves to
a mean spread of $0.012$ over $k/s(x) \in [0.7, 1.0]$, degrading to $0.041$ above the threshold
(Figure~\ref{fig:onset}). The intervals are wide --- they come from resampling passages, and near
the onset a handful of works out of a hundred carry the mean --- so the agreement of the point
estimates is finer than any one of them deserves --- re-measuring the last row at $458$ passages
moved its ratio from $0.895$ to $0.920$ (Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}). What the four pairs
jointly support is the proportionality, not the third decimal.

\paragraph{A pair nobody fine-tuned}
All four pairs above are built, so the law could be a property of the recipe. It is not:
Llama-3.1-70B base, which memorised \emph{Harry Potter} in
pretraining~\citep{cooper2025extracting}, leaks $0.0180$ at $k/s(x) = 0.964$ against $0.010$ to
$0.039$ for the built pairs, at both of the mechanism authors' decoding settings. Its budget grid
is too coarse to interpolate an onset from, so Appendix~\ref{app:collapse} gives that comparison
in full.

\paragraph{A refinement we derived and pre-registered}
The constant invites an explanation, and the geometry supplies a candidate. On the geometric path
the log-partition satisfies $\psi_t(1) = \log \sum_v p_s(v) e^{\ell_t(v)} = 0$ identically, so at
full tilt the per-token charge accumulated \emph{along a target} $x$ is
$\frac{1}{T}\sum_t D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{r,t}\,\|\,p_{s,t})$ on $x$'s own prefixes. When the risky
model is near-deterministic on a memorised $x$ that expectation collapses onto the realised token
and the charge becomes a difference of surprisals. Define
\begin{equation}
r(x) \;=\; s_s(x) \;-\; s_r(x),
\label{eq:req}
\end{equation}
the budget rate a decoder must permit before it can pay for $x$ at all. It needs no decoding and no
attack. It also predicts that the onset ratio is $1 - s_r/s_s$ and must therefore \emph{move} as
memorisation changes --- $0.940$, $0.925$, $0.908$ and $0.886$ for these four pairs.

Predictions for the third and fourth pairs were committed to the repository before either was
swept, so those two are a test and the first two, on which the alternatives were calibrated, are
not. Three rules were on the table: Eq.~\eqref{eq:req} used directly at the median of $r(x)$, which
has nothing fitted in it; the same quantity calibrated to a low quantile of the $r(x)$ distribution,
which pairs one and two place at $q_{25}$; and a single fitted constant $c\,s(x)$ with $c = 0.889$.
On the two held-out pairs their mean absolute errors are $0.034$, $0.224$ and $0.073$ nats. The
calibrated quantile is rejected: it misses the third pair by $0.31$ nats, outside its interval. The
parameter-free rule is the most accurate of the three --- more accurate than the fitted constant it
was meant to explain.

That is weaker support than it sounds. Two held-out pairs is two points, and every prediction from
both surviving rules falls inside its bootstrap interval, so this data excludes neither. And the
derivation's \emph{directional} claim fails: it says the ratio must fall as the memoriser leaves
more residual surprisal, and the rank correlation between predicted and measured ratio over the
four pairs is $+0.20$. Eq.~\eqref{eq:req} sets the scale and does not order the pairs.

It can do the one and not the other because affordability is necessary and far from sufficient: at
the onset budget $r(x) \le k$ holds for $27\%$ and $26\%$ of works on the two pairs where we can
check it, while $0\%$ and $1\%$ leak. The budget governs what a decoder can pay for, not what the
risky model memorised well enough to emit.

\paragraph{A population boundary, not a per-work screen}
Equation~\eqref{eq:req} is defined per work, so it is fair to ask whether it screens an individual
book. It does not, and measurably so: as a classifier for ``which passage leaks at budget $k$'',
$r(x)$ reaches a mean AUC of $0.678$ while the anchor surprisal $s(x)$ \emph{alone} reaches $0.751$
(Appendix~\ref{app:collapse}). Subtracting $s_r$ hurts, and measurably so: across works it carries
$6$--$7\%$ of the level of $s_s$ but $44$--$45\%$ of its standard deviation, so it removes a
near-constant shift and adds variance. For the population boundary the ablation simply cannot
separate them: rescaling by $s(x)$ against rescaling by $r$ gives $0.0059$ against $0.0046$ on two
pairs, $0.0070$ against $0.0096$ on three, and $0.0180$ against $0.0143$ on four, so the winner
changes every time a pair is added. A rights-holder can use either to locate the budget at which a
class of works becomes exposed; for one particular book neither is good enough to rely on.
