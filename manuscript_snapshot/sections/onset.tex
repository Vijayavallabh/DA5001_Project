\section{Where extraction begins, and why there}\label{sec:onset}

Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} locates the budget at which the certificate stops saying anything,
for every order of the charge. It does not say leakage begins there; this section derives where it
does, from the two models and the work alone.

\paragraph{Seven pairs}
The first is the audited one, a $1.8$B Common Pile anchor against a memorising Llama-3.1-8B. The
other six are built so that memorisation is the only difference: we fine-tune a model until it
reproduces the passages and fuse it against the \emph{unmodified} model it came from, so the two
share an architecture, a corpus, a tokenizer and every weight the memorisation did not move
(Appendix~\ref{app:second-anchor}). They run $7$B, $1.2$B, $350$M, $1.7$B, $520$M and $3.8$B across
four model families, and three are deliberately unlike the rest: two KL3M anchors whose tokenizer
emits about two characters per token where the others emit four, differing from each other in
memorisation alone ($s_r/s_s = 0.09$ against $0.35$), and a Phi-3.5-mini anchor with KL3M's
vocabulary size but the others' granularity. A pair enters only if the \emph{unconstrained} risky
model reproduces the passages, single-query recall at $k=-1$ above $0.10$: the seven reach $0.72$,
$0.72$, $0.88$, $0.91$, $0.41$, $0.52$ and $0.57$; one earlier memoriser failed that screen and is
excluded (Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}).

\paragraph{The onset tracks the vacuity threshold, to within about a fifth}
Measured as the budget at which mean single-query recall first reaches $0.01$, interpolated inside
its bracket:

Rates here are nats per \emph{token}, unlike Section~\ref{sec:scaling}, because the decoder meters
one charge per decoded token and each pair shares a vocabulary by construction. That cannot affect
the ratio, whose numerator and denominator are charged in the same units. Across pairs sharing no
anchor, no tokenizer and no risky model, $s(x)$ spans $1.61\times$ per token and $1.87\times$ per
character, and every onset falls between $0.88$ and $1.17$ of it. \emph{The fraction is not fixed,
and it is not noise either.} The seven pairs split by how finely the tokenizer cuts the text: the
five whose tokenizers emit $3.6$ to $4.2$ characters per token sit at $0.878$--$0.926$, and the two
KL3M pairs, at about two characters per token, sit at $1.053$ and $1.166$ --- both with
bootstrap intervals whose lower end exceeds $1$. On those two, leakage begins \emph{after} the
certificate has gone vacuous. What survives is a unit rather than a law: a quantity computable from
the anchor and the work alone locates the onset within about a fifth on every pair, across a
$1.87\times$ spread a deployer would otherwise have to guess.

The split is not about how well the model memorised. The two KL3M pairs were built to test that:
they share a tokenizer and a target length and differ in memorisation, $s_r/s_s = 0.09$ against
$0.35$, and the thorough one still sits at $1.053$ while its $s_r/s_s$ falls between two pairs that
sit at $0.892$ and $0.920$. Nor is it the length of the target: for a fixed corpus that
is the same variable as the tokenizer --- the passage is $276$ tokens at four characters per token
and $580$ at two --- so we decoded the KL3M pair on the first $276$ tokens of each target instead,
matching the step count with the tokenizer unchanged, and the ratio stayed at $1.09$
(Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}). Nor is it the size of the vocabulary, the reading one reaches for
first: Phi-3.5-mini carries $32{,}011$ types against KL3M's $32{,}768$ but cuts these passages at
$3.8$ characters per token against $2.0$, and it lands at $0.926$, with the coarse family and not
with the pair whose vocabulary it matches. Its interval, $[0.80, 1.09]$, is too wide to exclude the
KL3M values alone, so what it settles is the negative one. Nor is the pattern an artifact of the
recipe: Llama-3.1-70B base, which memorised \emph{Harry Potter} in
pretraining~\citep{cooper2025extracting}, leaks at the built pairs' rate where the grids overlap
(Appendix~\ref{app:collapse}).

\paragraph{A refinement we derived, pre-registered, and had to reject}
The constant invites an explanation, and the geometry supplies one. On the geometric path
$\psi_t(1) = \log \sum_v p_s(v) e^{\ell_t(v)} = 0$ identically, so at full tilt the per-token charge
\emph{along a target} $x$ is $\frac{1}{T}\sum_t D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{r,t}\,\|\,p_{s,t})$ on $x$'s own
prefixes. When the risky
model is near-deterministic on a memorised $x$ that expectation collapses onto the realised token
and the charge becomes a difference of surprisals. Define
\begin{equation}
r(x) \;=\; s_s(x) \;-\; s_r(x),
\label{eq:req}
\end{equation}
the budget rate a decoder must permit before it can pay for $x$ at all; it needs no decoding and no
attack. It also predicts an onset ratio of $1 - s_r/s_s$, which must \emph{move} with memorisation:
$0.997$, $0.940$, $0.925$, $0.911$, $0.908$, $0.886$ and $0.647$ here.

Predictions for the last five pairs were committed before any was swept, so those five are a test
and the first two, on which the alternatives were calibrated, are not. Three rules were on the
table: Eq.~\eqref{eq:req} at the median of $r(x)$, nothing fitted; the same quantity at a low
quantile, which pairs one and two place at $q_{25}$; and a fitted constant $c\,s(x)$, $c = 0.889$.
The third and fourth pairs decided nothing, all three landing within $0.22$ nats. The two KL3M
pairs decide it: every rule misses both, every miss is low, and all six of those predictions fall
outside the measured intervals. Held-out mean absolute errors are $0.352$, $0.251$ and $0.481$
nats.

The directional claim does not merely fail, it inverts. Eq.~\eqref{eq:req} says the ratio should
fall as the memoriser leaves more residual surprisal; the pair with by far the most residual
surprisal has the highest ratio of all, and the rank correlation between predicted and measured
ratio over seven pairs is $-0.18$, where the derivation requires it to be positive. So
Eq.~\eqref{eq:req} is a correct statement about what a decoder must \emph{afford} and a wrong one
about where leakage \emph{begins}. The affordability check already exposed the gap: at the onset
budget $r(x) \le k$ holds for $27\%$ and $26\%$ of works on the two pairs where we can check it
while $0\%$ and $1\%$ leak, and the fifth pair reaches $98\%$ covered against $1\%$ leaking. What a
budget can pay for is not what the risky model memorised well enough to emit.

Both quantities are defined per work, so it is fair to ask whether either screens an individual
book. Neither does: as a classifier for ``which passage leaks at budget $k$'', $r(x)$ reaches a
mean AUC of $0.678$ and the anchor surprisal alone $0.751$ (Appendix~\ref{app:collapse}). A
rights-holder can use either to locate the budget at which a class of works becomes exposed; for
one book neither is good enough.
