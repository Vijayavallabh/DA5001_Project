\section{What the evaluation hands the adversary}\label{app:seed}

\paragraph{Three quantities move together, and none of them is the tokenizer}
Each benchmark item is a prompt prefix followed by its continuation, both from the same novel, and
the attack seeds the adversary with \texttt{--seed-tokens 20}: a fixed number of \emph{tokens}. For one
fixed piece of text a finer tokenizer therefore changes three things at once --- the words the seed
buys, the decode steps before the continuation is reached, and the target's length in tokens --- and
all three track the onset ratio without ordering the nine exactly: of the $34$ anchor pairs whose
granularity differs at all, the ratio is discordant with it on $2$ and the step count on $3$, and
two further pairs share a tokenizer outright, so granularity separates nothing between them:

Seed length and characters per token are measured over the first twenty tokens of the $100$ passages
each run used --- the split the memorisers were fine-tuned on --- and not inferred from a corpus
average, which is why the granularity figures here differ from the corpus-wide ones in
Section~\ref{sec:onset} in either direction (Phi-3.5-mini, $3.88$ here against $3.78$ over all $608$
texts; Comma-7B, $3.93$ against $3.62$). The ordering within the coarse family is not stable between
the two, one more reason no ranking of these pairs can carry the claim on its own. Because a
twenty-token seed is exactly twenty times the characters per token, no choice of pairs can separate
these three quantities; only an intervention can.

Whatever the warp does to the onset, it does not do it through the memoriser. Matching the two arms
on memoriser strength --- dropping $62$ of $100$ passages from the KL3M pair to close a strength gap
of $0.519$ against $0.904$ --- changes the elasticity in the third decimal, $+0.72$ both times, and
$+0.61$ both times on Pleias-1.2B, although the onsets it is built from do move
($2.492 \to 2.462$ and $3.603 \to 3.550$; Pleias $2.780 \to 2.748$ and $3.740 \to 3.694$). What
matching costs is power: on $38$ and $86$ passages the upper ends reach $1.00$ and $1.03$, so
sub-proportionality is a full-sample statement and is reported as one, while the part that refutes
the null survives both. The $\tau = 0.7$ arms were run as a dose check and are uninformative by the
rule fixed before either was scored --- they move $s(x)$ by $0.09$--$0.11$ log units against the
$\tau = 0.4$ arms' $0.62$--$0.67$, so the elasticity divides by a lever six times too small, and
$+0.81$ $[-0.53, +1.23]$ and $-0.18$ $[-1.07, +2.58]$ both contain $0$, $1$ and both $\tau = 0.4$
estimates. They carry no weight in either direction. Figure~\ref{fig:units-law} plots every arm on
one axis.

\emph{The move column and the spread row do not carry the same noise.} Each row's move is one
memoriser measured at two seed lengths, so whatever draw its fine-tune landed on is common to both
columns and cancels: these interventions are paired, and the re-seed dispersion quoted above does not
apply to them. The spread and coefficient-of-variation rows are taken across nine different
fine-tunes and do carry it, so the residual $0.113$ after matching is an upper bound on any real
between-pair structure rather than a measurement of it. Figure~\ref{fig:context} draws the
matched-context arm, with the pairs already inside the window as the band.

$S_{\mathrm{match}}/S_{20} = 0.392$, inside the band specified for ``the context is the dominant
cause'' ($\le 0.5$), and the near-verbatim metric gives $0.399$ on the same arms. Both new arms
land inside their specified $[0.85, 0.96]$, the 1B at its upper edge at $0.959$. Their admission criterions
hold: at $30$ tokens the unconstrained 1B reproduces its target at $0.242$ against its own
control's $0.181$ and the 3B at $0.915$ against $0.924$, so neither arm weakened its memoriser and
the exclusion rule specified for such a case did not fire.

Two things make this more than a restatement of the ranking. First, equalising a variable that
merely \emph{correlates} with an outcome need not shrink the outcome's spread: if granularity acted
on the onset through the vocabulary, the target's length, or the tokenizer's fit to English prose,
lengthening the seed would leave the ratio where it was, because none of those changes. Second, the
mechanism is the one the single-pair arms already showed --- $s(x)$ moves by $0.5$--$0.7\%$ under
the longer seed while $k_{\mathrm{crit}}$ falls about $11\%$, so the seed is starting the target at
a cheaper point on a profile it does not otherwise touch.

What survives is $0.113$ of spread, with the two KL3M pairs still on top at $0.979$ and $0.939$.
The claim scored here is a fraction and not a closure, and the protocol said so before the
numbers existed: $61\%$ of the nine-pair spread is the benchmark's seed convention and $39\%$ is
not.

Split by how many words the adversary holds, the leave-one-out error of a law in $c\,s(x)$ is
$0.070$ nats over the five pairs above ten words and $0.195$ over the four below, against $0.245$
over all nine and $0.364$, $0.563$ and $0.388$ for a constant; the $s(x)$ spans are $1.49\times$,
$1.53\times$ and $1.61\times$, and the ratios run $0.878$--$0.926$ and $0.993$--$1.166$.

Two of the nine rows carry a measured re-seed dispersion, and it changes how the tighter family
reads. Re-training Pleias-1.2B, one of its members, at three seeds gives a standard deviation of
$0.0450$ in the ratio ($5.3\%$), where the family's dispersion \emph{across five different pairs} is
$0.0212$ ($2.4\%$) --- less than half of what one of its own members produces by being trained again,
and over more draws. The family's tightness is therefore not resolved against fine-tune noise, and
the $0.070$ and the exact $p = 0.008$ computed from it treat nine point estimates as carrying no draw
at all. We report them as computed and do not read that family as measurably tighter than the
retraining of its own members.

What survives is the separation, which is what the units claim rests on. The two family means differ
by $0.159$ ($0.901$ against $1.060$); the $\le 10$-word family's dispersion of $0.0748$ ($7.1\%$) is
four times the $0.0180$ ($1.7\%$) that re-training KL3M-520M, one of \emph{its} members, produces;
and the all-nine spread of $0.2874$ sits well above either floor. The split between the families is
larger than the noise; the internal tightness of one side of it is not, and the multiplicity check
inherits that limitation. That check is an exact enumeration, since a five-of-nine subgroup is the
shape a chance finding takes: of the $126$ subsets of size five exactly one --- this one --- has a
coefficient of variation this small, $p = 0.008$, down from $p = 0.018$ over the $56$ subsets of
eight and $p = 0.048$ over the $21$ of seven. It is still not a test of the grouping rule, which was
read off these same measurements. What tests that are the interventions above, which move a single
pair across the boundary in both directions with everything else fixed, and the eighth pair, whose
ratio was predicted from the other seven before its anchor was fine-tuned.
