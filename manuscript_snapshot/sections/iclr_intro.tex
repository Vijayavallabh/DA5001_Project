\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Language models memorise their training data: \texttt{Llama-3.1-70B} reproduces \emph{Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone} almost verbatim
from its first few words \citep{cooper2025extracting}, and
retraining that away is impractical against a stream of takedowns. Certified decoders need no
retraining. They serve a law $q$ that stays within a divergence budget $K$ of a \emph{safe} model $p_s$
trained without the protected work, $k$-near-access-freeness \citep{vyas2023provable}, so a small $K$
bounds how much likelier the served text is to be the work than the safe model's own output. The
deployed instance, anchored decoding \citep{he2026anchored}, charges the divergence per token from a
bucket of rate $k$, certifies a $T$-token response at $K = kT$, and publishes $k$ as a deployer would
publish the $\varepsilon$ of differential privacy.

A budget charged per token grows with the output, and a certificate about a work $x$ says nothing once
$K$ reaches the safe model's surprisal of it, $S(x) = -\log p_s(x)$, at every R\'enyi order
(Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}). Both grow in proportion to length, so vacuity is decided by $k$
against the surprisal rate $s(x)$ and never by $T$ (Figure~\ref{fig:selection}c). At the authors' book
setting, $k=3$ over $200$ tokens, the certificate is vacuous for all $758$ protected passages we test.

\textbf{We spend the budget once, on the draw.} Draw $n$ completions from the safe model, score them
with anything, and serve one (Figure~\ref{fig:selection}b). The served law satisfies
$q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ for every $y$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}): a pathwise certificate of
$\log n$ nats that holds for any scorer, an adversarial one included, and does not depend on the length
of the output. At $n=8$ it is $2.08$ nats where a meter at $k=10$ publishes $2000$. Self-consistency
\citep{wang2023selfconsistency} run over a safe model is an instance.

Against anchored decoding at the authors' own anchor, best-of-$64$ ($\log 64 = 4.16$ nats) is judged
better at every budget whenever the risky model continues text: in an earlier audit's released
configuration, at the authors' $70$B pair, and against their byte-level decoder
(Table~\ref{tab:served}). The difference survives fresh draws and five of six judges
(Figure~\ref{fig:h2h}); the meter wins only where the judge rewards instruction following, at budgets
whose certificate exceeds a $50$-token window's surprisal. With an adversarial scorer, selection's near-verbatim recall is $0.0000$ at every $n \le 64$ and at $n=256$, a bound \emph{relative} to a vetted anchor; with no judge, majority vote over a safe model lifts
GSM8K exact match from $0.320$ to $0.546$ for $3.47$ nats (Figures~\ref{fig:breadth}
and~\ref{fig:safety}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt,parsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{A certificate that does not grow with the output}: pathwise $\log n$ for any selection
rule, score and safe model (Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}), composing to exactly $m\log n$ over $m$
adaptive queries, set against a vacuity threshold $K = S(x)$ that no R\'enyi order moves
(Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}; Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item \textbf{Vacuous, or sparse: why a per-token rate cannot be repaired into one}: a budget that grows with the work is vacuous from $k = s(x)$ on, and a bounded one moves the output law at $O(K/\varepsilon)$ steps (Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}), a bound on the \emph{shape} of a spend and not on every causal policy. The deployed meter shows both horns: extraction begins at $0.88$ to
$1.17$ of $s(x)$ across nine pairs, and past the risky model's imitation rate its spend stops growing
with its cap (Section~\ref{sec:frontier}).
\item \textbf{A head-to-head scoped to what it measured}: better than the meter where the risky model
continues text, worse where the judge rewards instruction following, leakage bounded by the anchor's
own, at $21.8\times$ the meter's decode time unbatched and a loss at matched compute
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}
