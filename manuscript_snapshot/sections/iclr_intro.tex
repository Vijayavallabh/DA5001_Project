\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Language models reproduce passages of their training data, and for copyrighted data that is a legal
exposure rather than a benchmark curiosity: a $70$B open-weights model returns $50$-token windows of
a popular novel on roughly a quarter of prompts drawn from it \citep{cooper2025extracting}, and
retraining it away is impractical against a stream of takedowns. Certified decoders offer something
cheaper: bound, at decode time, how far the output may drift from a \emph{safe} model that
never saw the work. The drift is charged in nats, banked in a token bucket, and published as a
single $k$ --- anchored decoding \citep{he2026anchored} instantiating
$k$-near-access-freeness \citep{vyas2023provable} --- and the appeal is that $k$ looks like
$\varepsilon$ in differential privacy: a dial a deployer sets and a number a rights-holder reads.

\textbf{This paper moves the budget from the token to the draw.} Draw $n$ completions from the safe
model, score them by anything at all, and serve the best. The sampler never departs from the safe
model; what departs is the \emph{choice} among its draws, and that is all
Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} charges. The served law satisfies $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$, a
\emph{pathwise} certificate of exactly $\log n$ nats that bounds every event rather than an
average, sits at the strongest R\'enyi order in the family the deployed meters charge in, and does
not grow with the work where $K = kT$ does. Nothing in it mentions a reward, so self-consistency
\citep{wang2023selfconsistency} run over a safe model is an instance. Run over a web-trained model, as
usual, the bound is relative to a model that saw the work and says nothing about copyright.

\textbf{We keep two comparisons apart.} Against its \emph{own} safe model, best-of-$64$ is judged
better on our workload and on AlpacaEval for a bound of $3.175$ nats, at $n=8$ it beats its own
anchor at four of six anchors in two families, and it lifts exact match with no judge. Against
the \emph{metered decoder} (Figure~\ref{fig:selection}b, Table~\ref{tab:served}) the sign depends on
how the risky model is served: where it continues text, as in an earlier audit's released logs and
at the authors' own $70$B pair, selection is judged better at every budget we ran, by $+0.0655$
$[+0.040, +0.092]$ against the meter's measured $171.3$ nats; where the judge rewards instruction
following, the meter wins, only at budgets that certify nothing about a $50$-token window.

The certificate holds against an \emph{adversarial} scorer, which is what makes it a security
statement: a malicious score can raise the safe model's probability of any event by at most $n$ per
response and $m\log n$ nats over $m$ responses. What it can leak is the anchor's own leakage, amplified: zero near-verbatim recall at every
clean anchor, at $n \le 64$ and at $n=256$, even for an attacker scoring by the memorising model's
own likelihood --- a bound \emph{relative} to a vetted anchor (Ethics Statement), so the zero verifies
the anchor and not the mechanism --- and over twelve deliberately contaminated anchors $1.0$ to $7.0$ of the $64$ permitted on one draw and $1.0$ to
$3.0$ on a second.

\paragraph{Vacuous, or sparse --- and what that does not rule out.} A rate's budget
$K = kT$ grows with the work by construction; the question is whether a meter could be repaired to
spend less where it matters. For any causal policy the sequence divergence is \emph{exactly} the
sum of its per-step divergences, so one held to $K$ departs from the safe model by more than
$\varepsilon$ at no more than $K/\varepsilon$ steps in expectation (Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}).
This is a sparsity bound on the \emph{shape} of a spend, not an impossibility result: a per-token allowance either grows with the
work, so $K/S(x) \to k/s(x)$ never improves, or stays bounded and is spent at $O(1)$ steps. It forbids
\emph{spreading} a bounded budget, as a rate does, not \emph{concentrating} one, as selection does,
and whether a causal policy can concentrate as well is open. The deployed class takes the vacuous
horn, paying wherever its bucket is slack the full cost of \emph{imitating} the risky model
(Proposition~\ref{prop:imitation}). Of the four escapes left --- a sharper charge,
a lower cap, a better schedule, and a budget granted up front, the first and last of which
\citet{he2026anchored} ablate as their $\infty$-R\'enyi and Global variants --- three land on a horn
and the fourth falls short of selection (Section~\ref{sec:orders}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt,parsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{A certificate that does not grow with the work}: pathwise $\log n$ for any selection
rule, score and safe model (Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}), against a vacuity threshold that no
R\'enyi order moves (Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}), and a sparsity bound
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}) explaining why a rate cannot be repaired into one --- not a bound on
every causal policy.
\item \textbf{An audit of anchored decoding at its authors' own anchor and $70$B pair}: extraction
begins within about $0.8$--$1.35$ of $s(x)$ across nine pairs with nine distinct anchors, and
wherever the budget constrains a $50$-token window the decoder is judged indistinguishable from its
safe model.
\item \textbf{A comparison scoped to what it measured} (Table~\ref{tab:served}): better than the
meter when the risky model continues text, worse where the judge rewards instruction following,
leakage bounded by the anchor's own, at $61.3\times$ the meter's forward passes and $21.8\times$ its
wall-clock, and a loss at matched compute.
\end{itemize}
None of this contradicts the mechanisms' theorems --- no per-trajectory violation occurs in
$100{,}000$ budgeted trajectories; the gap is between what they bound and what $k$ is taken to mean.
