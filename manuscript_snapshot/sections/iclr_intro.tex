\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A growing family of inference-time mechanisms promises to bound how much a language model can
reproduce a protected work by metering how far its output distribution may drift from a
\emph{safe} model that never saw the work. The drift is charged in nats, accumulated in a token
bucket, and published as a single number $k$. Anchored decoding~\citep{he2026anchored} is the
current instance; the guarantee it enforces, $k$-near-access-freeness, goes back to
\citet{vyas2023provable}. The appeal is that $k$ looks like $\varepsilon$ in differential privacy:
a dial a deployer sets, a number a rights-holder can read.

This paper argues that $k$ does not carry that meaning, and --- more usefully --- says exactly what
does. Three results, in increasing order of what they cost to establish.

\textbf{The budget has a unit, and it is not nats.} The bound a budget $K$ implies on reproducing a
work $x$ is vacuous once $K$ reaches $S(x)$, the safe model's total surprisal of $x$, and the
threshold is the same for \emph{every} R\'enyi order of the charge
(Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}). Raising the order buys a tighter bound strictly below the
threshold and does not move it, so the failure cannot be repaired by strengthening the accounting.
The quantity that carries risk is therefore $k/s(x)$, not $k$. \citet{vyas2023provable} already
report their budget as a fraction of the safe model's entropy; what is new is an exact threshold at
the realised surprisal of a \emph{specific} work, holding for every order.

\textbf{Almost none of what a budget buys is utility.} A budget is one number, so whatever the
decoder buys with it comes from one account. Donsker--Varadhan at the sequence level gives
$K \ge \Lambda^*_s(\mathbb{E}[U])$, the Cram\'er rate function of the utility under the safe model
(Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl}). Both sides are measurable, and measuring them is what makes the result
useful: the decoder spends $79$ to $171$ nats of realised divergence to buy a utility gain an
optimal policy could buy for hundredths of a nat, a ratio above $10^{3}$ at every budget. The cost
of a metered decoder is therefore approximation overhead rather than information-theoretic
necessity --- what drives a budget past $s(x)$ is the price of imitating the risky model everywhere,
not the price of being better. Empirically the two useful regions still do not overlap: a judge
outside the audit cannot distinguish the decoder from serving its own safe model at $k=1$
($-0.45\sigma$) and can at $k=3$ ($-2.35\sigma$), where the certificate is already vacuous for every
protected passage.

\textbf{Extraction begins at a fixed fraction of that threshold.} On three pairs sharing no anchor,
tokenizer or risky model, and spanning $1.49\times$ in $s(x)$, leakage first becomes measurable at
$0.887$, $0.892$ and $0.895$ of $s(x)$ --- a standard deviation of $0.003$, with the onset spanning
the same $1.49\times$ as the threshold it tracks. The decoding geometry suggests why the constant
might not be constant: at full tilt the charge along a target reduces to $r(x) = s_s(x) - s_r(x)$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:req}), which predicts the ratio should move with how thoroughly the risky model
memorised the work. We pre-registered that prediction on a held-out pair and the measurement
rejects it. Proportionality to $s(x)$ is what the evidence supports; the refinement is not.

Read together these say something a deployer can act on. The three rates that matter --- the
surprisal rate $s(x)$, the requirement $r(x)$ that governs leakage, and the rate
$c_{\mathrm{use}}$ at which ordinary traffic diverges --- are properties of the models and the
content, computable before any mechanism is built. Measured across ten safe models and three
independently curated, openly licensed corpora, the separation between the rate that must be permitted and
the rate that must be forbidden \emph{widens} as safe models improve (Section~\ref{sec:scaling}),
correcting a claim in an earlier audit of this mechanism~\citep{vijayavallabh2026audit} that read
one side of that two-sided quantity and inverted the trend.

None of this contradicts the mechanisms' own theorems: across more than $100{,}000$ budgeted
trajectories here and in the audits this builds on, no per-trajectory violation occurs. The gap is
between what the theorems bound and what the published number is taken to mean.
