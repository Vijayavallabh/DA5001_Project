\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Language models reproduce passages of their training data, and for copyrighted data that is a legal
exposure rather than a benchmark curiosity: a $70$B open-weights model returns $50$-token windows of
a popular novel on roughly a quarter of prompts drawn from it \citep{cooper2025extracting}, and
retraining it away is impractical against a stream of takedown requests. Inference-time mechanisms
offer something cheaper: bound, at decode time, how far the output may drift from a \emph{safe} model
that never saw the work. The drift is charged in nats, accumulated in a token bucket, and published
as a single number $k$; anchored decoding \citep{he2026anchored} is the current instance and the
guarantee it enforces, $k$-near-access-freeness, goes back to \citet{vyas2023provable}. The appeal is
that $k$ looks like $\varepsilon$ in differential privacy: a dial a deployer sets and a number a
rights-holder can read.

\textbf{This paper shows the design is closed.} The obstruction is not that the budget is too loose,
that the divergence is the wrong one, or that the decoder schedules its spending badly. It is that a
budget charged per token cannot be both meaningful and useful, and the reason is one line of the
chain rule. For any autoregressive policy $q$ the sequence divergence is \emph{exactly} the sum of
its per-step divergences, so a policy holding to a sequence budget $K$ can depart from the safe model
by more than $\varepsilon$ at no more than $K/\varepsilon$ steps in expectation
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}). Every per-token mechanism therefore lands on one of two horns:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Either} the allowance grows with the work --- $K = kT$ against a work worth $S(x) = s(x)T$, so
$K/S(x) \to k/s(x)$ and never improves --- \emph{or} it is bounded and the decoder is the safe model
at all but $O(1)$ of its steps. Vacuous, or trivial.
\end{quote}

The first horn is not a loose bound a better analysis could tighten: the bound a budget $K$ implies
on emitting $x$ is empty exactly at $K = S(x)$, identically for every R\'enyi order of the charge
(Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}), so what carries risk is $k/s(x)$ and never $k$. Across nine model
pairs with nine distinct anchors and six tokenizers, extraction becomes measurable between $0.88$ and
$1.17$ of $s(x)$, on two pairs only after the certificate has expired (Section~\ref{sec:onset}).

\paragraph{The deployed class takes the vacuous horn, and takes it by construction}
A token-bucket decoder serving the largest feasible tilt spends, on every step where the bucket is
slack, the full $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_r \| p_s)$ of that step --- the cost of \emph{imitating} the risky
model, which is $\Theta(T)$ and does not depend on the utility achieved
(Proposition~\ref{prop:imitation}). Over $16{,}500$ logged trajectories that is what happens: the
realised spend stalls at $171.3$ nats while the cap quadruples to $4000$, and the budget above the
pair's own imitation rate is unreachable. The three repairs one would try next are corollaries of the
dichotomy rather than open questions, and all three fail where it says they must
(Section~\ref{sec:orders}). \emph{Sharpening the charge} cannot move a threshold that is
order-invariant --- yet the four orders we built span two orders of magnitude in what an adversary
recovers at one published budget. \emph{Lowering the budget} walks onto the trivial horn: at $k=0.5$,
inside the region where the certificate still covers all $758$ protected passages, neither of two
judges can distinguish the decoder from the safe model it wraps, and both do only at $k \ge 10$, where
it covers none. \emph{Scheduling better} is bounded by the same chain rule: optimal offline
reallocation of the decoder's own realised spend buys $3$ to $13\%$ of a gap four orders of magnitude
wide. They share a cause that is measurable: the currency a metered decoder buys with its nats
predicts judged quality worse than the answer's length does (Section~\ref{sec:orders}).

\paragraph{The escape is a place, not a rate}
Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse} closes the per-step axis but says nothing about a budget charged
somewhere the chain rule does not reach. Draw $n$ completions from the safe model, score them by
anything at all, and serve the best: every token emitted is still drawn from $p_s$, so the per-step
sum is zero, and the whole budget is spent once, on \emph{which draw to serve}. The resulting law
satisfies $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ for any score and any tie rule, a \emph{pathwise} certificate of
exactly $\log n$ nats --- the strongest order in the family, and constant in the length of the work.
On the workload where the metered decoder spends $171.3$ nats to become measurably better than its
own safe model, the best of eight draws costs $2.08$, reaches the same judged utility, admits $332$
composed queries against $2$, and reproduces none of the protected passages at any $n$ we tried, even
when an adversary picks the scorer.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\textbf{A dichotomy for per-token certificates.} The chain rule makes a causal policy's sequence
budget the sum of its per-step budgets, so an affordable policy is the safe model almost everywhere
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}), while the deployed rule is its opposite and pays $\Theta(T)$ for
imitation regardless of utility (Proposition~\ref{prop:imitation}). Beside the order-invariant
vacuity threshold at $K = S(x)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}) and the fact that a bounded
utility costs only $O(1)$ nats however long the sequence (Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl}), this says a
certificate and a useful mechanism are not intrinsically in conflict --- a per-token meter is what
puts them there.

\textbf{The measurements that place the deployed class on the vacuous horn.} Both factors of the
bound are read off per-step logs rather than assumed: the binding fraction falls from $0.954$ at
$k=0.1$ to $0.0000$ at $k=20$ while the rate it multiplies saturates at $0.857$ nats per token, and
the running spend is linear in the step index at every budget. Nine pairs locate extraction at
$0.88$--$1.17$ of $s(x)$; the residual variation is not a property of the models but tracks the
\emph{words} a fixed-token seed hands the adversary ($\rho = -0.958$, exact $p = 0.0002$), and
equalising it removes $61\%$ of the spread --- a caution for anyone who fixes a seed in tokens.

\textbf{A mechanism on the other side of the dichotomy.} Selection anchoring is what the theorem
leaves, and it delivers: the metered decoder's best judged utility at $1/142$ of its divergence,
under the strongest order rather than the weakest, at four anchors in three families, with zero
near-verbatim recall at any $n \le 64$
(Sections~\ref{sec:selection}--\ref{sec:experiments}). None of this contradicts the mechanisms' own
theorems: across more than $100{,}000$ budgeted trajectories no per-trajectory violation occurs,
and the gap is between what the theorems bound and what the published number is taken to mean.
