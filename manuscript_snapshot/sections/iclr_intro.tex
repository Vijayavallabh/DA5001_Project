\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Language models memorise their training data: \texttt{Llama-3.1-70B} reproduces \emph{Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone} almost verbatim
from its first few words \citep{cooper2025extracting}, and
retraining that away is impractical against a stream of takedowns. Certified decoders need no
retraining. They serve a law $q$ that stays within a divergence budget $K$ of a \emph{safe} model $p_s$
trained without the protected work, $k$-near-access-freeness \citep{vyas2023provable}, so a small $K$
bounds how much likelier the served text is to be the work than the safe model's own output. The
deployed instance, anchored decoding \citep{he2026anchored}, charges the divergence per token from a
bucket of rate $k$, certifies a $T$-token response at $K = kT$, and publishes $k$ as a deployer would
publish the $\varepsilon$ of differential privacy.

We ask what such a budget excludes. A certificate about a work $x$ says nothing once $K$ reaches the safe
model's surprisal of it, $S(x) = -\log p_s(x)$, and this threshold is the same at every R\'enyi order
(Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}). A budget charged per token grows with the output, so for a work served
at its own length vacuity is decided by $k$ against the surprisal rate $s(x)$
(Figure~\ref{fig:selection}c), and a $50$-token window of a $200$-token response is exposed from
$k = s(x)/4$. At the authors' book setting, $k=3$ over $200$ tokens, $K = 600$ nats and the certificate is
vacuous for all $758$ protected passages we test, whose median surprisal is $204.8$ nats.

\textbf{A budget that does not grow.} Draw $n$ completions from the safe model, score them with
anything, and serve one (Figure~\ref{fig:selection}b). The served law satisfies $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ for
every $y$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}): a pathwise certificate of $\log n$ nats that holds for any
scorer, an adversarial one included, whatever the length of the output, and adds up to $m \log n$ over $m$
queries; at $n=8$ it is $2.08$ nats where the authors' meter publishes $600$, and self-consistency
\citep{wang2023selfconsistency} run over a safe model is an instance. Selection never consults the risky
model, so it is no substitute for anchored decoding but a certified way to improve a licensed model, and it
cannot supply a capability that model lacks. What separates the two is what the budget
buys: a choice among the safe model's own draws, or the risky model's tokens, whose divergence from the
safe model a meter pays on every token it imitates, about $40$ nats for a $50$-token window of ordinary
text (Section~\ref{sec:frontier}).

\textbf{At a matched certificate}, where two certificates can be compared, selection and selection in
installments beat every meter we ran: anchored decoding at $k=0.5$, the one budget whose certificate still
says anything about a $50$-token window, is judged indistinguishable from its safe model, which best-of-$64$
($\log 64 = 4.16$ nats) improves on, and a meter that caps every $50$-token window, the design our
dichotomy leaves open, trails selection up to $40$ nats a window (Table~\ref{tab:matched}); where the risky
model is far stronger, served as a chat assistant, it wins, but it certifies only the exact window, and
at $125$ nats, where both judges prefer it, it leaks near-verbatim text. At larger budgets anchored decoding serves the risky model, and
which of the two is judged better depends on how that model is served. With an adversarial scorer selection's near-verbatim recall is
$0.0000$ at every $n \le 64$ and at $n=256$, a bound \emph{relative} to a vetted anchor (Figure~\ref{fig:safety}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt,parsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{What a budget certifies}: nothing about an event of surprisal $S \le K$, at every R\'enyi order
(Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}); for a whole-sequence per-token budget, vacuity if it grows and sparsity
if it is bounded (Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}), and the imitation cost a meter pays on every token it imitates
(Proposition~\ref{prop:imitation}), two short consequences of the chain rule; and for anchored decoding,
extraction from $0.88$ to $1.17$ of $s(x)$ over nine pairs, where its certificate is already void.
\item \textbf{A certificate that does not grow with the output}: pathwise $\log n$ for any selection
rule and score (Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}), $m\log n$ over $m$ adaptive queries, with what it does not
buy priced: the query horizon, the per-work audit and the vetted anchor (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item \textbf{Matched-certificate head-to-heads} under two judges, the windowed meter included,
with the risky-model comparisons apart and the cost, $21.8\times$ a metered decode and a loss
at matched compute.
\end{itemize}
