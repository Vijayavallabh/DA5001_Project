\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Language models reproduce passages of their training data, and for copyrighted data that is a legal
exposure rather than a benchmark curiosity: a $70$B open-weights model returns $50$-token windows of
a popular novel on roughly a quarter of prompts drawn from it \citep{cooper2025extracting}, and
retraining it away is impractical against a stream of takedown requests. Inference-time mechanisms
offer something cheaper: bound, at decode time, how far the output may drift from a \emph{safe} model
that never saw the work. The drift is charged in nats, accumulated in a token bucket, and published
as a single number $k$ --- anchored decoding \citep{he2026anchored} is the current instance of
$k$-near-access-freeness \citep{vyas2023provable} --- and the appeal is that $k$ looks like
$\varepsilon$ in differential privacy: a dial a deployer sets and a number a rights-holder reads.

\textbf{This paper shows the budget is in the wrong place, and moves it.} Draw $n$ completions from
the safe model, score them by anything at all, and serve the best. Every token emitted is still a
safe-model token, so there is nothing to meter at any step; the whole budget is spent once, on
\emph{which draw to serve}. The served law then satisfies $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ for any score and any
tie rule --- three lines, Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} --- and that inequality is a
\emph{pathwise} certificate of exactly $\log n$ nats. It bounds every event rather than an average,
it is the strongest R\'enyi order in the family the deployed meters charge in, and it is constant in
the length of the work where a per-token allowance $K = kT$ is linear in it. Nothing in it mentions a
reward, so self-consistency is an instance: the most widely deployed inference-time method in the
field already carries a copyright certificate, and a better one than the mechanism built for the
purpose.

That comparison is the paper's headline and it is not close. On the workload where a metered decoder
spends a measured $171.3$ nats of realised divergence to become judged-better than its own safe
model, eight draws cost $2.08$ and sixty-four cost $3.175$ --- and win, $+0.142$ against
$+0.072$: twice the utility for one fifty-fourth of the divergence, at four anchors in three
families spanning a $6\times$ range in parameters, on AlpacaEval's $805$ instructions, and on exact
match with no judge in the loop at all, where majority vote takes GSM8K accuracy from $0.320$ to
$0.546$. Put as the promise a rights-holder can actually be given --- the factor by which the
mechanism may raise the safe model's own chance of emitting their work --- it is $8$, against
$e^{2000}$.

\paragraph{The guarantee holds against the adversary, which is what makes it a security claim}
Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} assumes nothing about the score, so a \emph{malicious} one can
multiply the safe model's own probability of emitting a protected work by at most $n$ and never
introduce one that model would not have produced. So we hand the selector to the attacker. Scoring
the anchor's draws by the \emph{memorising} model's own likelihood, on the passages it was
fine-tuned to reproduce, recovers $0.0000$ at every $n$ up to $64$ and on every one of $100$
passages, against that model's own $0.3925$ --- and likewise where the memoriser is not ours to
build, against a \texttt{Llama-3.1-70B} that memorised \emph{Harry Potter} in pre-training, recovers
$0.2475$ unaided and reproduces two passages of fifty \emph{in full}. Where the premise does fail
the bound degrades gracefully: across twelve deliberately contaminated anchors the realised
amplification at $n=64$ runs between $1.0$ and $4.0$, against the $64$ the certificate permits.

\paragraph{Why this was available: a per-token budget cannot be repaired to match it}
The natural objection is that a meter could be fixed --- charge more sharply, cap more tightly,
schedule more cleverly. It cannot, and the reason is one line of the chain rule. For any causal
policy the sequence divergence is \emph{exactly} the sum of its per-step divergences, so a policy
holding to a sequence budget $K$ can depart from the safe model by more than $\varepsilon$ at no more
than $K/\varepsilon$ steps in expectation (Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}). Every per-token mechanism
therefore lands on one of two horns:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Either} the allowance grows with the work --- $K = kT$ against a work worth $S(x) = s(x)T$, so
$K/S(x) \to k/s(x)$ and never improves --- \emph{or} it is bounded and the decoder is the safe model
at all but $O(1)$ of its steps. Vacuous, or trivial.
\end{quote}

The chain rule forbids \emph{spreading} a bounded budget, not \emph{concentrating} one, and that
distinction is the whole gap selection anchoring walks through. It also explains why the gap was
still open: the deployed class takes the vacuous horn by construction, because a token-bucket
decoder serving the largest feasible tilt spends, wherever the bucket is slack, the full
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_r \| p_s)$ of that step --- the cost of \emph{imitating} the risky model, $\Theta(T)$
and independent of the utility achieved (Proposition~\ref{prop:imitation}). Over $16{,}500$ logged
trajectories realised spend stalls at $171.3$ nats while the cap quadruples to $4000$. The three repairs are then corollaries rather than open questions, and
Section~\ref{sec:orders} pre-registers and fails all three, each in a different place: a sharper
charge cannot move a threshold that is order-invariant, a smaller cap walks onto the trivial horn
where two judges cannot separate the decoder from the safe model it wraps, and a better schedule is
bounded by the same chain rule. They share a measurable cause --- the currency a metered decoder
buys with its nats predicts judged quality at AUC $0.526$, worse than the answer's length at
$0.537$.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\textbf{A copyright certificate that does not grow with the work.} A selection rule's whole budget
is a pathwise $\log n$ under any score, any tie rule and any safe model
(Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}), read against a vacuity threshold at $K = S(x)$ that does not
move with the R\'enyi order (Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}): $2.08$ nats at $n=8$ against a work
worth $849$, and $332$ composed queries under a per-user odometer against $2$.

\textbf{It works, and it survives an adversarial scorer.} Twice the metered decoder's judged utility
for one fifty-fourth of its divergence, at four anchors in three families, on AlpacaEval, and on two
judge-free tasks where the ranking of scoring rules reverses --- which bounds the claim as well as
making it --- with zero near-verbatim recall at every $n \le 64$ (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).

\textbf{Why no per-token budget can reach it.} Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse} bounds an affordable
causal policy's departures from the safe model while the deployed rule is its opposite
(Proposition~\ref{prop:imitation}), and bounded utility costs $O(1)$ nats however long the sequence
(Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl}): the conflict is the per-token axis, not the certificate. Both factors are
read off per-step logs, and nine pairs with nine distinct anchors locate extraction at $0.88$--$1.17$
of $s(x)$, tying the threshold to something a rights-holder would recognise. None of this
contradicts the mechanisms' own theorems --- across more than $100{,}000$ budgeted trajectories no
per-trajectory violation occurs --- and the gap is between what they bound and what the published
number is taken to mean.
