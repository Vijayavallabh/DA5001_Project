\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Language models memorise their training data: \texttt{Llama-3.1-70B} reproduces \emph{Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone} almost verbatim
from its first few words \citep{cooper2025extracting}, and
retraining that away is impractical against a stream of takedowns. Certified decoders need no
retraining. They serve a law $q$ that stays within a divergence budget $K$ of a \emph{safe} model $p_s$
trained without the protected work, $k$-near-access-freeness \citep{vyas2023provable}, so a small $K$
bounds how much likelier the served text is to be the work than the safe model's own output. The
deployed instance, anchored decoding \citep{he2026anchored}, charges the divergence per token from a
bucket of rate $k$, certifies a $T$-token response at $K = kT$, and publishes $k$ as a deployer would
publish the $\varepsilon$ of differential privacy.

A budget charged per token grows with the output, and a certificate about a work $x$ says nothing once
$K$ reaches the safe model's surprisal of it, $S(x) = -\log p_s(x)$, at every R\'enyi order
(Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}). For a work served at its own length both grow with it, so vacuity is
decided by $k$ against the surprisal rate $s(x)$ (Figure~\ref{fig:selection}c), and a $50$-token window
of a $200$-token response is exposed from $k = s(x)/4$. At the authors' book setting, $k=3$ over $200$
tokens, $K = 600$ nats and the certificate is vacuous for all $758$ protected passages we test, whose
median surprisal is $204.8$ nats.

\textbf{We spend the budget on the draw.} Draw $n$ completions from the safe model, score them with
anything, and serve one (Figure~\ref{fig:selection}b). The served law satisfies $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ for
every $y$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}): a pathwise certificate of $\log n$ nats that holds for any
scorer, an adversarial one included, whatever the length of the output. At $n=8$ it is $2.08$ nats where
the authors' meter publishes $600$. Self-consistency \citep{wang2023selfconsistency} run over a safe model
is an instance. What separates the two is what the budget buys: a choice among the safe model's own
draws, or the risky model's tokens, whose divergence from the safe model a meter pays on every token it
imitates, about $40$ nats for a $50$-token window of ordinary text (Section~\ref{sec:frontier}).

Against anchored decoding at the authors' own anchor, the one budget whose certificate says anything
about a $50$-token window, $k=0.5$, leaves the meter judged indistinguishable from its safe model, which
best-of-$64$ ($\log 64 = 4.16$ nats) improves on; at larger budgets the meter is the risky model, and
best-of-$64$ is judged better than that model continuing text at temperature $1.0$, with its Qwen scorer though not with a gemma one, and worse than it at the authors' $0.7$ or served as a chat assistant or following instructions (Table~\ref{tab:served}, Figure~\ref{fig:h2h}). With an adversarial scorer selection's
near-verbatim recall is $0.0000$ at every $n \le 64$ and at $n=256$, a bound \emph{relative} to a vetted anchor; with no
judge, majority vote over a safe model lifts GSM8K exact match from $0.320$ to $0.546$ for $3.47$ nats
(Figures~\ref{fig:breadth} and~\ref{fig:safety}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt,parsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{A certificate that does not grow with the output}: pathwise $\log n$ for any selection
rule and score (Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}), $m\log n$ over $m$ adaptive queries, against a vacuity
threshold exact at every R\'enyi order (Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}; Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item \textbf{What a per-token rate can certify}: vacuous from $k = s(x)$ if it grows with the
sequence, sparse if bounded (Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}), and the imitation cost on every token a meter
imitates (Proposition~\ref{prop:imitation}), tens of nats a window even for a windowed meter
(Section~\ref{sec:frontier}).
\item \textbf{A scoped head-to-head}: where the meter certifies anything selection matches or beats it; elsewhere the sign depends on how the risky model is served and decoded; selection costs $21.8\times$ a
metered decode served from the anchor alone and loses at matched compute (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}
