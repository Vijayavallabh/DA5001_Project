\section{Selection anchoring: protocol, pre-registration and diagnostics}\label{app:selection}

\paragraph{The strongest anchor at the largest $n$, and the check that stopped it being a headline}
Comma-7B was swept to $n=64$ on the same $500$ ordinary prompts, the same reward, the same two
judges and the same opponent, bands committed first
(\texttt{results/onset\_prediction\_selection\_n64\_comma7b.md}). Within the pass the gain rises
monotonically --- $+0.041$, $+0.060$, $+0.072$, $+0.103$, $+0.127$, $+0.173$ at $n = 2,4,8,16,32,64$
on the registered scorer and $+0.062$ to $+0.230$ on the second, Spearman $+1.000$ in $\log n$ in
both --- so the mechanism is still climbing at $n=64$ at the strongest anchor, for $3.17$ nats of
measured KL against a $\log 64 = 4.16$ pathwise certificate, and the reward overoptimisation that
turns such curves over is not observed anywhere on the grid.

The arm's own nested check, however, **failed**: its $n=8$ gain of $+0.072$ sits $0.039$ from the
breadth arm's $+0.111$ on the registered scorer, past the $0.03$ the pre-registration allowed, so by
its own committed rule the comparison against the earlier number is \emph{not readable} and the
abstract is unchanged. The band's premise was that the two arms shared generations; they did not,
and the discrepancy is the cross-pass floor of Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} rather than a scoring
fault. Rewriting a band around a premise found wrong after the number is what the register exists to
prevent, so it stands as written.

\paragraph{Candidates}
The $500$ ordinary prompts are the neutral, creative and factual classes of the same workload every
other arm in this paper uses, served raw under the harness's ``Complete the prefix:'' header at
temperature $1$ with a $200$-token cap. Candidates are the $k=0$ arm of the ordinary decoding
harness at $n$ trajectories per prompt, so they are anchor samples produced by the same code path as
every other anchor-only arm, and trajectory seeds are $(h \ll 16) \mid j$ for $j < n$: arm $n$ is
exactly the first $n$ candidates in seed order, the arms nest, and no candidate is scored twice. The
opponent in every judged comparison is the \emph{unconstrained} risky model's completion on the same
prompt at its lowest seed --- one fixed opponent per prompt, so every candidate of that prompt is
judged against the same text.

\paragraph{Why the $n=1$ control is the entry gate}
Selection at $n=1$ is the anchor served alone, which every arm of Section~\ref{sec:orders} already
measures. Under judge~A it reads $u = 0.319$ $[0.281, 0.358]$ against the $u_{\text{safe}} = 0.323$
on record from a different run, a different sample and a different judging pass. A pipeline that
mis-assembles prompts, mis-parses verdicts or mixes up the presentation order would not reproduce
that number, so the control is what licenses reading the refutation at $n=8$ as a refutation rather
than as a bug --- which matters, because the committed arm failed.

\paragraph{How the AUC diagnostic is computed}
The claim that the risky model's likelihood is a poor guide to judged quality is measured
\emph{within} prompt, never across prompts, because across prompts the comparison is confounded by
how hard each prompt is. For each prompt we form all pairs of its eight candidates whose judge~A
verdicts differ, giving $5{,}687$ ordered pairs over the $500$ prompts, and ask how often the
candidate the judge preferred also has the higher score. That fraction is the AUC. Three scores are
evaluated on the identical pair set: the risky model's per-token mean log-likelihood ($0.526$), its
summed log-likelihood ($0.477$) and the completion's length in tokens ($0.537$). A score that
carried no information would read $0.500$; the summed form reads \emph{below} chance because it is a
length preference with the sign the judge disagrees with, which is the same pathology the served-token
column of Table~\ref{tab:selection} shows directly ($98 \to 15$ tokens).

\paragraph{The composition arithmetic}
A per-user odometer at $B_{\text{user}} = 400$ nats --- half the anchor's median surprisal of a
protected passage, $849$ nats --- is the accounting an earlier audit of this mechanism uses to bound
what one user can accumulate across queries \citep{vijayavallabh2026audit}, and it is where a budget
that is not spent per token pays off twice. The metered decoder at $k=3$ spends a measured mean of
$165.0$ nats on a $200$-token response (\texttt{results/utility\_price.csv}), so a user exhausts
$400$ nats after $2$ queries; at $k=10$ the figure is $171.3$ and the answer is still $2$. Selection
at $n=8$ spends $\log 8 - 7/8 = 1.204$ nats whatever the response length, so the same user may issue
$332$. The spend of a selection rule does not depend on how long the response is, which is the
composition consequence of the same asymmetry Section~\ref{sec:selection} states for the
certificate.

\paragraph{The pre-registration record}
Bands, grid, entry gate, primary metric and the excluded alternatives were committed to the
repository before anything was generated, in \texttt{results/onset\_prediction\_selection.md} and
\texttt{results/onset\_prediction\_selection\_scaling.md}. The primary arm's band was: at $n=8$,
judged utility of at least $0.469$ means the mechanism works, $0.396$ to $0.469$ partial, below
$0.396$ refuted. It scored $0.313$ --- refuted, and by a margin that no reading of the band rescues.
The extraction band was near-verbatim recall at most $0.005$ at $n=64$; it scored $0.0000$. The
cross-judge follow-up was committed separately, after the primary arm had been scored and before the
second judge was run, with bands of at least $+0.244$ for a real capacity, $+0.10$ to $+0.244$ for a
partial one and below $+0.10$ for an artefact of the selecting judge; it scored $+0.081$, so the
recorded verdict is \textsc{artefact} --- the oracle's $+0.488$ was largely circular --- while the
surviving $+0.081$ has a paired interval excluding zero and is reported as such. Four alternatives
were excluded in advance and none was adopted afterwards: scoring the oracle against a band,
re-fitting a band after seeing a result, quoting the best $n$ rather than the committed one, and
changing the scoring template between arms.

\paragraph{What this does and does not establish}
It does not recommend any particular scorer. A judge is not a deployment's utility, $+0.081$ is a
small effect measured once, and the mechanism runs a second model $n$ times per response. What it
establishes is narrower and is the thing Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl}'s gap alone could not: the
impossibility --- that no budget certifies every protected work and also buys a detectable
improvement --- is a property of \emph{metering per token}, not of budgeted decoding. At $k=10$ the
metered decoder's $2{,}000$ nats cover none of the works; $\log 8 = 2.08$ nats cover all of them
with four hundred times the margin to spare, and reach the same judged utility.
