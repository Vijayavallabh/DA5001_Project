\paragraph{The scorer, and the one that failed.}\label{app:currency}
The deployed reward is $\log p(\text{Yes}) - \log p(\text{No})$ from \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct} on one
fixed template; it never consults the risky model. The first scorer we specified was the risky model's own
per-token log-likelihood, the objective anchored decoding tilts toward. At $n=8$ it moves judged utility
from $0.319$ to $0.313$, $-0.006$ against a band of $+0.077$; selecting by the judge's own verdict among the
same eight draws reaches $+0.488$ (circular, an upper bound), and selecting by judge~A while scoring with
judge~B $+0.081$ $[+0.034, +0.130]$ (order-averaging it leaves $+0.0405$
$[+0.019, +0.063]$), so the utility is in the draws and the likelihood does not find it.
Within a prompt, over $5{,}687$ pairs the judge placed differently, the per-token likelihood is concordant
with the judge on $0.526$ $[0.504, 0.548]$, the summed likelihood on $0.478$ $[0.454, 0.500]$ and the
completion's length on $0.537$ $[0.514, 0.560]$: no better than length, and not worse, the difference being
$+0.011$ $[-0.023, +0.046]$ (\texttt{results/\allowbreak currency\_\allowbreak auc.csv}).

\paragraph{The scorer prefers an empty completion.}\label{app:empties}
On the recovered text $13.9\%$ of the headline pool's $32{,}000$ draws are empty, and the reward scores them
above the rest (median $-18.75$ against $-26.12$), so best-of-$n$ serves an empty text on $12.8\%$ of prompts
at $n=1$, $21.8\%$ at $n=8$ and $8.2\%$ at $n=64$. The judge does not score those as losses, and at $n \ge 8$
the preference costs selection level rather than flattering it (prompts served empty are judged $0.4531$, $0.4771$ and $0.4939$ at $n=1$, $8$ and $64$, against $0.4536$, $0.5211$ and $0.5605$ for the rest). Excluding empty candidates from the argmax raises the
fresh-seed headline difference from $+0.0495$ to $+0.0695$ $[+0.0385, +0.0995]$
(\texttt{results/\allowbreak empty\_\allowbreak preference.csv}). On the repaired headline pass (post hoc, no band),
scoring every empty served text as a loss moves the difference from $+0.0505$ to $+0.0155$
$[-0.0245, +0.0555]$: selection serves $41$ empty texts at $n=64$ and the meter none, against $64$ and $62$
for the two anchor controls. Serving the best \emph{non-empty} draw, a selection rule over the same $n$
draws and so certified at the same $\log n$, changes $41$ picks; judged as the judge scores them it leaves
the difference at $+0.0505$, since the judge rates those $41$ replacements as it rated the empty texts,
and with empty answers as losses it keeps $+0.056$ $[+0.0195, +0.093]$, the served levels differing by
$+0.0655$ $[+0.039, +0.0915]$ (\texttt{results/\allowbreak empty\_\allowbreak as\_\allowbreak loss.csv},
\texttt{results/\allowbreak nonempty\_\allowbreak rule.csv}). Every GPU was held by another job when the
$41$ replacements were judged, so judge~B ran on CPU in float32; re-judging the $41$ committed picks the
same way reproduces their committed levels on $37$.

\paragraph{The judge.}\label{app:judgemethod}
Every judged number compares an arm's served text with the fixed opponent's on the same prompt. The judge
receives one user turn, in its own chat template:
\begin{quote}\footnotesize\ttfamily\raggedright
You are grading two candidate responses to the same instruction.\\
Instruction: \{prompt\}\quad Response A: \{a\}\quad Response B: \{b\}\\
Which response is the better answer to the instruction? Judge helpfulness, coherence and factual
accuracy. Ignore length and formatting. If they are of equal quality, answer Tie. Reply with
exactly one word: A, B, or Tie.
\end{quote}
Fields are truncated to $1{,}200$ characters, the prompt is the corpus prompt without the harness header,
decoding is greedy with at most four new tokens, and the verdict is the first letter (anything else a
tie). The cut reaches $19$ of the opponent's $500$ texts, $20$ of the meter's at $k=10$, $2$ of selection's and
no prompt; shown whole, the headline difference reads $+0.062$ $[+0.0265, +0.0975]$ against $+0.059$ cut, on
the same machine, and $+0.0217$ $[-0.0517, +0.0933]$ on the biographies alone
(\texttt{results/\allowbreak full\_\allowbreak response.csv}, registered). An order-averaged score shows the item in both orders and averages the two
(\texttt{analysis/\allowbreak order\_\allowbreak averaged\_\allowbreak h2h.py}). \textbf{Position dominates a
single order} \citep{zheng2023judging}: the same two texts win $261$ of $500$ shown second and $24$ shown
first, only $29.2\%$ of items get a consistent verdict, the first slot wins $12.7\%$ of decided
comparisons, and a single-order level moves when other arms join
the sweep (the audited anchor's $n=64$ gain read $+0.142$ and $+0.076$ on byte-identical text). Order
averaging draws nothing and reproduced exactly, $+0.1045$ $[+0.0820, +0.1280]$ both times, which is why every
head-to-head here is order-averaged and single-order gains are read only within one sweep. The correction
is asymmetric: read in one order against the order-averaged construction, selection at $n=64$ moves
$+0.110$ to $+0.1045$ and the meter at $k=10$ moves $+0.097$ to $+0.0400$, so the paired difference moves
$+0.013$ to $+0.0645$. We had predicted the opposite, both arms deflating together, and it was wrong; the
likely reason, offered rather than claimed, is that at $k=10$ the meter serves $p_r$ nearly unchanged,
leaving the judge two near-identical texts and little but slot order. The same arm found a defect behind
earlier numbers: both judging scripts showed the judge a prompt reconstructed as the served text minus the
generation, whose split point moves with how much each arm generated, so for the same item three arms
disagreed about it in $455$ of $500$ cases. Every judged number here takes the prompt from the corpus, and
under one true prompt the single-order difference is $+0.013$, not the $+0.070$ the arm-specific prompts
implied. A judged gain
re-run on a different $n$-grid moves by $0.04$ to $0.07$; the head-to-head escapes that floor by being
paired within one pass. Judge~C is the opponent's own checkpoint, \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}, a self-preference risk
\citep{panickssery2024llm}.

\paragraph{A recording defect, and its repair.}
The harness recorded each generation by slicing a left-padded batch row at that row's own prompt length,
so a row with $p$ pad tokens carried the last $p$ tokens of its prompt into the recorded text: the
opponent's and the anchor's texts on $73.6\%$ of headline prompts (mean $10.7$ characters), the $k=10$
meter's on $32.6\%$. Nothing served was affected, but the judge and the reward read the record. The slicing
is fixed and every recorded generation is recovered exactly as the decoded row minus its prompt. Re-judged
on the recovered text with nothing else changed, the headline difference moves from $+0.0645$
$[+0.0300, +0.0995]$ to $+0.0505$ $[+0.0155, +0.0860]$, and all twenty older order-averaged differences keep their sign class; of the forty single-arm gains beneath them three change class, selection's on MT-Bench ($+0.0375$ $[-0.0125, +0.0875]$) and CoTaEval-QA ($+0.0070$ $[-0.0165, +0.0300]$) now covering zero and the meter's on the public books falling below it ($-0.0275$ $[-0.0470, -0.0080]$) (\texttt{results/\allowbreak deecho\_\allowbreak rejudge.csv}).

\paragraph{Pre-registration.}
Every band this paper scores against was committed before the run it scores, with its grid, admission
criterion, stop rule and excluded outcomes; each registration and its scoring log are in the repository
(\texttt{results/\allowbreak onset\_\allowbreak prediction\_\allowbreak *.md}). Predictions that failed are reported as
results: the first scorer, the judge-C prediction, the $70$B pair's expected wins for the meter, the
CoTaEval turnover, the adversarial oracle on CoTaEval's infringement half, and the monotone vetting ladder.
Figure~\ref{fig:h2h}'s NewsQA row is registered, its $k=1.4$ fixed by a registered calibration rule before
any judge call. Read as one family of $21$ under Holm's correction, each $p$-value taken from its row's
interval, eight of Figure~\ref{fig:h2h}'s rows still exclude zero, seven in selection's favour and
AlpacaEval against it; the headline row alone ($p \approx 0.005$) does not survive a $21$-way correction
(\texttt{results/\allowbreak forest\_\allowbreak multiplicity.csv}, post hoc).

\section{The head-to-head in full}\label{app:sel-h2h}

\paragraph{By serving configuration.}\label{app:served}
Table~\ref{tab:served} comes from two registered arms, banded before any judge call, and one descriptive
re-judge that puts every budget in one pass; all three judge the same selection texts, whose per-prompt
levels agree across them on $500$ of $500$ prompts. Served through its chat template the $8$B was generated
afresh at $k=10$; its own second draw reads $0.504$ against the first, and it beats its templateless text by
$+0.184$ $[+0.155, +0.213]$. Against that stronger opponent selection still beats the committed
(templateless) meter, $+0.058$ $[+0.0325, +0.0845]$. At the $70$B pair we predicted the meter would win at
$k=20$ and at $k=1$, and both predictions were wrong: at temperature $1.0$ the base model continuing
\texttt{Complete the prefix:} is judged no better than the anchor, and at $k=20$ the meter serves its text
byte for byte on $500$ of $500$ prompts. Two post-hoc checks: the meter writes about twice selection's words
($151$ against $75$ at the median), and a length-controlled regression \citep{dubois2024length} leaves the
difference at $+0.0605$ $[+0.0236, +0.0971]$; on the $414$ prompts whose two anchor controls are non-empty it
is $+0.0441$ $[+0.0024, +0.0864]$ (\texttt{results/\allowbreak h2h\_\allowbreak length\_\allowbreak control\_\allowbreak note.md}).
Read with a Bonferroni correction over its rows, every row of Table~\ref{tab:served}'s released and chat
blocks still excludes zero in the same direction, the narrowest being the chat-template $k=1$ row at
$[+0.0015, +0.0820]$ (\texttt{results/\allowbreak served\_\allowbreak multiplicity.csv}, post hoc).
Served through the same chat template, the audited memoriser alone reaches near-verbatim recall $0.2193$
($0.4921$ on plain text); the meter stays below $0.01$ at every budget through $k=3.8$ and reaches it at
$k=3.941$ $[2.968, 4.435]$, $1.22$ of $s(x)$, with $25.3\%$ of resamples never reaching it by $k=4.5$, so the upper
end is a floor (\texttt{results/\allowbreak chat\_\allowbreak onset.csv}, registered). The chat-served
meter's first win, at $k=3$, and extraction onset coincide within that interval.

\paragraph{Under the authors' own utility metrics.}\label{app:hemetrics}
\citet{he2026anchored} score utility with Prometheus-2 fluency and FActScore precision, so we scored the same
recovered texts with both: their fluency rubric verbatim under \texttt{prometheus-7b-v2.0}
\citep{kim2024prometheus2} where they use \texttt{gpt-4.1-mini}, and FActScore \citep{min2023factscore} on
the $150$ biographies with \texttt{Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct} extracting and verifying against Wikipedia. On
fluency (1--5, non-empty responses) selection at $n=64$ scores $2.56$ against the meter's $1.88$ at $k=10$, a
paired $+0.70$ $[+0.53, +0.85]$, beats the meter at every budget of both text-continuation pairs
($+1.15$ $[+1.00, +1.30]$ over the $70$B pair at $k=0.5$), and loses to the chat-template model,
$-0.81$ $[-0.99, -0.64]$: the judge's pattern. FActScore reads the other way: selection's precision is its
anchor's ($0.042$ against $0.048$, paired $-0.008$ $[-0.041, +0.022]$) and below every metered arm
($-0.182$ $[-0.222, -0.145]$ against $k=10$), while it abstains on $20$ of $150$ biographies where a single
draw abstains on $80$. Best-of-$n$ is bounded by its best draw, not its average: FActScored draw by draw,
the most precise of the $64$ reaches $0.5054$ $[0.4553, 0.5564]$ ($0.2392$ among draws claiming at least five
facts) where one draw averages $0.0613$ and the reward's pick $0.0431$, so the draws hold far more precise
biographies than the reward serves. Asked instead whether every claim is accurate, the same scorer serves $0.0768$ by claiming
fewer facts and abstaining on $45$, paired $+0.0102$ $[-0.0144, +0.0399]$ over the committed pick and
$-0.1246$ $[-0.1727, -0.0725]$ below the meter at $k=10$: a scorer that asks for facts did not find them
(\texttt{results/\allowbreak factscore\_\allowbreak oracle.csv}, registered). The head-to-head is a statement
about fluency and helpfulness, not about factual recall, which the anchor bounds
(\texttt{results/\allowbreak he\_\allowbreak metrics.csv}).

\paragraph{The authors' byte-level decoder.}\label{app:anchoredbyte}
\citet{he2026anchored} meter every licensed anchor but TinyComma with AnchoredByte, which fuses a Comma
tokenizer with a Llama-3 one through ByteSampler \citep{hayase2026bytesampler}, and recommend Comma-7B with
the $70$B base at $k=0.5$ per byte. We ran their package unmodified (\texttt{anchoreddecode} at commit
\texttt{a12ecd9}) in Table~\ref{tab:served}'s configuration, with $B_{\max} = 800$ bytes so that $K = 800k$
nats and $S_w = 139.7$ under Comma-7B; selection is Comma-7B's best-of-$64$. Every trajectory stays within
$K$. Selection wins at every budget, as predicted, including $k=0.1$, where the certificate says something
about a window ($K = 80$) and the meter binds on $18.4\%$ of byte steps and is judged indistinguishable from
Comma-7B alone. Under the authors' metrics selection is the more fluent at every budget; at $k=0.5$ the
meter's FActScore precision is $0.323$ against selection's $0.0995$, the $70$B's facts passing through a
certificate that is vacuous for any window ($K/S_w = 2.86$), and at $k=0.1$ it is $0.0843$, inseparable from
selection's (\texttt{results/\allowbreak onset\_\allowbreak prediction\_\allowbreak anchoredbyte.md}).

\paragraph{Stronger opponents.}
Every judged arm is scored against one fixed opponent, so we varied it
(Table~\ref{tab:opponentladder}): strength is the opponent's order-averaged win rate against the anchor's
own $n=1$ completion. Both mechanisms beat their own controls against every opponent; the difference between
them decays as the opponent strengthens and crosses zero between $0.773$ and $0.825$ (Spearman $-0.900$ over
the five). Judge~C agrees on the shape and crosses earlier, confirming only the committed opponent (column judge~C), its shift strength-dependent ($-0.017$, $-0.058$, $-0.021$, $-0.004$) rather than the uniform one we had predicted; read on the three new rungs alone the decay looked like one judge's, our first reading, and that was wrong; and
\texttt{Mixtral-8x7B} on the rung where they differ reads $+0.0355$ $[-0.0105, +0.0810]$, so three
instruments do not pin the boundary. Against \texttt{Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct} order consistency is
$0.550$ for selection and $0.482$ for the meter, the latter below chance agreement between
presentation orders, which is why only order-averaged figures are quoted. Strength is not parameter count: \texttt{Qwen2.5-0.5B}, sixteen times
smaller than the committed opponent, scores $0.704$, and the committed opponent writes the longest
completions ($154.1$ words against $109.5$--$133.2$). Every alternative opponent was generated one prompt
at a time by a different decoder from the committed one's, a pipeline change that alone moves the committed
opponent's strength on AlpacaEval from $0.6792$ to $0.8519$; only paired differences within one pass are
compared, and the same ladder on AlpacaEval rises with strength (Spearman $+0.900$); only divided by the room a difference of win rates has does it point the same way ($-0.800$), five points each, and neither is a significance claim. No
instruction-tuned opponent weaker than $0.555$ was available, so the registered test of that account went untested, and the decay is a shape in the data, not a mechanism we have shown. The judged head-to-head is therefore opponent-dependent, which is why the paper rests its claim
on the certificate and the judge-free comparisons as well.

\paragraph{Other workloads.}\label{app:workload}
At the paper's own $k=10$ the budget is barely active on any workload ($24$ of $299{,}843$ steps, $0.008\%$, for the committed arm; $0.016\%$ on AlpacaEval), and the meter serves the opponent's text byte for byte on $794$ of $805$
AlpacaEval prompts, so each workload was re-run at a budget chosen to bind by matching a rate: over $k = 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10$ AlpacaEval's activity reads $0.765$, $0.684$, $0.077$, $0.005$ and $0.0002$, and $k=1.0$ binds on $8.008\%$ of steps, about a thousand times the committed arm's rate. Figure~\ref{fig:h2h} gives the six binding-budget differences on the recovered text.
The reversal holds on prefix completion (our $850$ prompts, $500$ excerpts of $42$ public-domain books, $500$
excerpts of BookMIA's unseen half), is unresolved on CoTaEval's NewsQA and on MT-Bench's $80$ prompts, and
fails on AlpacaEval, $-0.0329$ $[-0.0528, -0.0127]$ ($-0.0339$ $[-0.0534, -0.0137]$ as recorded, selection gaining $+0.0835$ $[+0.0665, +0.1016]$ and the meter $+0.1174$ $[+0.0991, +0.1354]$), which a disjoint re-draw reproduces ($-0.0255$ $[-0.0453, -0.0062]$). Judge~C reads $+0.0903$ $[+0.0629, +0.1176]$ on ours and $-0.0969$ $[-0.1264, -0.0677]$ on AlpacaEval, \texttt{Mixtral-8x7B} $+0.0226$ $[-0.0018, +0.0476]$ and $-0.0854$ $[-0.1112, -0.0593]$: no judge crosses, so the split is not one judge's. Our own $850$ prompts through the same pipeline hold at both budgets, $+0.0482$ $[+0.0303, +0.0662]$ at $k=10$ and $+0.0965$ $[+0.0765, +0.1162]$ at $k=0.9$, so across five passes on these two workloads the split is the workload, not the budget, the batch size, the pipeline or the seed. The two respond to a binding budget in \emph{opposite} directions, as a support ceiling would predict and the recorded-text decomposition below refutes. Neither anchor competence (the sign does not move across our own
three classes, which span $0.137$ of the anchor's win rate, twice the $0.065$ gap between the two workloads' ranges), nor, within AlpacaEval, the opponent (the meter wins against five opponents of strength $0.7752$ to $0.8807$, $-0.0584$ to $-0.0814$ with every interval clear of zero, though even the weakest beats the anchor three times in four) explains the split, and neither does a support ceiling: decomposed, the swing from our workload to

\paragraph{One certificate, split between the draw and the token.}\label{app:hybrid}
Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} holds for a pool drawn from any law with $q_B \le e^{B}p_s$, so best-of-$n$
over pathwise-metered drafts at budget $B$ is certified at $\log n + B$. At a fixed $C = \log 64$ the level
rises monotonically with the share spent on the draw, $0.4400$, $0.4610$, $0.4995$ and $0.5550$, and every
split loses to pure selection: $-0.1150$ $[-0.1380, -0.0915]$ for the meter alone, $-0.0940$
$[-0.1170, -0.0715]$ and $-0.0555$ $[-0.0780, -0.0330]$ for the two hybrids; the meter given the whole
certificate is judged no better than a single anchor draw ($0.4400$ against $0.4535$), and at fixed $n=8$ the tilt is worth $-0.0120$ $[-0.0300, +0.0060]$.

\paragraph{Stopping early.}\label{app:adaptive}
A rule that stops at the first reward above a threshold, capped at $n_{\max}=64$, is certified at $\log 64$
however early it stops. With the threshold at the first draw's $0.75$ quantile it stops after $6.8$ draws on
average, loses to fixed $n=64$ by $-0.0515$ $[-0.0720, -0.0310]$, and reads $+0.0040$ $[-0.0125, +0.0205]$ against fixed $n=7$, the same expected draws; at $q=0.90$ it uses $17.4$ draws, $27\%$ of the budget, and straddles zero against both $n=64$ ($-0.0155$ $[-0.0340, +0.0020]$) and $n=17$: a deployer who wants fewer draws should
lower $n$.

\paragraph{Scorer size on the judged workload.}\label{app:frontier}\label{app:saturation}

$+0.1075$ against $+0.1045$ and the meter's $+0.0400$ exactly), gives $n=64$ gains of $+0.0230$,

favour. \textbf{The $0.5$B scorer never
reaches the meter}: its best cell, $n=16$ at $3.68\times$ the meter's forward passes, gains
$+0.0395\,[+0.0205,+0.0585]$, and \textbf{at matched compute, $n=4$ at $0.92\times$, it loses outright,
$-0.0395\,[-0.0720,-0.0065]$}. Its curve peaks at $n=16$ and ends lower, as though $\log n$ is not a free knob; that
reading did not survive its registered test, the terminal drop $g(64)-g(16)$, $-0.0160\,[-0.0340,+0.0010]$,
\textsc{flat}; the Spearman of gain against $\log n$ is $0.5429$ for $0.5$B and exactly $1.0$ for each
larger scorer. Capability saturates early: only the first adjacent step at $n=64$ separates,
$+0.0700\,[+0.0500,+0.0900]$ from $0.5$B to $1.5$B, then $+0.0040\,[-0.0145,+0.0225]$ and
$+0.0095\,[-0.0090,+0.0285]$, so a $1.5$B scorer reaches $87.3\%$ of the $7.6$B gain for $35.2\%$ of its

unstable where a gain is not: the $7.6$B scorer first matches the meter at $n=8$ ($7.66\times$) in one

\paragraph{The judge-free axis.}\label{app:judgefree}
Both arms run at Comma-7B at temperature $0.7$ and score the \emph{served} string, which the tie rule
returns as an index into the sample, never a synthesised answer. GSM8K: the first $500$ test problems,
$8$-shot, $256$-token cap, $64$ samples drawn once and read nested, scored by the final integer after
\texttt{\#\#\#\#}. TriviaQA \texttt{rc.nocontext}: the first $500$ validation questions, $5$-shot,
$24$-token cap, scored by containment of a gold alias as a whole-word span, a rule fixed before any number
because exact match scores format (a smoke run scored \texttt{The answer is David Seville} $0/8$ on
questions it had right). A number is extracted from $99.96\%$ and $98.67\%$ of the $500 \times 64$ samples
against a gate of $90\%$. The risky model alone reads $0.786$ greedy and $0.726$ sampled on GSM8K, $0.722$
and $0.674$ on TriviaQA. Majority vote lifts $4.2\times$ less on TriviaQA than on GSM8K, the direction
registered in advance: a closed-book question offers less spread between draws than a derivation where one
slip decides the answer, and $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ means no $n$ supplies a fact the anchor lacks. The two
lift ratios carry paired-bootstrap intervals: vote over reward on GSM8K $3.42$ $[2.20, 8.91]$ (Fieller
gives $[2.20, 8.81]$), vote on GSM8K over vote on TriviaQA $4.19$ $[2.63, 8.40]$; the third ratio has a
negative denominator and is undefined on $82\%$ of resamples, so we do not quote it
(\texttt{results/\allowbreak lift\_\allowbreak ratio\_\allowbreak interval.csv}).

\emph{A forced choice.} On MMLU ($5$-shot, $T_{\max}=24$, the first $500$ of the shuffled test set,
chance $0.25$) majority vote at Comma-7B gains $+0.084$ $[+0.042, +0.126]$ at $n=64$ over its own $0.404$,
$+0.108$ $[+0.066, +0.150]$ at $n=32$, and the pointwise reward $+0.056$ $[+0.008, +0.104]$. Both registered
gates passed: the anchor reads $0.404$ $[0.362, 0.448]$ and the risky model $0.676$ greedy. The same
protocol at TinyComma-1.8B read $0.188$ $[0.154, 0.222]$, below chance with $49\%$ of answers the letter
B, which the gate calls invalid, so the judge-free comparison against a metered decoder remains one task.

\emph{Against the meter, on TriviaQA.} At TinyComma, $T_{\max}=24$, the meter wins the level, and only at
$k=20$, where its accuracy is the risky model's $0.618$ $[0.576, 0.660]$ to three decimals, its
certificate $480$ nats and its realised mean spend $44.8473$. At $k=0$ and $k=0.5$ ($12$ nats) it reads
$0.112$, at $k=1$ ($24$ nats) $0.114$ and at $k=3$ ($72$ nats) $0.166$, against selection's $0.146$,
$0.174$ and $0.190$ at $n=8,32,64$ for $2.079$, $3.466$ and $4.159$. At every accuracy selection buys over
the shared control it pays $0.032$, $0.061$, $0.093$, $0.056$ and $0.071$ of what the cheapest metered
arm reaching that accuracy spends ($n=4$ to $64$); on the pathwise axis two ratios exceed a tenth
($0.106$ and $0.141$). Teacher-forcing the anchor on the accepted aliases gives a median $S(x)$ of
$5.86$ nats (p10 $1.35$, p90 $12.14$), so the meter's certificate is vacuous on $89.6\%$ of questions at
$k=0.5$, $99.4\%$ at $k=1$ and $100\%$ at $k=3$ and $k=20$, and selection's on $10.4\%$ at $n=4$ and
$35.4\%$ at $n=64$: selection's worst budget is vacuous less often than the meter's best. Both figures are lower bounds, since scoring the single most likely
alias rather than their sum overstates $S(x)$. At matched
compute the meter wins outright: it runs both models whatever $k$, so selection reaches parity at
$n = 5.57$, reads about $0.13$ there against $0.618$, and at $11.5\times$ the compute is still at $0.190$
(\texttt{results/\allowbreak compute\_\allowbreak matched\_\allowbreak judgefree.csv}). The meter buys
accuracy with compute cheaply and with certificate dearly, and selection the reverse.

\paragraph{CoTaEval, and the scorer size a task needs.}
CoTaEval's news half \citep{wei2024cotaeval} is a domain no other corpus here uses: $500$ in-domain
article-and-question items scored by SQuAD token-F1 \citep{rajpurkar2016squad}, CoTaEval's own utility
metric, at seven anchors in three families, bands fixed in advance. \textbf{Four anchors cannot read a
news article} ($n=1$ F1 of $0.003$ to $0.088$ at both Pleias and both KL3M models, against a registered
floor of $0.10$), so no band was computed for them. At the four that can, the $7$B pointwise reward
\emph{turns over at every one} (Table~\ref{tab:scorerladder}): at the headline anchor it gives away $43\%$
of the anchor's own F1 by $n=64$, and reproduces on a disjoint re-draw. Majority vote, with no scorer to
overoptimise against, neither falls nor climbs ($+0.0284$ $[-0.0012,+0.0576]$). We registered the opposite
prediction and it was wrong. Swapping the scorer to $14$B, nothing else changed, removes the turn-over at two of four anchors, neither of which began to climb (both marginal):
the \textsc{scorer-bound} reading, where we predicted \textsc{scorer-independent} and were wrong; at $72$B
nothing turns over anywhere, the two anchors that flipped at $14$B climb, and the Spearman of accuracy
against $\log n$ inverts on both tasks, CoTaEval $-0.8929 \to +0.7500$ and TriviaQA $-0.6071 \to +0.8571$.
We had registered that the headline anchor's turn-over would survive $72$B; it did not. The comparison is
legitimate because every arm re-scores cached generations: majority vote never consults the scorer, so all
seven of its cells must be identical across scorers, and they are on all six arms; the GSM8K control
climbs at $4.55$ half-widths. The turn-over is therefore rescoped, not withdrawn: it is a property of
optimising a $7$B pointwise proxy on these tasks, the $7$B scorer is the one every selection arm here
uses, and a deployer needs a larger one on a checkable task. It does not transfer to the judged
workload, where the same two larger scorers lift nothing (the scorer-size paragraph above): a statement about the \emph{task}, not the mechanism. The turn-over touches neither the certificate, a claim about the served law that does not depend on the served text being good, nor the GSM8K majority-vote result ($0.320 \to 0.546$), a different task and rule.

\paragraph{How loose is $\log n$?}\label{app:realisedkl}
If no two candidates share a score, $q(y) = n\,p_s(y)\,F(f(y))^{n-1}$ with $F$ the score's CDF under
$p_s$, so the pointwise ratio approaches $n$ as $F \to 1$ and the KL is $\log n - (n-1)/n$; both are upper
bounds approached as $p_s(y^\star) \to 0$. For $200$-token completions a repeated draw essentially never
occurs, and what lowers the realised figures is a tie in the score: over the headline arm's $32{,}000$
rewards that happens on $5.0\%$ of prompts at $n=2$, $13.8\%$ at $n=8$ and $7.0\%$ at $n=64$, with a mean
tied set of $1.31$, so the quoted figures are the realised ones on $86$--$95\%$ of prompts and conservative
on the rest (\texttt{results/\allowbreak selection\_\allowbreak realised\_\allowbreak kl.csv}). Where draws
do repeat, majority vote's served law can be computed rather than bounded: at $n=64$ its realised pathwise
divergence is $0.92$ nats at the median question on GSM8K and $1.02$ on TriviaQA (at most $1.97$ and
$2.43$), against a certificate of $4.16$; its KL is $0.73$ and $0.68$ against a bound of $3.17$. The
certificate also does not depend on the scorer being reproducible: bf16 non-determinism changes which
candidate is served on $5.6\%$ of prompts at $n=64$ from a batch-size change alone and $8.6\%$ across two
hosts (\texttt{results/\allowbreak selection\_\allowbreak argmax\_\allowbreak stability\_\allowbreak note.md}),
and cannot loosen $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ by a single nat.

\paragraph{Vetting the anchor.}\label{app:vetting}
$\Pr_{p_s}[E]$ is the one quantity in the construction measurable with no attack, no risky model and no
access to the mechanism: draw from the candidate on the protected passages' own prefixes and score
near-verbatim recall. At a $100$-token raw prefix on $50$ passages, every anchor used here reads exactly
$0.000$, while \texttt{OLMo-2-7B} leaks on $2$ of $50$ passages and \texttt{OLMo-2-13B} on $6$ (up to
$0.566$ of one), which is why the open-\emph{data} families are excluded, and Llama-3.1-70B leaks on
$0.500$. The prefix length is part of the test (Table~\ref{tab:vetladder}): the $70$B is nearly invisible
at $20$ and $35$ tokens and seen from $50$, and the screen is not monotone, against our registered
prediction: \texttt{OLMo-2-13B} leaks on $6$ passages at $100$ and $150$ tokens and on $3$ at $200$, where only $57$--$94$ tokens of each passage remain. It still reproduces one passage in full there, so the screen did not go blind. Screened instead at a $20$-token seed that includes the \texttt{Complete the prefix:} header, the $70$B
also reads $0.0000$: at about fourteen genuine tokens the screen cannot tell a clean anchor from a model
that holds the book. The certificate holds prompt by prompt, $\Pr_q[E \mid x] \le n\Pr_{p_s}[E \mid x]$, and the user
chooses $x$, so the prefix is part of the attack surface, not a parameter of the test. The
recommendation is therefore a schedule: screen at every prefix length up to the
longest genuine prefix the deployment accepts. Across the three web-trained models the leaking fraction
rises with scale ($0.040$, $0.120$, $0.500$), a pattern on three points that confounds scale with corpus. The six anchors read $0$ of $50$ at all seven lengths. Twenty-six of the new rungs ran on a second machine (host check in Table~\ref{tab:vetladder}). The test is sound but not complete: recall at a given prefix is a lower bound on
contamination, so passing is evidence and failing is a demonstration.

\paragraph{When the premise fails.}\label{app:contaminated}\label{app:sel-contaminated}
With $\Pr_{p_s}[E] = 0$ the bound $\Pr_q[E] \le n \Pr_{p_s}[E]$ is met by anything we could measure, so
we tested it where it can fail: twelve deliberately memorised anchors (LoRA copies fine-tuned on the
protected passages, GPT-NeoX, Llama, Mixtral, Phi and Qwen, $170$M to $7$B), the same adversarial
selector, the same $100$ passages and seeds, and all twelve reproducing the memoriser's own control at
$0.3925$ mean recall. The selector indexes each padded candidate from its first real token; a version
that read the left-padded batch as right-padded ranked partly on padding, and no number from it is
quoted. Table~\ref{tab:contam} and Figure~\ref{fig:safety}(b) give $E = \{\text{recall} \ge 0.8\}$.
\textbf{Six anchors never enter $E$} in $25{,}600$ draws. At the six that do, the adversary raises the
one-draw rate by $1.0$ to $7.0\times$ at $n=64$, at most $7.0/64$ of what the certificate allows, and by
$1.25$ to $10.0\times$ at $n=256$; a second draw under a disjoint seed reads $1.0$ to $3.0\times$ at $n=64$ and $1.0$ to $4.0\times$ at $256$, because the one-draw denominator is a count over $100$ passages (Llama-3.2-1B's moved
from one passage to three while its per-draw rate moved from $0.0179$ to $0.0182$). Against the per-draw rate the served rates are $1.12$ to $3.91\times$ at $n=64$ and $1.12$ to $5.59\times$ at $256$ on the first draw, $1.12$ to $4.95\times$ and $1.12$ to $6.61\times$ on the second. Where an anchor leaks
at all the bound is vacuous by $n=256$ ($256\hat p$ from $1.73$ to $26.40$, and $1.85$ to $26.13$ on the second draw), and what caps the served
rate is what the pool holds. The band we registered, $A(64) \le 4$ at every anchor, is crossed by one
anchor on the first draw and by none on the second, so we give both readings. Selection multiplies what
the anchor already leaks; the anchor is the thing to vet.

\paragraph{A per-work certificate a rights-holder can audit.}\label{app:perwork}
The bound needs two numbers: $n$, which the deployer declares, and $\Pr_{p_s}[E_x]$, which the public
anchor determines. For the exact continuation $x_{>c}$ of a work served for its own prefix the second is
$e^{-S(x_{>c} \mid x_{\le c})}$, and after $m$ adaptive queries the bound is $m\,n\,e^{-S}$: at the
$50$-token window, $S_w = 159.8$ nats, it is below $10^{-60}$ at $n=64$ even after $m=10^4$ queries. A
near-verbatim event has no closed form, so the rights-holder samples: $N$ anchor draws with no event give
$\Pr_{p_s}[E_x] \le 3/N$ at $95\%$ and so $\Pr_q[E_x] \le 3n/N$. Certifying one work at $1\%$ with $n=64$
takes $19{,}200$ draws; our $n=256$ pools certify the average passage at $3 \times 64/25{,}600 = 0.75\%$.
For a pathwise-metered decoder at the published $k = 0.5$ the multiplier is
$e^{100} \approx 2.7 \times 10^{43}$, and no sample bounds an event below its reciprocal.

\paragraph{The channel the certificate prices.}\label{app:channel}
One response carries at most $\log_2 n$ bits about anything. A scorer colluding with a receiver through a
shared key, serving a draw whose keyed hash falls in the cell that encodes the next bits, realises $1.32$,
$2.22$ and $3.97$ bits per response at $n = 8, 16, 64$ against $\log_2 n = 3, 4, 6$, short of the i.i.d.\
$4.23$ at $n=64$ because $12.6\%$ of draws are duplicates. A compressed $50$-token window is $1{,}423$ bits,
so it takes $357$ responses at $n=64$ and $237$ even at capacity; the $400$-nat odometer stops the channel at
$\lfloor 400/\ln n \rfloor = 96$ responses and $400/\ln 2 = 577$ bits whatever $n$ is, less than one
compressed window. Only a code built on the anchor itself fits: the window's surprisal is $231$ bits.

\paragraph{The attack the composed certificate prices.}\label{app:prefixext}
A user holds a memorised passage's $20$-token seed and extends it one query at a time. A scorer that
knows the work serves, among $n$ fresh draws from the anchor, the one whose first token the leakage
table's memoriser finds most likely, and the user appends that token; since only first tokens are read,
each query is simulated exactly by $n$ draws from the anchor's next-token law (CPU, float32, the
leakage table's $100$ passages, pre-registered in
\texttt{results/\allowbreak onset\_\allowbreak prediction\_\allowbreak prefix\_\allowbreak extension.md}).
After $50$ queries the transcript is certified at $50\log n$ nats, against a window surprisal whose
median is $179.5$ nats (Table~\ref{tab:prefixext}).
