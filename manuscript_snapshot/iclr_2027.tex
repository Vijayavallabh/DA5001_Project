\begin{abstract}
Inference-time copyright defences meter how far a language model's output may drift from a
\emph{safe} model that never saw the protected work, charging the drift per decoded token and
publishing the allowance as a single number $k$. We show the budget is in the wrong place. Draw $n$
completions from the safe model, score them by anything at all, and serve the best: every token
emitted is still a safe-model token, so the per-step charge is identically zero and the whole budget
is spent once, on \emph{which draw to serve}. The served law satisfies $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$ for any
score, any tie rule and any safe model --- a \emph{pathwise} certificate of exactly $\log n$ nats,
the strongest R\'enyi order in the family the deployed meters charge in, and constant in the length
of the work where a per-token allowance is linear in it. Self-consistency is an instance, so the
most widely deployed inference-time method already carries one. On the workload where a metered
decoder spends a measured $171.3$ nats to become judged-better than its own safe model, eight draws
cost $2.08$ and do better: $+0.142$ against $+0.072$ at one fifty-fourth of the divergence, at four
anchors in three families, on AlpacaEval, and on exact match with no judge at all. It reproduces no
protected passage at any $n \le 64$ --- not when an adversary picks the scorer, and not against a
$70$B model that memorised the book in pre-training and recovers $0.2475$ unaided. This is
not luck but the only place left. One line of the chain rule makes a causal policy's sequence budget
the sum of its per-step budgets, so a per-token meter is \emph{vacuous} --- its allowance $K = kT$
grows with the work, the bound it implies is empty exactly at $K = S(x)$ identically for every
R\'enyi order, and across nine pairs with nine distinct anchors extraction becomes measurable
between $0.88$ and $1.17$ of $s(x)$ --- or \emph{trivial}, the safe model at all but $O(1)$ of its
steps, where at $k=0.5$ neither of two judges can separate it from that model at $600$
comparisons. The deployed class takes
the first horn by construction, paying $\Theta(T)$ nats to \emph{imitate} the risky model rather
than to buy utility, and the three repairs this predicts fail where it says they must. A
rights-holder can be promised an amplification of $8$, against $e^{2000}$.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Ethics Statement}
Auditing a copyright defence requires demonstrating that it can be defeated, and this paper does
that: it fine-tunes memorising models, attacks a protected decoder, and reports where extraction
begins. We judge the disclosure net positive and have bounded it deliberately. The passages are
already published as extraction benchmarks (CopyBench, BookMIA), only aggregate recall statistics are
reported and never reconstructed passages, and the accompanying anonymised repository ships the
fine-tuning recipes and every analysis script but not the merged memorising weights, which reproduce
copyrighted text on demand. Nothing here enables an attack that possession of a fine-tuned model
would not already permit.

The constructive direction carries its own risk, which is misplaced confidence. A $\log n$
certificate bounds the probability that the served text is a protected work \emph{relative to the
safe model's own probability of producing it}, so it is only as good as that model. We measure what
happens when it is not: across twelve deliberately memorised anchors, an adversary selecting by the
memorising model's own likelihood over $64$ draws raises the rate at which most of a passage is
reproduced by a factor of $1.0$ to $4.0$, against the $64$ the certificate permits
(Appendix~\ref{app:selection}). The mechanism is a multiplier on the anchor's own leakage, not a
floor under it. It is a statement about a mechanism, not a legal conclusion about infringement, and
we make no claim about legal thresholds for copyright infringement. The deployer-facing
recommendation is narrow and we would rather it were quoted than the theorems: publish $k/s(x)$
rather than $k$, name the work it was computed against, prefer a budget whose size does not grow
with the thing it protects, and vet the anchor, because $n$ multiplies whatever it already has.

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}
Every number in this paper is produced by a committed script and written to a CSV before it is
quoted, with the producing command recorded alongside it. Proofs of
Propositions~\ref{prop:threshold} and~\ref{prop:outrun} and of Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl} are in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, stated with their assumptions; Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} is proved
where it is stated. The three rates of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rates} require only forward passes and no
decoding, so Section~\ref{sec:onset} and Appendix~\ref{app:scaling} reproduce on a single GPU from
openly licensed checkpoints. Every band this paper scores against was committed to the repository before the run that scores it,
together with the grid, the entry gate and the alternatives excluded in advance; the records are the
forty-two pre-registration logs in \texttt{results/}, of which
\texttt{results/onset\_prediction\_selection.md} is the one Section~\ref{sec:experiments} scores. Budget accounting is
checked per trajectory rather than on averages, and every run reports the $k=-1$ (risky only) and
$k=0$ (safe only) baselines on the same prompts and seeds. An anonymised repository with the code,
the CSVs and the reproduction commands accompanies the submission.

\section*{LLM Usage}
LLMs were used for editorial purposes in this manuscript, and all outputs were inspected by the
authors to ensure accuracy and originality. Instruction-tuned models also appear in two further
roles, which we separate deliberately. As \emph{measurement instruments} they judge output quality
in Sections~\ref{sec:orders} and~\ref{sec:experiments}; their resolution is measured rather than
assumed --- by scoring one decoder against itself under two seeds, by including a null arm, and by
running two judges that are reported separately wherever they disagree. As a \emph{component of the
proposed mechanism} one of them supplies the score that selection anchoring ranks by, which is why
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} never scores an arm with the model that selected it, and why
Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} is stated for an arbitrary score: the certificate must not depend
on that model behaving well. Total compute for this work is approximately $220$ GPU-hours on A100
hardware (\texttt{results/compute\_hours.csv}), most of it the budget sweeps and the judged arms;
the memorisation fine-tunes account for at most $28$ of those hours, some of whose run directories
also contain the sweep that followed.
