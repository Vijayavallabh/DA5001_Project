\title{Vacuous or Trivial\\
A Dichotomy for Per-Token Copyright Certificates, and Where the Budget Must Go Instead}

\author{Anonymous authors\\Paper under double-blind review}

\begin{abstract}
Inference-time copyright defences meter how far a language model's output may drift from a
\emph{safe} model that never saw the protected work, and publish the allowance as a single number
$k$. We show the design is closed. For any autoregressive policy the sequence divergence is exactly
the sum of its per-step divergences, so a policy holding to a budget $K$ can differ from the safe
model by more than $\varepsilon$ at no more than $K/\varepsilon$ steps in expectation. A per-token
meter therefore faces a dichotomy. Either its allowance $K = kT$ grows with the work, and it is
\emph{vacuous} --- the bound it implies on reproducing $x$ is empty exactly at $K = S(x)$,
identically for every R\'enyi order, and across nine pairs with nine distinct anchors and six tokenizers
extraction becomes measurable between $0.88$ and $1.17$ of $s(x)$ --- or the budget is
bounded and the decoder is the safe model at all but $O(1)$ of its steps, where at $k=0.5$ neither
of two judges can separate it from the safe model at $600$ comparisons per budget. The deployed
class takes the first horn by construction: its spend is the cost of \emph{imitating} the risky
model, $\Theta(T)$ nats independent of the utility bought, stalling at $171.3$ over $16{,}500$
trajectories while its certificate is written against $4000$. Three repairs --- a sharper charge, a
smaller cap, a better schedule --- are corollaries of the dichotomy and fail as it predicts. The
escape is not a better meter but a different place to spend, outside the per-step factorisation the
chain rule reaches. Serving the best of $n$ safe-model draws satisfies $q(y) \le n\,p_s(y)$, a
\emph{pathwise} certificate of exactly $\log n$ under any score and any tie rule, vacuous only at
$n = e^{S(x)}$. At $n = 8$ that is $2.08$ nats against a work worth $849$, and it gains judged
utility at four anchors in three families and exact-match accuracy with no judge at
all, reaches the utility a metered decoder needs $171.3$ nats for, promises an amplification of $8$ against $e^{2000}$, and reproduces none of the protected
passages at any $n \le 64$, even when the adversary picks the scorer.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Ethics Statement}
Auditing a copyright defence requires demonstrating that it can be defeated, and this paper does
that: it fine-tunes memorising models, attacks a protected decoder, and reports where extraction
begins. We judge the disclosure net positive and have bounded it deliberately. The passages are
already published as extraction benchmarks (CopyBench, BookMIA), only aggregate recall statistics are
reported and never reconstructed passages, and the accompanying anonymised repository ships the
fine-tuning recipes and every analysis script but not the merged memorising weights, which reproduce
copyrighted text on demand. Nothing here enables an attack that possession of a fine-tuned model
would not already permit.

The constructive direction carries its own risk, which is misplaced confidence. A $\log n$
certificate bounds the probability that the served text is a protected work \emph{relative to the
safe model's own probability of producing it}, and is worthless if the safe model is itself
contaminated; it is a statement about a mechanism, not a legal conclusion about infringement, and we
make no claim about legal thresholds for copyright infringement. The deployer-facing recommendation
is narrow and we would rather it were quoted than the theorems: publish $k/s(x)$ rather than $k$,
name the work it was computed against, and prefer a budget whose size does not grow with the thing it
protects.

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}
Every number in this paper is produced by a committed script and written to a CSV before it is
quoted, with the producing command recorded alongside it. Proofs of
Propositions~\ref{prop:threshold} and~\ref{prop:outrun} and of Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl} are in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, stated with their assumptions; Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} is proved
where it is stated. The three rates of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rates} require only forward passes and no
decoding, so Section~\ref{sec:onset} and Appendix~\ref{app:scaling} reproduce on a single GPU from
openly licensed checkpoints. Every band this paper scores against was committed to the repository before the run that scores it,
together with the grid, the entry gate and the alternatives excluded in advance; the records are the
forty pre-registration logs in \texttt{results/}, of which
\texttt{results/onset\_prediction\_selection.md} is the one Section~\ref{sec:experiments} scores. Budget accounting is
checked per trajectory rather than on averages, and every run reports the $k=-1$ (risky only) and
$k=0$ (safe only) baselines on the same prompts and seeds. An anonymised repository with the code,
the CSVs and the reproduction commands accompanies the submission.

\section*{LLM Usage}
LLMs were used for editorial purposes in this manuscript, and all outputs were inspected by the
authors to ensure accuracy and originality. Instruction-tuned models also appear in two further
roles, which we separate deliberately. As \emph{measurement instruments} they judge output quality
in Sections~\ref{sec:orders} and~\ref{sec:experiments}; their resolution is measured rather than
assumed --- by scoring one decoder against itself under two seeds, by including a null arm, and by
running two judges that are reported separately wherever they disagree. As a \emph{component of the
proposed mechanism} one of them supplies the score that selection anchoring ranks by, which is why
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} never scores an arm with the model that selected it, and why
Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} is stated for an arbitrary score: the certificate must not depend
on that model behaving well. Total compute for this work is approximately $207$ GPU-hours on A100
hardware (\texttt{results/compute\_hours.csv}), most of it the budget sweeps and the judged arms;
the memorisation fine-tunes account for at most $28$ of those hours, some of whose run directories
also contain the sweep that followed.
