\begin{abstract}
Inference-time mechanisms for copyright protection meter how far a language model's output may
drift from a safe model that never saw the protected work, and publish the allowance as a single
number $k$. We show that $k$ carries no risk information on its own, and identify what does. The
bound such a budget implies on reproducing a work $x$ becomes vacuous at $K = S(x)$, the safe
model's surprisal of $x$, and this threshold is identical for every R\'enyi order of the charge, so
no strengthening of the accounting moves it. A second result prices the other thing a budget buys:
Donsker--Varadhan at the sequence level bounds the budget needed for mean utility by the Cram\'er
rate function of that utility under the safe model. Measuring both sides, the decoder spends $79$ to
$171$ nats of realised divergence for a utility gain worth hundredths of a nat --- three to four
orders of magnitude of overhead, so what pushes a budget past the vacuity threshold is the price of
imitating the risky model, not the price of being better than the safe one. The two useful regions
still do not overlap: a judge cannot separate the decoder from its own safe model at $k=1$
($-0.45\sigma$) and can at $k=3$ ($-2.35\sigma$), where the certificate is already vacuous. Finally, extraction begins at a fixed fraction of that
threshold, though not at a fixed fraction of it: on six model pairs sharing no anchor, tokenizer
or risky model, and spanning $1.87\times$ in $s(x)$, leakage first becomes measurable between
$0.88$ and $1.17$ of it, the four whose tokenizer packs four characters into a token agreeing to within
$0.05$, and the two that pack two beginning only after the certificate is already vacuous. We derive a candidate explanation from the decoding geometry
and pre-register it on three held-out pairs; the pair built to discriminate refutes it, along with
the fitted constant it was meant to explain. Across ten safe models and
three openly licensed corpora, the separation between the rate that must be permitted and the rate
that must be forbidden widens as safe models improve.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This work audits a copyright-protection mechanism, so it necessarily demonstrates extraction of
copyrighted text. We use passages already published as benchmarks (CopyBench, BookMIA) and report
only aggregate recall statistics, never reconstructed passages. The memorising models are the
adversary's construction rather than an artifact we advocate: the accompanying anonymised
repository contains the fine-tuning recipes and every analysis script, but not the merged
memorising weights, which reproduce copyrighted text on demand. We make no claim about legal thresholds for infringement. The findings are reported so
that deployers of divergence-budgeted decoders can choose budgets that mean what they think they
mean; nothing here enables an attack that a fine-tuned model would not already permit.

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}
Every number in this paper is produced by a committed script and written to a CSV before it is
quoted, with the producing command recorded alongside it. Proofs of
Propositions~\ref{prop:threshold} and~\ref{prop:outrun} and of Theorem~\ref{thm:nfl} are in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, stated with their assumptions. The three rates of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:rates} require only forward passes and no decoding, so
Sections~\ref{sec:scaling} and~\ref{sec:onset} reproduce on a single GPU from openly licensed
checkpoints. Budget accounting is checked per trajectory rather than on averages, and every run
reports the $k=-1$ (risky only) and $k=0$ (safe only) baselines on the same prompts and seeds. An
anonymised repository with the code, the CSVs and the reproduction commands accompanies the
submission.

\section*{LLM Usage}
LLMs were used for editorial purposes in this manuscript, and all outputs were inspected by the
authors to ensure accuracy and originality. An instruction-tuned model is also used as a
\emph{measurement instrument} in Section~\ref{sec:orders}, where it judges output quality; its
resolution is measured rather than assumed, by scoring one decoder against itself under two seeds
and by including a null arm. Total compute for this work is approximately $63$ GPU-hours on A100
hardware, dominated by the memorisation fine-tunes and the budget sweeps.
